# Brand Voice AI Foundation Framework

Use this framework to build an AI-assisted communication system that preserves a brand’s identity, knowledge, judgment, and humanity before increasing the speed or volume of content production.

This framework is part of the **Brand Voice AI** methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC**.

> Most people use AI to create more content. Brand Voice AI uses AI to preserve continuity, direction, and identity.

---

## Framework Purpose

The Brand Voice AI Foundation Framework helps organizations establish the information, standards, safeguards, and approval systems required for consistent AI-assisted communication.

It is designed to answer five essential questions:

1. Who are we?
2. Who do we serve?
3. What do we believe and offer?
4. How do we naturally communicate?
5. What must remain under human control?

The framework may be used by:

- Founders
- Solopreneurs
- Coaches
- Consultants
- Service businesses
- Marketing teams
- Agencies
- Educators
- Nonprofits
- Veteran-owned businesses
- Healthcare organizations
- Government contractors
- Community leaders
- Content creators
- Customer-support teams
- Sales teams
- AI workflow designers

# Core Belief

## AI Should Amplify Human Expression

AI should help people communicate more clearly and consistently.

It should not:

- Replace the founder’s perspective
- Invent company beliefs
- Fabricate customer language
- Create unsupported claims
- Flatten a distinctive voice
- Remove judgment from important decisions
- Publish without appropriate approval
- Conceal uncertainty
- Expose confidential information
- Pretend to be human when transparency is required

The goal is not to make a person sound like AI.

The goal is to help AI understand how the person already thinks, speaks, decides, teaches, serves, and tells stories.

# Layer Four: Voice

## 15. Voice Versus Tone

### Voice

Voice is the recognizable character of the brand across situations.

Voice should remain relatively stable.

### Tone

Tone changes according to:

- Audience
- Channel
- Purpose
- Emotional context
- Journey stage
- Level of urgency
- Risk
- Relationship

A serious customer problem may require a serious tone without abandoning the brand’s voice.

## 31. Core AI Instruction Template

```text
You are assisting [brand or organization].

Your role is to help [task or function].

The primary audience is [audience].

The business helps [audience] achieve [outcome] through [method].

The brand voice is [voice summary].

Use the approved source material provided.

Do not invent facts, claims, quotations, customer stories, pricing, deadlines, credentials, or permissions.

When information is missing, contradictory, uncertain, or outdated, identify the issue instead of guessing.

Preserve confidential information.

Adapt tone to the channel and situation while preserving the brand’s core voice.

Provide content for human review.

Do not publish, send, schedule, or execute external communication without the required human approval.
```

---

## 32. Missing-Information Rules

The AI should:

- Identify missing information
- Ask focused questions
- Label assumptions
- Distinguish facts from interpretation
- Distinguish source material from generated suggestions
- Avoid filling gaps with invented details
- Avoid using outdated information as current
- Escalate high-risk uncertainty
- Stop before execution when approval is missing

### Approved Missing-Information Response

```text
The available source material does not confirm [missing information].  
Before this content is finalized, please provide or approve [required information].
```

# Human Approval Before Execution

## 35. Human Approval Principle

AI may prepare the work.

A human remains responsible for:

- Purpose
- Accuracy
- Judgment
- Ethics
- Privacy
- Permissions
- Claims
- Brand alignment
- Customer impact
- Publication
- Distribution
- Execution

The preferred operating model is:

```text
AI prepares
   ↓
Human reviews
   ↓
Human corrects
   ↓
Human approves
   ↓
System executes
   ↓
Human monitors
```

---

## 36. Approval Levels

### Level One: Low Risk

Examples:

- Internal brainstorming
- Early outlines
- Draft summaries
- Low-risk formatting

Approval may occur at the project or template level.

### Level Two: Moderate Risk

Examples:

- Public social posts
- Articles
- Email campaigns
- Lead magnets
- Sales-support content

Each final asset should receive human approval.

### Level Three: High Risk

Examples:

- Customer-specific communication
- Automated campaigns
- Public claims
- Sensitive customer stories
- Financial or legal topics
- Reputation-sensitive communication

Require named review and recorded approval.

### Level Four: Critical Risk

Examples:

- Medical guidance
- Legal conclusions
- Financial recommendations
- Safety communication
- Regulated information
- Crisis communication
- Restricted personal data

Require specialized review before use.

# Context Before Creation

## 38. Context Standard

Before asking AI to create content, provide:

- The task
- The audience
- The problem
- The journey stage
- The business objective
- The source material
- The brand voice
- The offer
- The CTA
- The constraints
- The approval requirements

Weak prompt:

```text
Write a LinkedIn post about AI.
```

Stronger prompt:

```text
Write a LinkedIn post for founder-led service businesses that are using AI but feel their content sounds generic.

The audience is in the problem-recognition stage.

Use the Brand Voice AI position that AI should amplify human expression rather than replace it.

Open with a recognizable symptom, explain why generic AI content occurs, provide one practical action, and close with a question that invites a real conversation.

Use the approved brand voice profile and do not invent statistics or customer stories.

Target length: 180 to 250 words.

Provide a draft for human review.
```

# Voice Before Volume

## 39. Volume Is Not the First Goal

Creating more content before defining the voice may amplify:

- Confusion
- Contradictions
- Generic language
- Inaccurate offers
- Unsupported claims
- Weak positioning
- Inconsistent customer experiences

The recommended sequence is:

1. Define identity.
2. Define audience.
3. Define offer.
4. Capture voice.
5. Capture knowledge.
6. Establish AI instructions.
7. Establish human approval.
8. Produce consistently.
9. Measure and improve.
10. Scale carefully.

# Brand Voice AI Maturity Model

## 47. Level One: Fragmented

Characteristics:

- Knowledge lives in scattered files.
- Voice is undocumented.
- Offers are described inconsistently.
- AI prompts are improvised.
- Review is informal.
- Content sounds generic.

### Priority

Capture and organize the foundation.

---

## 48. Level Two: Documented

Characteristics:

- Mission, audience, offers, and voice are documented.
- Approved examples exist.
- Basic prompts exist.
- Human review occurs.
- Files have identifiable owners.

### Priority

Improve consistency and version control.

---

## 49. Level Three: Operational

Characteristics:

- Teams use shared guidance.
- AI instructions use approved context.
- Prompt and approval workflows are documented.
- Claims and sources are tracked.
- Content is reviewed systematically.

### Priority

Connect the system across channels.

---

## 50. Level Four: Integrated

Characteristics:

- Approved knowledge supports multiple teams.
- Retrieval systems provide current context.
- Human approvals are built into workflows.
- Customer journey stages guide communication.
- Performance data improves the knowledge base.

### Priority

Strengthen governance and monitoring.

---

## 51. Level Five: Governed Intelligence

Characteristics:

- Knowledge, voice, workflows, approvals, and analytics are connected.
- AI assists with research, messaging, campaigns, reporting, and training.
- Access controls protect sensitive knowledge.
- Human judgment remains visible.
- Updates flow through versioned systems.
- Automation can be monitored, corrected, paused, and retired.

### Priority

Scale without losing identity or accountability.

# Quick-Start Version

Use this abbreviated sequence when beginning a small Brand Voice AI implementation.

## Step One: Define the Brand

Complete:

- Mission
- Purpose
- Values
- Audience
- Offer
- Positioning

## Step Two: Capture the Voice

Document:

- Five voice characteristics
- Preferred vocabulary
- Language to avoid
- Three approved examples
- Three off-brand examples
- Signature phrases
- Storytelling style

## Step Three: Organize Knowledge

Collect:

- Offer descriptions
- FAQs
- Customer research
- Founder stories
- Approved claims
- Existing content
- Policies
- Current pricing

## Step Four: Create AI Instructions

Include:

- Role
- Audience
- Voice
- Sources
- Task
- Constraints
- Missing-information rules
- Human-review requirement

## Step Five: Test One Workflow

Choose one:

- Article
- Email
- Social post
- Lead magnet
- Campaign brief
- Customer-support response

## Step Six: Review

Check:

- Accuracy
- Voice
- Audience
- Journey stage
- Offer consistency
- Claims
- Privacy
- Permissions
- CTA

## Step Seven: Approve and Learn

Record:

- Final approval
- Corrections
- Audience response
- New customer language
- New knowledge
- Required framework updates

## Important Principle

Brand Voice AI does not begin with a prompt.

It begins with a person, a purpose, an audience, a body of knowledge, and a decision about what should remain human.

Only then should the machine enter the room.
