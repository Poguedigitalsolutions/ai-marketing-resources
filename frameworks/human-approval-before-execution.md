# Human Approval Before Execution Framework

Use this framework to define when AI-assisted work may proceed, who has authority to approve it, which reviews are required, and how an organization remains accountable before content is published, messages are sent, automations are activated, or consequential actions are taken.

This framework is part of the **Brand Voice AI** methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC**.

> AI may prepare the work. A human remains responsible for the decision to execute it.

---

# Core Principle

## Preparation Is Not Permission

A system may be able to:

- Draft
- Personalize
- Schedule
- Publish
- Send
- Recommend
- Route
- Approve technically
- Trigger another system

Technical capability does not equal authorization.

The operating model should remain:

```text
Human defines purpose
        ↓
AI prepares work
        ↓
Human reviews evidence and risk
        ↓
Human requests corrections
        ↓
Human approves the final version
        ↓
Authorized system executes
        ↓
Human monitors the result
```

# Why Approval Matters

## 1. AI Can Produce Confident Errors

AI-generated work may contain:

- Incorrect facts
- Fabricated sources
- Invented quotations
- Outdated information
- Unsupported claims
- Incorrect prices
- Incorrect deadlines
- Broken links
- Misleading summaries
- Unapproved stories
- Generic language
- Inappropriate tone
- Confidential information
- Incorrect personalization
- Missing context

Fluent language can make weak information appear reliable.

Human approval creates a checkpoint between plausibility and action.

---

## 2. Execution Creates Consequences

A draft can be corrected privately.

An executed action may:

- Reach thousands of people
- Damage customer trust
- Expose confidential information
- Trigger legal obligations
- Create financial loss
- Mislead a customer
- Create public confusion
- Start an unwanted workflow
- Send duplicate messages
- Publish an unsupported claim
- Affect a vulnerable person
- Create reputational harm

The closer an AI system moves toward execution, the stronger the approval requirement should become.

---

## 3. Responsibility Cannot Be Outsourced to the Tool

The organization remains responsible for:

- What was created
- What was approved
- What was sent
- What was published
- What was automated
- What customers experienced
- What data was used
- What claims were made
- What corrections were required

A tool does not become accountable merely because it generated the output.
