# Context Before Creation

Use this framework to make sure content starts with the right business, audience, offer, and situational context before drafting begins.

This framework is part of the Brand Voice AI methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC.**

> Better content does not start with better wording. It starts with better context.

---

## Framework Purpose

The Context Before Creation Framework helps organizations:

- clarify what the content is supposed to do
- define who the content is for
- align the message with the audience’s situation
- connect the content to the correct offer and customer journey stage
- reduce generic AI output
- improve relevance, consistency, and conversion potential
- support better human review and approval

---

## When to Use This Framework

Use this framework before:

- writing articles
- drafting emails
- creating social media posts
- building lead magnets
- preparing landing page copy
- repurposing long-form content
- developing campaign assets
- prompting AI tools for brand content

---

## Core Principle

AI should not create in a vacuum.

Before content is generated, the system should understand:

- the business objective
- the intended audience
- the customer’s problem
- the desired outcome
- the offer or next step
- the brand voice
- the facts, sources, and constraints

---

## Context Inputs

### 1. Business Context

Define the organizational context behind the content.

Include:

- business goal
- campaign goal
- strategic priority
- relevant offer
- brand positioning
- key differentiators

### 2. Audience Context

Define who the content is for.

Include:

- audience segment
- customer type
- industry or niche
- level of awareness
- problems and frustrations
- desired outcomes
- objections or concerns

### 3. Offer Context

Define what is being promoted or supported.

Include:

- offer name
- offer type
- value proposition
- benefits
- supporting proof
- call to action
- next step

### 4. Customer Journey Context

Define where the audience is in the journey.

Examples:

- unaware
- problem aware
- solution aware
- offer aware
- evaluating
- purchasing
- onboarding
- retention
- advocacy

### 5. Channel Context

Define where the content will appear.

Include:

- platform or channel
- content format
- length expectations
- platform norms
- audience expectations
- technical or formatting constraints

### 6. Brand Voice Context

Define how the message should sound.

Include:

- tone
- voice characteristics
- vocabulary preferences
- phrases to use
- phrases to avoid
- emotional tone
- storytelling style

### 7. Source and Fact Context

Define what information the content should rely on.

Include:

- approved source materials
- brand documents
- product details
- research
- statistics
- examples
- stories
- claims requiring verification

### 8. Approval and Risk Context

Define boundaries before drafting starts.

Include:

- privacy concerns
- compliance issues
- legal sensitivity
- accessibility expectations
- required review steps
- escalation needs
- restricted claims

### 9. Output Context

Define what the final deliverable should be.

Include:

- content type
- desired structure
- target length
- required sections
- required CTA
- success criteria
- deadline

---

## Context Questions

Before creating content, answer:

- What is this content supposed to accomplish?
- Who exactly is it for?
- What problem is the audience dealing with?
- What does the audience want next?
- What offer or action should this support?
- What stage of the journey is this for?
- What tone and voice should be used?
- What facts or sources must be included?
- What should be avoided?
- What does success look like?

---

## Minimum Context Packet

At minimum, every content request should include:

- objective
- audience
- offer
- customer journey stage
- brand voice guidance
- relevant source material
- CTA
- format requirements

---

## Warning Signs of Missing Context

Missing context often produces content that is:

- generic
- repetitive
- off-brand
- vague
- disconnected from the offer
- misaligned to the audience
- weak on calls to action
- hard to approve
- difficult to trust

---

## Human Review

A human reviewer should confirm that:

- the context is complete enough to create responsibly
- the content request matches the business goal
- the audience definition is clear
- the offer and CTA are correct
- the facts and sources are appropriate
- the requested output is realistic and safe

---

## Outcome

When context is established before creation, teams are more likely to produce content that is:

- relevant
- useful
- brand-aligned
- strategically focused
- easier to review
- more effective in moving the audience forward
