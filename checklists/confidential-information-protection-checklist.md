Use this checklist to identify, minimize, protect, review, and safely handle confidential information across marketing, research, customer communication, AI-assisted workflows, automation, file storage, collaboration, and publication.

This resource is part of the **Brand Voice AI** methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC**.

> The safest confidential information is the information that never enters a system unnecessarily.

# Part One: Information Classification

## 3. Classification Levels

### Public

Information approved for unrestricted public use.

Examples:

- Published website content
- Approved social media posts
- Public press releases
- Published reports
- Public contact information
- Approved marketing materials

### Internal

Information intended for authorized team members but not for public distribution.

Examples:

- Internal procedures
- Draft content
- Team notes
- Internal calendars
- Nonpublic planning documents
- General operating information

### Confidential

Information that could harm individuals, customers, employees, partners, or the business if exposed.

Examples:

- Customer records
- Employee information
- Private contracts
- Financial reports
- Unreleased campaign plans
- Customer interview transcripts
- Internal pricing strategy
- Vendor agreements

### Restricted

Highly sensitive information requiring the strongest access controls.

Examples:

- Passwords
- API keys
- Access tokens
- Payment-card information
- Protected health information
- Government identifiers
- Legal strategy
- Trade secrets
- Security configurations
- Encryption keys

# Part Seven: Data Minimization

## 18. Necessity Review

For each information element, ask:

- Is it necessary?
- Is it required for the stated purpose?
- Can the task be completed without it?
- Can a less-sensitive version be used?
- Can the information be aggregated?
- Can the information be anonymized?
- Can the information be replaced with a placeholder?
- Can the information be viewed without being copied?
- Can access be temporary?

Confirm:

- [ ] Only necessary information is collected.
- [ ] Optional fields are truly optional.
- [ ] Duplicate information is removed.
- [ ] Historical information is not retained without purpose.
- [ ] AI prompts contain the minimum context required.
- [ ] Files include only relevant pages or sections.
- [ ] Sensitive attachments are excluded when unnecessary.

## 21. Anonymization

Remove or transform:

- Names
- Email addresses
- Phone numbers
- Addresses
- Account identifiers
- Customer IDs
- Employee IDs
- Device identifiers
- Precise locations
- Unique job titles
- Rare personal characteristics
- Dates that identify an individual
- Free-text details that reveal identity

Confirm:

- [ ] Direct identifiers are removed.
- [ ] Indirect identifiers are reviewed.
- [ ] Small groups cannot be reidentified easily.
- [ ] Free-text fields are reviewed manually.
- [ ] Multiple datasets cannot easily be combined to reveal identity.
- [ ] Anonymous examples remain useful without becoming identifiable.

---

## 22. Pseudonymization

When identity must remain linkable internally:

- [ ] A substitute identifier is used.
- [ ] The identity key is stored separately.
- [ ] Access to the identity key is restricted.
- [ ] The substitute identifier is not meaningful publicly.
- [ ] The linking method is documented.
- [ ] The identity key has a retention limit.

## 66. Immediate Actions

When an incident occurs:

1. Stop the exposure.
2. Revoke access.
3. Disable public links.
4. Rotate affected credentials.
5. Pause affected automations.
6. Preserve necessary evidence.
7. Notify the incident owner.
8. Determine what information was involved.
9. Identify who may have accessed it.
10. Escalate for specialized review.
11. Document actions taken.
12. Notify affected parties when required.
13. Correct the underlying process.
14. Monitor for additional risk.
