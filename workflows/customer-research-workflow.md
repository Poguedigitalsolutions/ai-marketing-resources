# Customer Research Workflow

Use this workflow to plan, conduct, organize, analyze, approve, apply, archive, and refresh customer research without distorting customer language, exposing private information, overstating evidence, or allowing AI-generated assumptions to masquerade as customer truth.

This workflow is part of the **Brand Voice AI** methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC**.

> Customer research should reveal what people actually experience, not confirm what the business already hopes is true.

---

## Workflow Purpose

The Customer Research Workflow helps organizations:

- Define the decision the research should support
- Identify the correct customer segment
- Select appropriate research methods
- Prepare neutral research questions
- Obtain consent
- Protect privacy and confidentiality
- Capture direct customer language
- Separate evidence from interpretation
- Identify patterns without erasing differences
- Use AI to organize transcripts and themes
- Prevent AI from inventing customer insights
- Evaluate evidence quality
- Document unanswered questions
- Develop responsible recommendations
- Require human review before applying findings
- Connect findings to messaging, offers, content, and customer journeys
- Preserve research records
- Schedule future research
- Retire outdated findings

This workflow may be used for:

- Customer interviews
- Prospect interviews
- Buyer interviews
- Lost-deal interviews
- Customer surveys
- Onboarding research
- Customer-support analysis
- Review analysis
- Community research
- Sales-call analysis
- Usability research
- Offer validation
- Messaging research
- Customer-journey research
- Retention research
- Churn research
- Win-loss research
- Market-entry research
- Event feedback
- Workshop feedback
- Product research
- Service research
- Audience development
- Brand voice research
- Lead-magnet development
- Campaign planning
- Content strategy
- AI knowledge-base development

# Phase Seven: Prepare Research Questions

## 30. Question Design Principles

Questions should be:

- Clear
- Neutral
- Open when depth is needed
- Specific when clarity is needed
- Connected to actual experience
- Free from unnecessary assumptions
- Appropriate to the participant
- Easy to answer
- Respectful

Avoid:

- Leading questions
- Double questions
- Loaded language
- Questions that assume a problem
- Questions that pressure praise
- Questions that request confidential information unnecessarily
- Questions that ask participants to predict behavior with false certainty

---

## 31. Weak and Strong Questions

Weak:

```text
How helpful was our amazing onboarding process?
```

Stronger:

```text
Walk me through what happened after you signed up.
```

Weak:

```text
Would you agree that time is your biggest problem?
```

Stronger:

```text
What makes this problem difficult to solve?
```

Weak:

```text
Would you buy this?
```

Stronger:

```text
How would you decide whether something like this is worth paying for?
```

- Treating one story as proof

- [ ] Questions were asked consistently.
- [ ] Deviations are documented.
- [ ] Negative feedback was not challenged.
- [ ] The participant was not pressured.

- Clear customer language
- Consistent context
- Appropriate sample
- Limited contradictory evidence

### Directional Finding

Supported by:

- Small sample
- One method
- Emerging pattern
- Limited context
- Meaningful but incomplete evidence

### Anecdotal Finding

Supported by:

- One person
- One isolated event
- One quotation
- No broader verification

Anecdotes may inspire questions.

They should not automatically become strategy.

## Important Principle

Customer research should not turn people into data points and then forget the people.

AI may help organize transcripts, identify possible themes, compare evidence, and surface unanswered questions.

A human must still protect the participants, interpret the evidence, acknowledge uncertainty, and decide how the findings should be used.
