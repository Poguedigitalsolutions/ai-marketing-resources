# Performance Reporting Workflow

Use this workflow to define, collect, reconcile, analyze, review, approve, distribute, correct, refresh, archive, and retire marketing performance reports without sacrificing data integrity, privacy, accessibility, context, or human accountability.

This workflow is part of the **Brand Voice AI** methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC**.

> A report should not merely display numbers. It should help the right person understand what happened, why it may have happened, what remains uncertain, and what decision should come next.

---

## Workflow Purpose

The Performance Reporting Workflow helps organizations:

- Define the reporting objective
- Identify the intended report audience
- Clarify which decisions the report should support
- Establish reporting periods
- Assign data ownership
- Define metrics and formulas
- Document data sources
- Establish targets and benchmarks
- Review campaign, channel, content, lead, sales, revenue, cost, and customer metrics
- Check data completeness
- Reconcile conflicting sources
- Identify missing, delayed, duplicated, or excluded data
- Document attribution limitations
- Separate facts from interpretations
- Use AI responsibly for analysis and summaries
- Detect anomalies
- Review forecasts and assumptions
- Protect confidential information
- Apply access controls
- Aggregate data appropriately
- Create understandable charts and tables
- Use accessible formatting
- Prepare executive summaries
- Develop recommendations
- Require human verification
- Record approval
- Distribute reports responsibly
- Correct errors
- Refresh recurring reports
- Archive completed reports
- Retire outdated reporting systems

This workflow may be used for:

- Campaign reports
- Channel reports
- Content-performance reports
- Social-media reports
- Email reports
- Website reports
- Lead-generation reports
- CRM reports
- Sales-pipeline reports
- Revenue reports
- Cost reports
- Customer-acquisition reports
- Customer-retention reports
- Executive dashboards
- Monthly marketing reports
- Quarterly business reviews
- Annual marketing reviews
- Client reports
- Board reports
- Partner reports
- Internal team reports
- Experiment reports
- Attribution reports
- Forecasts
- Post-campaign reviews

# Workflow Overview

```text
Define the reporting objective
        ↓
Define the audience and decisions
        ↓
Define the reporting period
        ↓
Define metrics, targets, benchmarks, and formulas
        ↓
Identify data sources and owners
        ↓
Collect and preserve source data
        ↓
Review completeness, accuracy, and consistency
        ↓
Reconcile conflicting or duplicated records
        ↓
Calculate approved metrics
        ↓
Analyze performance and anomalies
        ↓
Separate facts, observations, interpretations, assumptions, and forecasts
        ↓
Create tables, charts, summaries, and recommendations
        ↓
Review privacy, confidentiality, and accessibility
        ↓
Human verification
        ↓
Final approval
        ↓
Distribute
        ↓
Correct, refresh, archive, or retire
```

## 48. Common Marketing Formulas

### Conversion Rate

```text
Conversions ÷ Eligible Visitors × 100
```

### Click-Through Rate

```text
Clicks ÷ Impressions × 100
```

### Lead Qualification Rate

```text
Qualified Leads ÷ Total Leads × 100
```

### Cost per Lead

```text
Campaign Cost ÷ Total Leads
```

### Cost per Qualified Lead

```text
Campaign Cost ÷ Qualified Leads
```

### Customer Acquisition Cost

```text
Total Acquisition Cost ÷ New Customers
```

### Return on Advertising Spend

```text
Revenue Attributed to Advertising ÷ Advertising Cost
```

### Marketing Return on Investment

```text
Attributed Return Minus Marketing Cost ÷ Marketing Cost × 100
```

### Customer Retention Rate

```text
Customers at End Minus New Customers ÷ Customers at Start × 100
```

### Unsubscribe Rate

```text
Unsubscribes ÷ Delivered Emails × 100
```

## Important Principle

A dashboard can be accurate and still be useless.

A report becomes valuable when it connects trustworthy evidence to a meaningful decision, while showing where the evidence ends and interpretation begins.

AI may help organize, calculate, compare, summarize, visualize, and identify patterns.

A human must still decide which metrics matter, whether the data deserves trust, what conclusions are justified, and which actions should follow.
