# Customer Interview Question Bank

Use this question bank to conduct neutral, respectful, evidence-focused interviews with prospects, customers, former customers, lost opportunities, users, buyers, partners, and other relevant participants.

This resource is part of the **Brand Voice AI** methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC**.

> The purpose of a customer interview is not to persuade someone to confirm what the business already believes. It is to understand what happened, how the person interpreted it, what they considered, and which words they naturally used.

---

# Interview Principles

## 1. Begin With a Decision

Before creating questions, complete this statement:


- Revise an offer
- Change the target audience
- Improve a landing page
- Build a lead magnet
- Change onboarding
- Update sales qualification
- Improve retention
- Understand lost deals
- Develop content
- Update AI instructions

## 2. Learn, Do Not Sell

During a research interview:

- Do not pitch
- Do not defend the business
- Do not correct the participant
- Do not teach unless safety requires clarification
- Do not promise a solution
- Do not pressure the participant
- Do not convert the interview into a sales call
- Do not force the participant to criticize or praise
- Do not treat agreement as the goal

A participant who disagrees may provide the most useful evidence in the room.

---

## 3. Ask About Real Experiences

Questions about real events usually produce stronger evidence than hypothetical questions.

Weak:

```text
Would you use a tool that solved this problem?
```

Stronger:

```text
Tell me about the last time you tried to solve this problem.
```

Weak:

```text
Would this feature be useful?
```

Stronger:

```text
How do you handle this task today?
```

Weak:

```text
Would you pay for this service?
```

Stronger:

```text
What have you already spent trying to address this problem?
```

---

## 4. Ask One Question at a Time

Avoid:

```text
What did you try, why did it fail, and how did that make you feel?
```

Use:

```text
What did you try?
```

Then:

```text
What happened?
```

Then:

```text
How did you interpret that result?
```

---

## 5. Preserve Silence

Silence may mean the participant is:

- Remembering
- Comparing
- Deciding what is safe to share
- Searching for the right words
- Recognizing something new

Do not rush to fill every pause.

---

## 6. Separate Evidence From Interpretation

Record separately:

- Exact quotation
- Observable event
- Participant memory
- Participant belief
- Participant interpretation
- Interviewer interpretation
- Assumption
- Unknown

Do not place all of these into one blended note.

# Consent and Opening

## 14. Interview Opening Script

```text
Thank you for taking the time to speak with me.

The purpose of this conversation is to understand your experience in your own words. This is a research conversation, not a sales call. There are no correct answers, and honest criticism is useful.

I may ask follow-up questions so I can understand specific events, decisions, or phrases more clearly.

You may skip any question, ask to pause, or end the interview at any time.
```

---

## 15. Recording Consent

```text
With your permission, I would like to record this conversation so I can review your words accurately instead of relying only on my notes.

The recording will be used for [approved purpose], stored according to [storage practice], and accessed by [approved people or roles].

Do I have your permission to record?
```

# Questions to Avoid

## 93. Leading Questions

Avoid:

```text

```

```text

```

## 94. Confirmation Questions

Avoid:

```text

```

Use:

```text

```

## 95. Hypothetical Purchase Questions

Avoid:

```text

```

Use:


---

## 96. Loaded Questions

Avoid:

Use:


## 97. Double Questions

Avoid:

# Interviewer Conduct

## 101. During the Interview

The interviewer should:

- Listen more than speak
- Use the participant’s words
- Ask for examples
- Ask about sequence
- Clarify meaning
- Preserve pauses
- Avoid visible approval or disapproval
- Avoid correcting language
- Avoid defending the organization
- Avoid offering solutions
- Avoid transforming criticism into a debate
- Respect time
- Respect emotional boundaries
- Protect private information

---

## 102. Interviewer Notes

Keep separate columns for:

| Evidence | Interviewer Interpretation |
|---|---|
| “[Exact statement]” | [Interpretation] |
| [Observable event] | [Interpretation] |
| [Participant memory] | [Interpretation] |

This prevents interpretation from wearing the costume of fact.

---

## 103. Interviewer Bias Review

After the interview, ask:

- Did I ask leading questions?
- Did I interrupt?
- Did I defend the business?
- Did I reward certain answers?
- Did I ignore contradictions?
- Did I focus too heavily on one theme?
- Did I treat memory as verified fact?
- Did I ask for specific examples?
- Did I preserve the participant’s language?
- Did I make assumptions about motive?

## 106. Confidence Levels

### High Confidence

- Supported by multiple independent participants
- Supported by behavior
- Current

### Medium Confidence

- Repeated by a limited number of participants

- Contains some contradiction
- Based partly on memory

- One participant

- Unverified memory
- Conflicting evidence

## Important Principle

A good customer interview does not make the interviewer look intelligent.

It creates enough space for the participant to remember, explain, question, contradict, and describe their experience without being pushed toward the answer the business hoped to hear.

AI may help transcribe, organize, code, compare, and retrieve approved interview evidence.

A human must still protect consent, preserve context, recognize uncertainty, and decide what the evidence actually supports.
