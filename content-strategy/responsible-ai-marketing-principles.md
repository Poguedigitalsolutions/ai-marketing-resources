# Responsible AI Marketing Principles

Use this guide to establish the human, ethical, operational, and governance principles required when artificial intelligence supports marketing research, strategy, writing, design, analysis, automation, personalization, publishing, customer communication, and decision-making.

This guide is part of the **Brand Voice AI** methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC**.

> AI may assist with the work. Humans remain responsible for the judgment, the consequences, and the promises made in public.

---

## Guide Purpose

The Responsible AI Marketing Principles help organizations:

- Preserve human accountability
- Protect truthfulness
- Verify facts and sources
- Protect privacy
- Protect confidential information
- Respect consent
- Respect permissions
- Review bias
- Review exclusion
- Review accessibility
- Provide appropriate transparency
- Define acceptable AI uses
- Define prohibited AI uses
- Require human approval before execution
- Classify risk
- Escalate high-risk situations
- Correct errors
- Document decisions
- Monitor AI-assisted systems
- Control automation
- Manage vendors and tools
- Preserve version history
- Remove unsafe or outdated practices
- Maintain public trust

These principles may apply to:

- Customer research
- Market research
- Brand voice development
- Content strategy
- Copywriting
- Editing
- Image generation
- Video generation
- Personalization
- Email marketing
- Social-media publishing
- Lead generation
- Advertising
- Customer support
- Sales enablement
- Analytics
- Reporting
- Marketing automation
- AI agents
- Retrieval systems
- Custom GPTs
- Recommendation systems
- Internal marketing tools

# Responsible AI Marketing Overview

```text
Define the business purpose
        ↓
Identify the people affected
        ↓
Classify the risk
        ↓
Confirm approved data and permissions
        ↓
Define acceptable and prohibited AI uses
        ↓
Generate or analyze
        ↓
Verify facts, claims, sources, privacy, bias, and accessibility
        ↓
Human review
        ↓
Human approval before execution
        ↓
Monitor outcomes
        ↓
Correct, pause, escalate, update, or retire
```

# Core Principle One: Human Accountability

## 1. Accountability Statement

The organization remains responsible for:

- Inputs
- Instructions
- Data selection
- Tool selection
- Generated outputs
- Published claims
- Automated actions
- Customer effects
- Errors
- Corrections
- Governance

Responsibility does not transfer to:

- The AI model
- The prompt
- The software provider
- The automation platform
- The customer
- The reviewer who was not given adequate context

# Core Principle Three: Source Integrity

## 8. Approved Source Types

Sources may include:

- Official documentation
- Primary research
- Customer interviews
- Approved customer evidence
- Internal operational records
- Qualified subject-matter experts
- Reputable third-party research
- Approved brand documentation
- Approved product documentation
- Other: [Describe]

---

## 9. Source Integrity Rules

- Preserve source identity.
- Preserve source date.
- Preserve relevant context.
- Preserve limitations.
- Preserve contradictions.
- Separate evidence from interpretation.
- Do not cite a source for a claim it does not support.
- Do not convert AI-generated summaries into primary evidence.
- Do not remove qualifiers merely to strengthen the message.

---

## 12. Data Minimization

Use only the data necessary for the approved purpose.

Ask:

- Is this information required?
- Can the task use less data?
- Can the data be anonymized?
- Can identifying details be removed?
- Is the tool approved for this information?
- Is retention necessary?
- Who can access the result?

# Core Principle Eight: Transparency

## 24. Transparency Standard

Transparency should be proportionate to:

- The role AI played
- The audience’s reasonable expectations
- The risk of misunderstanding
- The level of automation
- The sensitivity of the interaction
- Legal or platform requirements
- The possibility of material harm

---

## 25. Possible Transparency Statements

Examples:

```text
This content was developed with AI assistance and reviewed by a human.
```

```text
This response was generated by an automated assistant using approved company information.
```

```text
The image is AI-generated.
```

```text
AI assisted with summarization. The findings and recommendations were reviewed and approved by a human.
```

---

## 26. Transparency Review

Confirm:

- [ ] Disclosure requirements are identified.
- [ ] Disclosure is understandable.
- [ ] Disclosure is placed where people can see it.
- [ ] Disclosure does not exaggerate human review.
- [ ] Automated interactions are not falsely presented as human.
- [ ] Synthetic media is labeled where appropriate.
- [ ] Limitations are explained where material.
- [ ] Transparency language matches actual practice.

# Core Principle Nine: Appropriate AI Use

## 27. Generally Appropriate Uses

AI may assist with:

- Organizing approved information
- Summarizing approved sources
- Generating preliminary ideas
- Comparing options
- Drafting from approved context
- Identifying repetition
- Suggesting structural alternatives
- Preparing metadata
- Supporting accessibility work
- Categorizing feedback
- Identifying possible anomalies
- Preparing reports for human review

---

## 28. Conditionally Appropriate Uses

Require additional review for:

- Personalization
- Customer segmentation
- Automated responses
- Synthetic voices
- Synthetic images of people
- Predictive scoring
- Lead prioritization
- Sensitive audience targeting
- Health-related marketing
- Financial marketing
- Legal marketing
- Employment-related marketing
- Political or civic communication
- Children or vulnerable audiences

---

## 29. Prohibited Uses

AI should not:

- Fabricate evidence
- Fabricate endorsements
- Impersonate a real person deceptively
- Hide material automation
- Publish high-risk claims without review
- Use prohibited confidential data
- Make sensitive decisions without approved governance
- Manipulate vulnerable audiences
- Generate discriminatory targeting rules
- Continue operating after a critical failure
- Override required human approval

# Core Principle Seventeen: Automation Control

## 52. Automation Levels

### Assistive

AI prepares work for a human.

### Recommendatory

AI recommends an action, but a human decides.

### Conditional

AI acts only after defined approval or conditions.

### Autonomous

AI acts without immediate human approval.

Higher automation requires stronger controls.

---

## 53. Automation Review

Confirm:

- [ ] Automation level is documented.
- [ ] Human override exists.
- [ ] Stop conditions exist.
- [ ] Error handling exists.
- [ ] Logs are preserved.

- [ ] Customers can reach a human where appropriate.
- [ ] High-risk autonomous use is prohibited unless formally governed.

- The purpose no longer exists

- Data use is no longer permitted
- Sources become unreliable
- Bias cannot be controlled
- Accessibility remains inadequate
- Monitoring reveals harm
- Laws or policies change
- A safer process replaces it
- Human oversight is unavailable
- Maintenance exceeds value

## Important Principle

Responsible AI is not a sticker placed on the workflow after the machine has finished.

It begins before the prompt.

It shapes which data enters.

It determines which claims require proof.

It decides where automation must stop.

It protects the people represented in the output and the people affected by the action.

It keeps the human hand on the wheel when the road becomes narrow.

AI may help people research, organize, create, analyze, personalize, and scale.

A human must still answer the harder questions:

Is it true?

Is it fair?

Is it permitted?

Is it understandable?

Is it safe?

Is someone accountable?

And should this be done at all?
