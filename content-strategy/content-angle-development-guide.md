# Content Angle Development Guide

Use this guide to define, compare, test, approve, apply, measure, revise, combine, and retire content angles that turn a broad topic into a distinct, useful, audience-relevant framing.

This guide is part of the **Brand Voice AI** methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC**.

> A content angle is the lens through which a topic becomes meaningful. The topic tells you what you are discussing. The angle decides why this version deserves the audience’s attention.

---

## Guide Purpose

The Content Angle Development Guide helps organizations:

- Turn an approved topic into a distinct framing
- Separate angles from topics
- Separate angles from headlines
- Separate angles from hooks
- Separate angles from formats
- Separate angles from opinions
- Select an appropriate angle type
- Connect the angle to audience awareness
- Connect the angle to customer journey stages
- Test relevance
- Test originality
- Test evidence strength
- Test emotional weight
- Test brand fit
- Avoid manufactured controversy
- Avoid clickbait
- Avoid fear amplification
- Avoid generic AI framing
- Define the content promise
- Define supporting proof
- Define examples and stories
- Define the opening
- Define the structure
- Define the call to action
- Compare multiple angle options
- Use AI responsibly
- Require human approval
- Test angle performance
- Revise weak angles
- Combine overlapping angles
- Retire angles that no longer serve the content

This guide may be used for:

- Articles
- Blog posts
- LinkedIn posts
- Social-media posts
- Videos
- Podcasts
- Newsletters
- Email campaigns
- Webinars
- Workshops
- Lead magnets

# Phase One: Understand What a Content Angle Is

## 3. Content Angle Definition

A content angle is the specific perspective, framing, or interpretive lens used to present a topic.

A strong angle connects:

- Audience need
- Audience awareness
- Customer journey stage
- Strategic theme
- Brand point of view
- Evidence
- Emotional relevance
- Content promise
- Appropriate next step

---

## 4. Angle Versus Topic

### Topic

```text
AI-generated content and brand voice
```

### Angle

```text
Why faster content production can make a brand less recognizable
```

The topic is the subject.

The angle determines how the subject is framed.

---

## 5. Angle Versus Headline

### Angle

```text
Inconsistent prompts create inconsistent brand language
```

### Headline

```text
Why Your AI Sounds Like a Different Company Every Tuesday
```

The angle guides the idea.

The headline packages the angle for attention and understanding.

---

## 6. Angle Versus Hook

### Angle

```text
The hidden cost of skipping human review
```

### Hook

```text
The most expensive AI mistake may happen after the draft is finished.
```

The hook opens the content.

The angle shapes the entire content.

---

## 7. Angle Versus Format

### Angle

```text
A step-by-step method for evaluating AI voice consistency
```

### Format

```text
Checklist
Article
Video
Workshop
Carousel
```

The format determines delivery.

The angle determines perspective.

---

## 8. Angle Versus Opinion

### Opinion

```text
AI content is often generic.
```

### Angle

```text
Three specific ways generic AI language weakens customer trust
```

An opinion may be part of an angle, but an angle must create a useful path for the audience.

---

## 9. Angle Classification Test

Ask:

- Does it change how the topic is framed?
- Does it create a specific audience benefit?
- Does it fit the customer journey stage?
- Does it support one clear content promise?
- Can it guide the opening, proof, structure, and CTA?
- Is it broader than one headline?
- Is it more focused than the topic?
- Is it supported by evidence or expertise?

If most answers are no, it may be a headline, hook, opinion, format, or unsupported idea rather than a viable angle.

# Phase Seven: Select an Angle Type

## 27. How-To Angle

Purpose:

- Teach a process
- Help the audience complete a task
- Reduce uncertainty

Example:

```text
How to review AI-generated content for brand consistency
```

Use when:

- The audience is ready to act
- The process is understood
- The organization has practical expertise

---

## 28. Comparison Angle

Purpose:

- Compare methods, tools, options, or outcomes
- Support evaluation or decision-making

Example:

```text
Template-based content versus voice-grounded content
```

Use when:

- Alternatives are clearly defined
- Comparison criteria are fair
- Claims can be supported

---

## 29. Story Angle

Purpose:

- Use experience to reveal a lesson
- Make an abstract point concrete

Example:

```text
What answering my father’s business phone taught me about trust and referrals
```

Use when:

- The story is accurate
- The lesson fits the topic
- Permission and privacy are protected

---

## 30. Contrarian Angle

Purpose:

- Challenge a common assumption
- Offer a credible alternative view

Example:

```text
More content may be making your brand harder to recognize
```

Use when:

- The market assumption is represented fairly
- Evidence supports the challenge
- The angle does not manufacture conflict

---

## 31. Myth Angle

Purpose:

- Correct a common misunderstanding

Example:

```text
Myth: Brand voice is just tone
```

Use when:

- The myth is genuinely common
- The correction is evidence-based
- The content explains the nuance

---

## 32. Mistake Angle

Purpose:

- Help the audience avoid a recurring error

Example:

```text
The mistake of approving AI content without checking the source material
```

Use when:

- The mistake is real
- Consequences are represented accurately
- The content offers a constructive correction

---

## 33. Framework Angle

Purpose:

- Organize complexity into a usable model

Example:

```text
Voice Before Volume
```

Use when:

- The framework clarifies rather than oversimplifies
- The steps are practical
- The organization has authority to teach it

---

## 34. Proof Angle

Purpose:

- Demonstrate that a claim, method, or result is supported

Example:

```text
What customer interviews reveal about generic AI content
```

Use when:

- Evidence is available
- Limitations are visible
- Results are not exaggerated

---

## 35. Warning Angle

Purpose:

- Surface a meaningful risk

Example:

```text
Why confidential customer information should not enter public AI tools
```

Use when:

- The risk is real
- Severity is represented accurately
- Fear is not amplified beyond evidence
- Practical safeguards are included

---

## 36. Decision Angle

Purpose:

- Help the audience choose or evaluate a next step

Example:

```text
When to automate content approval and when to keep it manual
```

Use when:

- Decision criteria are clear
- Tradeoffs are visible
- No universal answer is implied

---

## 37. Question Angle

Purpose:

- Explore a focused audience question

Example:

```text
Does your AI content still sound like you?
```

Use when:

- The question is real
- The content provides a meaningful answer
- The question is not empty engagement bait

---

## 38. Research Angle

Purpose:

- Present findings, patterns, or evidence

Example:

```text
What customer interviews reveal about trust in AI-assisted marketing
```

Use when:

- Research methods are clear
- Sample limitations are visible
- Findings are not overgeneralized

---

## 39. Behind-the-Scenes Angle

Purpose:

- Explain process, judgment, or production

Example:

```text
How a brand voice guide becomes an AI review system
```

Use when:

- Internal details may be shared
- The process is genuinely useful
- Confidential information is protected

# Phase Thirteen: Avoid Manufactured Controversy

## 55. Manufactured Controversy Warning Signs

Watch for:

- Inventing an opponent
- Exaggerating disagreement
- Misrepresenting common advice
- Using outrage as a substitute for value
- Treating nuance as weakness
- Framing ordinary differences as conflict
- Attacking people instead of ideas
- Claiming something is “controversial” without evidence

---

## 56. Controversy Review

Ask:

- Is there a real disagreement?
- Is the opposing view represented fairly?
- Does the audience benefit from the comparison?
- Does the angle clarify rather than inflame?
- Is the evidence strong enough?
- Would the content remain useful without the conflict?

# Phase Fifteen: Avoid Fear Amplification

## 59. Fear Amplification Warning Signs

Watch for:

- Exaggerating consequences
- Presenting rare risks as common
- Hiding available safeguards
- Removing audience agency
- Using uncertainty as danger
- Threatening status or identity
- Presenting one failure as inevitable
- Using high-stakes language without evidence

---

## 60. Fear Review

- [ ] Frequency is represented accurately.
- [ ] Severity is represented accurately.
- [ ] Safeguards are included.
- [ ] Limitations are visible.

- [ ] The angle informs rather than alarms.

## Important Principle

A topic tells you where the conversation is happening.

The angle determines which door you open.

Choose the wrong door and the content may feel loud, generic, manipulative, or disconnected from what the audience actually needs.

Choose the right one and the subject sharpens.

The audience recognizes why it matters.

The evidence has somewhere to land.

The story has a purpose.

The call to action feels earned.

AI may help generate possibilities, compare framings, identify overlap, and prepare first drafts.

A human must still decide which framing is honest, useful, supportable, emotionally appropriate, and true to the brand.
