# Content Performance Review Guide

Use this guide to evaluate whether a published content asset reached the right audience, created meaningful progress, supported the intended customer journey stage, and produced evidence strong enough to guide the next decision.

This guide is part of the **Brand Voice AI** methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC**.

> Content performance is not a popularity contest. The real question is whether the asset helped the right people move in the right direction.

---

## Guide Purpose

The Content Performance Review Guide helps organizations:

- Define the performance question before reviewing metrics
- Match metrics to the content objective
- Match metrics to the intended audience
- Match metrics to the customer journey stage
- Match metrics to the format
- Match metrics to the channel
- Match metrics to the CTA
- Separate reach from relevance
- Separate engagement from progress
- Separate completion from conversion
- Separate conversion from customer quality
- Review retention signals
- Review trust signals
- Identify vanity metrics
- Review audience quality
- Compare expected and actual performance
- Identify content issues
- Identify distribution issues
- Identify offer issues
- Identify timing issues
- Identify measurement issues
- Preserve context and caveats
- Preserve sample limitations
- Preserve attribution limits
- Avoid unsupported causal claims
- Use AI responsibly
- Decide whether to keep, refresh, repurpose, promote, combine, pause, or retire the asset

This guide may be used for:

- Articles
- Blog posts
- LinkedIn articles
- Social-media posts
- Newsletters
- Emails
- Videos
- Podcasts
- Webinars
- Workshops
- Lead magnets
- Landing pages
- Case studies
- Reports
- Presentations
- Customer education
- Sales-enablement content
- Content series
- AI-assisted marketing systems


# Phase Three: Define the Measurement Model

## 10. Metric Categories

Review only the categories that support the performance question.

### Reach

Measures whether the asset was seen.

Examples:

- Impressions
- Unique reach

- Email delivery

### Engagement

Examples:

- Likes
- Reactions

# Phase Six: Separate Useful Metrics From Vanity Metrics

## 26. Vanity Metric Definition

A metric becomes a vanity metric when it looks impressive but does not help answer the performance question or guide a decision.

Possible examples:

- Impressions without audience relevance
- Likes without meaningful engagement
- Followers without fit
- Views without completion
- Clicks without destination quality
- Leads without qualification
- Downloads without use
- Revenue without attribution context

# Phase Twenty-Three: Select the Next Action

## 75. Keep

Use when:

- The asset remains accurate
- The audience is appropriate
- The objective is being met
- No major revision is needed

---

## 76. Refresh

Use when:

- Facts changed
- Sources are outdated
- Links broke
- Examples became stale
- CTA changed
- Performance declined because the asset aged

---

## 77. Repurpose

Use when:

- The core idea performs well
- Another format may reach the audience better
- The asset contains reusable sections
- Customer questions suggest derivative content

---

## 78. Promote

Use when:

- The asset performs well with relevant audiences
- Organic distribution is limited
- The CTA and offer are ready
- Paid or partner distribution is justified

---

## 79. Expand

Use when:

- Audience questions remain
- The topic has depth
- The asset could become a guide or series
- Strong completion or engagement signals exist

---

## 80. Combine

Use when:

- Assets overlap
- Each asset is too thin
- Search and navigation would improve
- A stronger consolidated resource can be created

---

## 81. Pause

Use when:

## Important Principle

Content metrics are footprints, not eyewitnesses.

They can show where people arrived, where they paused, where they continued, and where they disappeared into the digital woods.

They cannot always explain why.

A thousand impressions may represent broad visibility or broad irrelevance.

Ten thoughtful replies may reveal more strategic value than ten thousand passive views.

One qualified conversation may matter more than a glittering bucket of reactions.

AI may help organize metrics, compare results, group feedback, and surface possible patterns.

A human must still decide whether the right audience was served, whether the evidence supports the interpretation, whether causation is being overstated, and what the organization should do next.
