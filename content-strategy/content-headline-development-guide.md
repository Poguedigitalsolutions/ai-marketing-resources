# Content Headline Development Guide

Use this guide to define, compare, test, approve, publish, measure, revise, reuse, archive, and retire headlines that accurately represent the content, serve the audience, fit the platform, and earn attention without relying on clickbait, vague promises, or fabricated urgency.

This guide is part of the **Brand Voice AI** methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC**.

> A headline should make the value of the content easier to recognize. It should not make the truth harder to find.

---

## Guide Purpose

The Content Headline Development Guide helps organizations:

- Turn an approved topic, angle, and hook into a clear headline
- Distinguish headlines from hooks
- Distinguish headlines from titles
- Distinguish headlines from email subject lines
- Distinguish headlines from captions
- Match headlines to audience awareness
- Match headlines to customer journey stages
- Match headlines to platforms and formats
- Test clarity
- Test specificity
- Test relevance
- Test credibility
- Test curiosity
- Test brand fit
- Use appropriate headline structures
- Avoid clickbait
- Avoid vague claims
- Avoid inflated certainty
- Avoid keyword stuffing
- Avoid generic AI language
- Preserve accuracy during repurposing
- Compare multiple headline options
- Use AI responsibly
- Require human approval
- Test headline performance
- Revise weak headlines
- Reuse proven structures responsibly
- Archive or retire outdated headlines

This guide may be used for:

- Articles
- Blog posts
- LinkedIn articles

- Newsletters

# Phase One: Understand What a Headline Is

## 3. Headline Definition

A headline is the primary label or promise attached to a content asset.

A strong headline should:

- Represent the content accurately
- Reflect the approved angle
- Support the content promise
- Fit the audience’s awareness
- Fit the customer journey stage
- Fit the platform and format
- Be easy to understand
- Create appropriate interest
- Sound like the brand

---

## 4. Headline Versus Hook

### Headline

```text
Why Your AI Content Sounds Different Every Week
```

### Hook

```text
Your AI may be following your instructions perfectly and still making your brand sound confused.
```

The headline helps the audience decide whether to begin.

The hook begins the content after that decision.

---

## 5. Headline Versus Title

The terms are often used interchangeably, but they may serve different functions.

### Headline

Usually emphasizes immediate relevance, value, or interest.

```text
Five Signs Your AI Content Does Not Sound Like You
```

### Title

May identify a formal resource, report, book, course, or series.

```text
The Brand Voice Consistency Audit
```

---

## 6. Headline Versus Email Subject Line

### Headline

```text
How to Review AI Content for Brand Consistency
```

### Email Subject Line

```text
Does this draft still sound like you?
```

A subject line must work inside the inbox and may need preview-text support.

---

## 7. Headline Versus Caption

### Headline

Names or frames the main asset.

### Caption

Provides context, explanation, commentary, or a CTA around an asset.

A social caption may contain a headline-like opening, but it usually performs a broader communication role.

---

## 8. Headline Classification Test

Ask:

- Does it accurately represent the asset?
- Does it reflect the approved angle?
- Does it create a clear expectation?
- Can the content fulfill that expectation?
- Does it fit the platform?
- Does it fit the format?
- Does it fit the audience?
- Does it sound like the brand?

If most answers are no, it may be a hook, slogan, series label, caption, subject line, or unsupported promise rather than a viable headline.

# Phase Eight: Select a Headline Structure

## 30. Benefit Headline

Purpose:

- State the useful result

Example:

```text
Build a Brand Voice System Your AI Can Actually Follow
```

Use when:

- The audience understands the problem
- The result is realistic
- The content provides a credible path

---

## 31. Problem Headline

Purpose:

- Name a recognizable issue

Example:

```text
Why Your AI Content Sounds Different Every Week
```

Use when:

- The problem is real
- The audience recognizes it
- The content offers useful explanation or action

---

## 32. Question Headline

Purpose:

- Surface an audience question

Example:

```text
Does Your AI Content Still Sound Like You?
```

Use when:

- The question is genuine
- The content answers it
- The question is not empty engagement bait

---

## 33. List Headline

Purpose:

- Organize a defined set of items

Example:

```text
Seven Questions to Ask Before Approving AI-Generated Content
```

Use when:

- The number is accurate
- Every item is useful
- The list is not padded

---

## 34. How-To Headline

Purpose:

- Promise practical instruction

Example:

```text
How to Build a Brand Voice Guide for AI-Assisted Content
```

Use when:

- The content teaches the process
- The audience is ready to act
- The scope is realistic

---

## 35. Comparison Headline

Purpose:

- Compare methods, options, or outcomes

Example:

```text
Template-Based Content Versus Voice-Grounded Content
```

Use when:

- Comparison criteria are fair
- Both options are represented accurately
- Claims are supported

---

## 36. Story Headline

Purpose:

- Frame a lesson through experience

Example:

```text
What Answering My Father’s Business Phone Taught Me About Referrals
```

Use when:

- The story is central
- The lesson is clear
- Privacy and permission are protected

---

## 37. Proof Headline

Purpose:

- Lead with research, evidence, or demonstration

Example:

```text
What Customer Interviews Reveal About Generic AI Content
```

Use when:

- Evidence exists
- Methods and limitations are visible
- Findings are not overgeneralized

---

## 38. Mistake Headline

Purpose:

- Help the audience avoid a recurring error

Example:

```text
The AI Content Mistake That Happens Before the Prompt Is Written
```

Use when:

- The mistake is real
- Consequences are accurate
- The content provides a correction

---

## 39. Decision Headline

Purpose:

- Help the audience evaluate a choice

Example:

```text
When to Automate Content Approval and When to Keep It Manual
```

Use when:

- Tradeoffs are visible
- Decision criteria are included
- No universal answer is implied

---

## 40. Framework Headline

Purpose:

- Introduce a usable model

Example:

```text
Voice Before Volume: A Better Way to Scale AI Content
```

Use when:

- The framework is meaningful
- The model clarifies rather than oversimplifies
- The content explains application

---

## 41. Warning Headline

Purpose:

- Surface a real risk

Example:

```text
Before You Upload Customer Data to an AI Tool, Check These Five Risks
```

Use when:

- Risk is evidence-based
- Severity is accurate
- Practical safeguards are included

# Phase Seventeen: Avoid Inflated Certainty

Watch for:

- “Always”
- “Never”
- “Guaranteed”
- “Proven” without evidence
- “The only way”
- “Everyone”
- “No one”
- “Will” when the outcome is uncertain
- Universal conclusions from small samples

---

## 61. Certainty Review

Confirm:

- [ ] Certainty matches the evidence.
- [ ] Sample size is considered.
- [ ] Exceptions remain possible.
- [ ] Forecasts are labeled.
- [ ] Advice is not universalized unnecessarily.
- [ ] Guarantees are removed unless formally supportable.

- Redundant modifiers

## Important Principle

A headline is a promise printed on the front door.

It should tell the audience enough to decide whether entering is worthwhile.

It should not repaint the building, invent a treasure inside, or pretend the exit disappears in ten minutes.

AI may help generate variations, compare structures, shorten language, and adapt approved headlines for different platforms.

A human must still decide whether the headline is clear, honest, relevant, supportable, recognizable, and faithful to what the content actually delivers.
