# Content Outline Development Guide

Use this guide to turn an approved topic, angle, hook, headline, and content promise into a clear structure that guides research, drafting, review, production, publication, repurposing, and measurement.

This guide is part of the **Brand Voice AI** methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC**.

> An outline is the load-bearing frame of the content. If the logic wobbles here, polished sentences will only decorate the wobble.

---

## Guide Purpose

The Content Outline Development Guide helps organizations:

- Turn an approved idea into a structured content plan
- Distinguish outlines from briefs
- Distinguish outlines from drafts
- Distinguish outlines from scripts
- Distinguish outlines from tables of contents
- Select an appropriate structure
- Sequence ideas logically
- Assign a purpose to every section
- Connect sections through transitions
- Place evidence where it is needed
- Place stories where they strengthen understanding
- Place examples where they clarify application
- Address relevant objections
- Define calls to action
- Prevent repetition
- Prevent missing logic
- Prevent unsupported claims
- Prevent mid-content drift
- Adapt outlines to different formats
- Use AI responsibly
- Require human approval
- Review and revise outlines
- Preserve version history
- Archive and retire outdated structures

This guide may be used for:

- Articles
- Blog posts
- LinkedIn articles
- Social-media posts
- Videos
- Podcasts
- Newsletters
- Email sequences
- Webinars
- Workshops
- Lead magnets
- Case studies
- Reports
- Presentations
- Customer education
- Sales-enablement content
- Content series
- AI-assisted content systems

# Phase One: Understand What an Outline Is

## 3. Outline Definition

A content outline is a structured map of:

- What the content must accomplish
- What the audience needs first
- What ideas belong
- What order the ideas should follow
- What evidence supports each section
- What stories or examples clarify the message
- What objections require attention
- What the audience should understand or do next

An outline should be detailed enough to guide production without pretending to be the finished draft.

---

## 4. Outline Versus Content Brief

### Content Brief

Defines the broader assignment:

- Objective
- Audience
- Journey stage
- Format
- Channel
- Brand voice
- Sources
- CTA
- Production requirements

### Outline

Defines the internal structure of the asset:

- Opening
- Main sections
- Sequence
- Supporting evidence
- Transitions
- Conclusion

The brief explains the job.

The outline explains how the content will perform that job.

---

## 5. Outline Versus Draft

### Outline

```text
Section 2: Explain why inconsistent inputs create inconsistent voice
- Define the problem
- Provide one example
- Connect to brand trust
```

### Draft

Uses complete language, voice, rhythm, transitions, and detail.

The outline maps the path.

The draft walks it.

---

## 6. Outline Versus Script

A script usually contains:

- Full spoken language
- Timing
- Stage directions
- Visual cues
- Speaker cues
- Audio cues

An outline may guide the script, but it does not need to contain every word.

---

## 7. Outline Versus Table of Contents

A table of contents lists sections.

An outline explains:

- Why each section exists
- What it must cover
- What supports it
- How it connects to the next section

---

## 8. Outline Classification Test

Ask:

- Does it identify the content objective?
- Does it define the opening?
- Does it define the main sections?
- Does each section have a purpose?
- Is the order logical?
- Are evidence and examples assigned?
- Are transitions considered?
- Is the conclusion defined?
- Can a writer or producer use it?

If most answers are no, it may be a topic list, table of contents, loose notes, or partial brief rather than a usable outline.

## Important Principle

An outline should not be a drawer full of good ideas dumped onto the page in the order they arrived.

It should create a path.

The audience begins somewhere.

Each section moves them.

Evidence strengthens the bridge.

Stories make the terrain visible.

Transitions keep the route connected.

The conclusion arrives at the destination promised by the opening.

AI may help organize ideas, identify gaps, compare structures, and adapt approved material to different formats.

A human must still decide what belongs, what comes first, what requires proof, what should be removed, and whether the final structure serves the audience instead of merely containing information.
