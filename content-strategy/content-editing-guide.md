# Content Editing Guide

Use this guide to turn an approved content draft into a publication-ready asset that is clear, accurate, coherent, accessible, on-brand, properly sourced, and ready for final approval.

This guide is part of the **Brand Voice AI** methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC**.

> Editing should improve the content without sanding away the human fingerprints that make it recognizable.

---

## Guide Purpose

The Content Editing Guide helps organizations:

- Turn an approved draft into publication-ready content
- Separate editing stages
- Perform developmental editing
- Perform substantive editing
- Perform line editing
- Perform copyediting
- Perform proofreading
- Review structure
- Review logic
- Review evidence
- Review claims
- Review stories and examples
- Review objections
- Review transitions
- Review conclusions
- Review calls to action
- Preserve approved meaning
- Strengthen clarity
- Strengthen flow
- Strengthen brand voice
- Strengthen situational tone
- Remove filler
- Remove unnecessary repetition
- Replace vague language
- Replace generic AI phrasing
- Protect privacy
- Confirm permissions
- Verify citations
- Verify links
- Improve accessibility
- Match platform requirements
- Use AI responsibly
- Compare edited versions
- Resolve reviewer conflicts
- Approve final copy
- Archive replaced versions
- Retire obsolete edits

# Phase One: Understand the Editing Stages

## 3. Developmental Editing

Developmental editing reviews the whole asset.

Focus on:

- Objective
- Audience
- Customer journey stage
- Content promise
- Structure
- Logic
- Completeness
- Section order
- Evidence
- Examples
- Objections
- Conclusion
- CTA
- Strategic alignment

Developmental editing asks:

```text
Does this content work as a complete asset?
```

---

## 4. Substantive Editing

Substantive editing improves the strength and organization of the content.

Focus on:

- Section purpose
- Paragraph purpose
- Argument development
- Missing explanations
- Redundant sections
- Weak examples
- Unsupported sections
- Unclear transitions
- Inconsistent depth
- Content drift

Substantive editing asks:

```text
Does every part earn its place and support the whole?
```

---

## 5. Line Editing

Line editing improves the language and flow.

Focus on:

- Sentence clarity
- Rhythm
- Word choice
- Tone
- Voice
- Concision
- Emphasis
- Transitions
- Repetition
- Awkward phrasing
- Generic language

Line editing asks:

```text
Does this sound clear, natural, intentional, and recognizable?
```

---

## 6. Copyediting

Copyediting corrects technical language issues.

Focus on:

- Grammar
- Punctuation
- Spelling
- Capitalization
- Usage
- Style consistency
- Numbers
- Dates
- Titles
- Terminology
- Citations
- Formatting

Copyediting asks:

```text
Is the language technically correct and consistent?
```

---

## 7. Proofreading

Proofreading reviews the final prepared version.

Focus on:

- Typographical errors
- Missing words
- Repeated words
- Broken formatting
- Incorrect links
- Heading errors
- Caption errors
- Layout problems
- Version errors
- Final platform display

Proofreading asks:

```text
Is this exact version ready to publish?
```

---

## Important Principle

Editing should make the content easier to trust, easier to understand, and easier to recognize as belonging to the brand.

It should not turn every sentence into polished wallpaper.

The strongest edit preserves meaning while removing friction.

It strengthens logic without flattening personality.

It protects evidence without burying the reader in caution tape.

It improves rhythm without replacing the writer’s pulse.

AI may help identify repetition, grammar issues, vague language, structural gaps, and possible revisions.

A human must still decide what the author meant, which details matter, what should remain imperfect, what requires verification, and whether the final asset still sounds alive.
