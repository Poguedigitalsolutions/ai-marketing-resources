# Content Draft Development Guide

Use this guide to turn an approved outline into a complete first draft that preserves the intended audience, customer journey stage, headline, hook, angle, structure, evidence, stories, brand voice, and content promise.

This guide is part of the **Brand Voice AI** methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC**.

> A first draft does not need to be polished. It does need to be complete enough for a human reviewer to see the whole bridge, including where the boards are solid, where the supports are missing, and where the writer accidentally built a balcony instead of continuing across the river.

# Phase One: Understand What a First Draft Is

## 3. First-Draft Definition

A first draft is a complete working version of the content.

It should include:

- The opening
- The core argument, lesson, or process
- All major sections
- Evidence
- Sources
- Stories or examples
- Objection handling
- Transitions
- The conclusion
- The CTA
- Notes for unresolved issues

A first draft does not need perfect wording, but it should contain the full intended substance.

---

## 4. Draft Versus Outline

### Outline

```text
Section 3: Explain why inconsistent inputs create inconsistent voice
- Define the problem
- Show one example
- Connect to customer trust
```

### Draft

Explains the idea in complete language using the approved voice, evidence, examples, and transitions.

The outline maps the route.

The draft travels it.

---

## 5. Draft Versus Edit

### Drafting

Focuses on:

- Completeness
- Meaning
- Logic
- Evidence
- Examples
- Voice direction
- Forward movement

### Editing

Focuses on:

- Precision
- Clarity
- Flow
- Grammar
- Style
- Concision
- Final consistency

Drafting builds the house.

Editing checks the doors, removes the spare lumber from the hallway, and makes sure the kitchen is not somehow upstairs.

---

## 6. Draft Classification Test

Ask:

- Is the opening present?
- Are all approved sections present?
- Is the main idea fully explained?
- Are evidence and sources included?
- Are stories and examples included?
- Are objections addressed?
- Are transitions present?
- Is the conclusion complete?
- Is the CTA present?
- Can a reviewer assess the full asset?

If most answers are no, the work may still be notes, fragments, partial sections, or an expanded outline rather than a complete first draft.

## 30. Evidence Integration Rules

- Place evidence near the claim it supports.
- Explain why the evidence matters.
- Preserve qualifiers.
- Preserve sample limitations.
- Distinguish fact from interpretation.
- Distinguish correlation from causation.
- Preserve contradictory evidence.
- Use current sources when recency matters.
- Do not cite a source that does not support the exact statement.

## Important Principle

A first draft should not be mistaken for either a pile of notes or a finished monument.

Its job is to make the whole idea visible.

The argument should stand.

The sections should connect.

The evidence should be present.

The stories should have a purpose.

The voice should feel recognizable.

The unresolved parts should be labeled instead of covered with confident fog.

AI may help expand approved points, organize language, suggest transitions, and adapt drafts across formats.

A human must still decide what is true, what belongs, what needs proof, what sounds authentic, what should be removed, and whether the draft has earned the right to move into editing.
