# Content Topic Development Guide

Use this guide to define, test, prioritize, approve, schedule, measure, refresh, combine, and retire specific content topics that translate an approved content theme into useful, evidence-based assets.

This guide is part of the **Brand Voice AI** methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC**.

> A content topic is not just something interesting to say. It is a focused answer to a real audience question, connected to a strategic theme, supported by evidence, and shaped for a specific moment in the customer journey.

---

## Guide Purpose

The Content Topic Development Guide helps organizations:

- Turn approved themes into specific content topics
- Distinguish topics from pillars
- Distinguish topics from themes
- Distinguish topics from formats
- Distinguish topics from campaigns
- Distinguish topics from content series
- Connect topics to audience questions
- Connect topics to customer journey stages
- Test usefulness
- Test evidence
- Test originality
- Test timing
- Test strategic fit
- Define the content angle
- Define the content promise
- Define the supporting proof
- Define the story or example
- Define the call to action
- Prevent duplicate ideas
- Prevent thin ideas
- Prevent generic ideas
- Prevent trend-chasing
- Prioritize topics
- Use AI responsibly
- Require human approval
- Schedule production
- Measure performance
- Refresh outdated topics
- Combine overlapping topics
- Retire topics that no longer serve the strategy

This guide may be used for:

- Articles

# Phase One: Understand What a Content Topic Is

## 3. Content Topic Definition

A content topic is a focused subject or question narrow enough to become one primary content asset.

A strong topic connects:

- One audience
- One primary question
- One journey stage
- One strategic theme
- One clear promise
- Supporting evidence
- A useful next step

---

## 4. Topic Versus Pillar

### Pillar

A broad strategic territory.

```text
Brand Voice
```

### Topic

A specific subject within that territory.

```text
Five signs your AI-generated content does not sound like you
```

---

## 5. Topic Versus Theme

### Theme

A recurring strategic angle.

```text
Voice consistency
```

### Topic

A specific expression of that angle.

```text
Why inconsistent AI prompts create inconsistent brand language
```

---

## 6. Topic Versus Format

### Topic

```text
How to capture a founder’s natural language patterns
```

### Format

```text
Article
Video
Checklist
Carousel
Workshop
```

The topic is what the asset addresses. The format is how it is delivered.

---

## 7. Topic Versus Campaign

### Topic

```text
Why generic AI language weakens trust
```

### Campaign

```text
Stop Sounding Like Everyone Else’s AI
```

A campaign coordinates multiple assets, channels, messages, and calls to action over time.

---

## 8. Topic Versus Content Series

### Topic

```text
How to identify the three phrases your brand overuses
```

### Series

```text
The Brand Voice Audit Series
```

A series contains several connected topics in a deliberate order.

---

## 9. Topic Classification Test

Ask:

- Can this become one primary asset?
- Does it answer one main question?
- Is it narrower than the theme?
- Is it broader than one sentence or tip?
- Does it fit an approved pillar and theme?
- Does it serve a journey stage?
- Is there enough evidence to support it?
- Can the audience recognize its value?

If most answers are no, it may be a theme, campaign, format, series, fragment, or unsupported idea rather than a viable topic.

## Important Principle

A content topic should not be selected merely because it sounds clickable, feels timely, or gives the publishing calendar something to nibble on.

A strong topic has a job.

It answers a real question.

It belongs to a clear theme.

It serves a specific point in the customer journey.

It promises something useful and has enough evidence, insight, proof, and human judgment to deliver.

AI may help organize ideas, compare duplicates, suggest angles, and prepare outlines.

A human must still decide whether the audience truly needs the topic, whether the brand has the authority to address it, whether the evidence supports it, and whether publishing it will create clarity instead of more digital confetti.
