# AI Transparency and Disclosure Guide

Use this guide to decide when, where, and how an organization should disclose the role artificial intelligence played in marketing content, automated interactions, personalization, synthetic media, recommendations, and customer-facing actions.

This guide is part of the **Brand Voice AI** methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC**.

> Transparency should help people understand what they are experiencing. It should not bury the truth beneath legal fog, inflate the amount of human review, or make a machine appear more human than it is.

---

## Guide Purpose

The AI Transparency and Disclosure Guide helps organizations:

- Decide when AI use should be disclosed
- Match disclosure depth to risk
- Match disclosure depth to audience expectations
- Match disclosure depth to automation level
- Match disclosure depth to possible harm
- Distinguish AI assistance from AI generation
- Distinguish automated interaction from human interaction
- Distinguish synthetic media from traditional media
- Distinguish recommendation from autonomous action
- Write clear disclosure language
- Avoid misleading disclosures
- Avoid exaggerating human review
- Place disclosures where people can reasonably see them
- Disclose synthetic images
- Disclose synthetic video
- Disclose synthetic audio
- Disclose virtual spokesperson content
- Explain uncertainty
- Explain material limitations
- Explain personalization
- Explain automation
- Prevent deceptive impersonation
- Prevent false presentation of AI as human
- Review platform requirements
- Review contractual requirements
- Review legal or regulatory requirements
- Review organizational requirements
- Record disclosure decisions
- Record approvals
- Test disclosure clarity
- Monitor audience understanding
- Correct inadequate disclosures
- Reapprove disclosures when systems change
- Retire misleading or outdated disclosure practices

This guide may be used for:

- AI-assisted articles
- AI-generated posts
- AI-generated images
- AI-generated video
- AI-generated audio
- Virtual spokesperson content
- Chatbots

- Interactive experiences
- Recommendation systems
- Autonomous workflows

# Transparency and Disclosure Overview

```text
Define the activity
        ↓
Identify the role AI played
        ↓
Identify the audience and their reasonable expectations
        ↓
Classify the risk and possible harm
        ↓
Identify legal, platform, contractual, and organizational requirements
        ↓
Determine whether disclosure is required
        ↓
Select disclosure depth, language, placement, and timing
        ↓
Human review and approval
        ↓
Publish or activate
        ↓
Monitor understanding
        ↓
Correct, revise, reapprove, or retire
```

- Automation affects customer outcomes
- AI makes or recommends high-impact decisions
- The audience may reasonably expect a human
- Platform rules require disclosure
- Contract terms require disclosure
- Legal or regulatory requirements apply
- Lack of disclosure could create material misunderstanding

# Phase Five: Distinguish Disclosure Types

## 18. AI-Assistance Disclosure

Use when AI supported the work but a human substantially directed, reviewed, and approved it.

Example:

```text
This content was developed with AI assistance and reviewed by a human.
```

---

## 19. AI-Generation Disclosure

Use when AI generated a substantial portion of the final output.

Example:

```text
This content was generated with artificial intelligence and reviewed before publication.
```

Do not imply a deeper review than actually occurred.

---

## 20. Automated-Interaction Disclosure

Use when a person is interacting with an automated assistant.

Example:

```text
You are interacting with an automated assistant using approved company information.
```

---

## 21. Synthetic-Media Disclosure

Use for AI-generated or materially altered:

- Images
- Video
- Audio
- Voices
- Virtual presenters
- Reenactments
- Composite media

Example:

```text
This image was generated with artificial intelligence.
```

---

## 22. Personalization Disclosure

Use when AI materially changes content, recommendations, pricing, or experience based on user data.

Example:

```text
Recommendations may be personalized using information associated with your activity.
```

---

## 23. Recommendation Disclosure

Use when AI suggests but does not decide.

Example:

```text
This recommendation was generated by an automated system and should be reviewed before action.
```

---

## 24. Autonomous-Action Disclosure

Use when AI acts without immediate human review.

Example:

```text
This action was completed automatically under predefined rules and may be reviewed by a human upon request.
```

# Phase Six: Write Clear Disclosure Language

## 25. Disclosure Writing Principles

A disclosure should be:

- Clear
- Specific
- Accurate
- Proportionate
- Visible
- Understandable
- Consistent with actual practice
- Free from inflated claims
- Free from technical fog
- Free from deceptive reassurance

---

## 26. Avoid Misleading Language

Avoid statements such as:

- “Fully human reviewed” when review was partial
- “Human approved” when only a template was approved
- “No AI involved” when AI materially shaped the result
- “AI-assisted” when the output was almost entirely generated
- “Automated for convenience” when the system makes important decisions
- “Personalized for you” without explaining material data use where required

## Important Principle

Transparency is not a confession that AI exists.

It is an act of orientation.

It tells people who or what they are interacting with, how much human judgment was involved, what the system may not know, and where responsibility still lives.

A disclosure that is technically present but practically invisible does not create trust.

A disclosure that exaggerates human review creates a second problem while pretending to solve the first.

AI may help draft wording, compare versions, and test clarity.

A human must still decide what people reasonably need to know before they trust, rely on, respond to, or act upon the experience.
