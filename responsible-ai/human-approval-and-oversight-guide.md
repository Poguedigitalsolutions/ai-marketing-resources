# Human Approval and Oversight Guide

Use this guide to design, operate, document, monitor, and improve human review and approval systems for AI-assisted marketing activities.

This guide is part of the **Brand Voice AI** methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC**.

> Human approval is not a decorative checkbox placed beside an automated decision. It is a real transfer point where a qualified person can understand the work, challenge it, change it, reject it, or stop it.

---

## Guide Purpose

The Human Approval and Oversight Guide helps organizations:

- Identify which AI-assisted activities require review
- Identify which activities require formal approval
- Separate reviewer and approver responsibilities
- Separate publisher and operator responsibilities
- Separate monitoring and escalation responsibilities
- Match review depth to risk
- Give reviewers sufficient context
- Ensure approval applies to the exact output
- Ensure approval applies to the exact version
- Prevent automation from bypassing approval
- Define conditional approval
- Define rejection
- Define revision requirements
- Define stop conditions
- Define time-limited approval
- Manage approval queues
- Prevent rubber-stamping
- Record decisions
- Record comments
- Record corrections
- Preserve accountability
- Renew approval when material conditions change
- Monitor oversight effectiveness
- Pause or retire ineffective oversight systems

This guide may be used for:

- AI-generated content
- AI-assisted editing
- Customer-facing messages
- Email marketing
- Social-media publishing
- Advertising
- Personalization
- Lead scoring
- Customer segmentation
- Sales enablement
- Customer support
- Synthetic media
- AI agents
- Custom GPTs
- Retrieval systems
- Recommendation systems
- Analytics
- Reporting
- Marketing automation
- High-volume communication workflows

# Human Oversight Overview

```text
Define the AI-assisted activity
        ↓
Classify its risk
        ↓
Identify required review and approval points
        ↓
Assign qualified roles
        ↓
Provide the exact output, version, context, evidence, and limitations
        ↓
Review
        ↓
Approve, approve conditionally, revise, reject, pause, or escalate
        ↓
Record the decision
        ↓
Execute only the approved version
        ↓
Monitor outcomes
        ↓
Renew, revise, suspend, or retire approval
```

# Phase Two: Separate Oversight Roles

## 7. Reviewer

The reviewer evaluates the work.

Responsibilities may include:

- Checking accuracy
- Checking evidence
- Checking brand voice
- Checking audience fit
- Checking privacy
- Checking permissions
- Checking bias
- Checking accessibility
- Identifying risks
- Recommending revisions

A reviewer does not automatically have final approval authority.

---

## 8. Approver

The approver has authority to permit, condition, reject, pause, or escalate the activity.

Responsibilities include:

- Reviewing the exact output
- Reviewing material risks
- Confirming required controls
- Confirming reviewer completion
- Accepting or rejecting residual risk
- Authorizing execution
- Recording the decision

---

## 9. Publisher or Operator

The publisher or operator executes only the approved action.

Responsibilities include:

- Selecting the approved version
- Confirming publication settings
- Confirming scheduling
- Confirming recipients
- Confirming links
- Confirming tracking
- Stopping execution when conditions are not met

The publisher should not silently substitute a different version.

---

## 10. Monitoring Owner

The monitoring owner evaluates what happens after execution.

Responsibilities include:

- Reviewing complaints
- Reviewing errors
- Reviewing unexpected outputs
- Reviewing privacy concerns
- Reviewing bias signals
- Reviewing performance
- Triggering corrections
- Triggering reapproval

---

## 11. Escalation Owner

The escalation owner coordinates higher-level review when:

- Risk increases
- A critical error occurs
- A reviewer rejects the output
- Permission is unclear
- Sensitive information is exposed
- Specialist review is needed
- Automation acts outside its boundaries

# Phase Seven: Define Approval Types

## 27. Full Approval

Use when:

- Required reviews are complete
- Required controls are active
- No unresolved blocker remains
- Exact version is identified
- Execution conditions are satisfied

---

## 28. Conditional Approval

Use when execution is allowed only after specific conditions are completed.

Examples:

- Replace one citation
- Correct one claim
- Add a disclosure
- Remove private information
- Limit the audience
- Limit the send volume
- Complete a pilot first

Conditional approval should identify:

- Exact condition
- Condition owner
- Verification method
- Deadline
- Person authorized to confirm completion

---

## 29. Limited Approval

Use when approval is restricted by:

- Time
- Audience
- Channel
- Geography
- Volume
- Campaign
- Tool
- Model
- Data source
- Use case

---

## 30. Rejection

Reject when:

- Purpose is unclear
- Claims are unsupported
- Data use is unauthorized
- Risk is unacceptable
- Bias is uncontrolled
- Permission is missing
- Required review is unavailable
- Output is deceptive
- Automation cannot be controlled
- Harm cannot be reversed responsibly

# Phase Eight: Prevent Approval Bypass

## 32. Bypass Risks

Watch for:

- Publishing from a draft folder

- Replacing approved media
- Changing recipients
- Changing links
- Changing claims
- Changing prompts
- Changing data sources
- Changing the model
- Expanding audience size
- Increasing automation
- Reusing expired approval
- Using approval from another asset

- Role-based permissions
- Required approval status
- Publishing checklist
- Workflow gate
- Audit log
- Version identifier
- Restricted automation trigger
- Dual approval

- Human confirmation before execution

# Phase Fifteen: Use AI Responsibly in Oversight

## 54. Approved AI Uses

AI may assist with:

- Organizing review packages
- Identifying missing context
- Comparing versions
- Highlighting changed claims
- Identifying possible unsupported statements

## Important Principle

Human oversight only works when the human can do more than nod.

The reviewer must know what they are reviewing.

The approver must have authority.

The publisher must use the approved version.

The system must stop when approval is missing, expired, conditional, rejected, or attached to something else.

A queue moving quickly is not proof that oversight is working. Sometimes it is only a conveyor belt carrying unexamined decisions toward the publish button.

AI may help organize review materials, compare versions, flag missing context, and track approvals.

A human must still understand the decision, challenge the output, accept or reject the risk, and remain accountable for what happens next.
