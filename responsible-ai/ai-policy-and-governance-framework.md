# AI Policy and Governance Framework

Use this framework to establish the organization-wide policy, authority, controls, review requirements, documentation standards, and lifecycle rules governing artificial intelligence in marketing, communication, research, automation, analytics, customer experience, and decision support.

This framework is part of the **Brand Voice AI** methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC**.

> AI governance is not a folder of policies sleeping on a shared drive. It is the living system that decides what AI may do, what it must never do, who remains accountable, and when the organization must stop.

---

## Framework Purpose

The AI Policy and Governance Framework helps organizations:

- Establish responsible AI principles
- Define policy scope
- Define organizational applicability
- Define approved AI uses
- Define restricted AI uses
- Define prohibited AI uses
- Assign roles and accountability
- Classify risk
- Protect privacy
- Protect confidentiality
- Govern data use
- Review bias and exclusion
- Support inclusion
- Support accessibility
- Require transparency
- Require appropriate disclosure
- Require human approval
- Govern tools and vendors
- Govern integrations and agents
- Monitor active systems
- Audit actual behavior
- Respond to incidents
- Correct failures
- Train users
- Preserve documentation
- Control exceptions
- Enforce policy
- Require reapproval
- Suspend unsafe systems
- Retire outdated systems
- Maintain version history
- Review the policy regularly

This framework may apply to:

- Employees
- Contractors
- Consultants
- Agencies
- Vendors
- Partners
- Business units
- Marketing teams
- Sales teams
- Customer-support teams
- Research teams
- Data teams
- Technology teams
- Leadership
- Anyone using AI on behalf of the organization

# Section One: Organizational AI Principles

## 3. Human Accountability

The organization remains responsible for:

- AI inputs
- AI instructions
- Data selection
- Tool selection
- Generated outputs
- Automated actions
- Customer effects
- Published claims
- Errors
- Corrections
- Monitoring
- Governance

Responsibility does not transfer to the model, vendor, prompt, automation, or customer.

---

## 4. Human Before Machine

AI should support:

- Human judgment
- Human creativity
- Human decision-making
- Human communication
- Human accountability

AI should not replace required human authority in high-impact, sensitive, or customer-facing decisions.

---

## 5. Truthfulness

AI-assisted work must not:

- Invent facts
- Invent sources
- Invent quotations
- Invent testimonials
- Invent credentials
- Invent results
- Invent scarcity
- Invent urgency
- Present assumptions as verified evidence
- Present generated information as firsthand knowledge

---

## 6. Privacy and Confidentiality

AI use must:

- Follow approved data practices
- Minimize data
- Protect personal information
- Protect confidential information
- Exclude prohibited information
- Respect access controls
- Respect retention limits
- Support deletion where required
- Prevent unauthorized disclosure

---

## 7. Fairness, Inclusion, and Accessibility

AI use should:

- Avoid discriminatory treatment
- Avoid harmful stereotypes
- Avoid exclusionary defaults
- Preserve minority viewpoints
- Consider affected groups
- Consider language access
- Consider disability access
- Support accessible communication and experiences

---

## 8. Transparency

The organization should disclose AI involvement when:

- Audience expectations require it
- Automation may cause misunderstanding
- Synthetic media is involved
- Personalization materially affects the experience
- High-impact recommendations are made
- Platform, contractual, legal, or organizational rules require it

---

## 9. Human Approval Before Execution

Human approval should occur before:

- Publication
- Customer communication
- Automated sending
- Advertising launch
- Synthetic-media release
- High-risk recommendation
- Record modification
- Offer modification
- Pricing modification
- Autonomous action
- Removal of important content

---

## 10. Risk Proportionality

Controls should increase as risk increases.

Higher risk may require:

- Specialist review
- Additional approvals
- Restricted access
- Reduced automation
- Enhanced monitoring
- Stronger documentation
- Pilot testing
- Legal or compliance review
- Executive approval

# Section Five: Prohibited Uses

## 21. Prohibited Activities

AI must not be used to:

- Fabricate evidence
- Fabricate testimonials
- Fabricate endorsements
- Fabricate credentials
- Deceptively impersonate a real person
- Publish high-risk claims without approval
- Expose confidential information
- Use unauthorized personal data
- Exploit vulnerable audiences
- Create discriminatory targeting
- Override required human approval
- Continue after a critical stop condition
- Hide material automation
- Create deceptive synthetic media
- Access systems without authorization
- Make prohibited autonomous decisions
- Circumvent organizational controls

---

- Passwords

- Recovery codes
- Complete payment-card information
- Unapproved financial records
- Unapproved health records
- Unapproved legal records
- Restricted customer records
- Confidential information in unapproved tools
- Information prohibited by law, contract, or policy

# Section Seven: Risk Classification

## 32. Low-Risk Activities

Typical characteristics:

- Public or low-sensitivity data
- Internal use
- Assistive AI
- Low scale
- Easy reversibility
- Minimal potential harm
- Human review before use

---

## 33. Moderate-Risk Activities

Typical characteristics:

- Customer-facing output
- Personalization
- Moderate-sensitivity data
- Material claims
- Recommendatory or conditional automation
- Meaningful scale
- Required monitoring

---

## 34. High-Risk Activities

Typical characteristics:

- Sensitive data
- Vulnerable audiences
- Regulated claims
- Autonomous action
- Large scale
- Difficult reversibility
- Significant potential harm
- Decisions affecting access or opportunity
- Synthetic media involving real people

---

## 35. Prohibited Risk

Use when:

- Purpose is deceptive
- Data use is unauthorized
- Required oversight is unavailable
- Harm cannot be controlled
- The activity violates law, contract, policy, or values
- The system cannot be governed responsibly

---

## 36. Risk Review Requirement

Every moderate- or high-risk use should include:

- Risk classification record
- Required controls
- Stop conditions
- Assigned reviewers
- Assigned approver
- Monitoring plan
- Correction plan
- Reapproval triggers

## Important Principle

Governance begins where convenience stops being enough.

A useful tool can still be unsafe.

A fast workflow can still be unaccountable.

A technically accurate output can still be unfair, inaccessible, private, misleading, or attached to the wrong decision.

Policy gives the organization a shared compass.

It tells people which roads are open, which require permission, where the guardrails belong, and when the bridge ahead is closed.

AI may help organize policies, compare records, monitor systems, and surface potential gaps.

A human must still define the rules, enforce the boundaries, protect the people affected, and decide when an AI system has lost the privilege to act on behalf of the organization.
