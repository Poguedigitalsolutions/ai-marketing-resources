# AI Incident Response and Correction Guide

Use this guide to detect, contain, investigate, correct, document, monitor, and learn from incidents involving AI-assisted marketing systems.

This guide is part of the **Brand Voice AI** methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC**.

> An AI incident is not solved when the visible error disappears. The affected workflow, data, source, prompt, model, automation, derivative content, and approval process must all be examined before the system earns permission to continue.

# Incident Response Overview

```text
Detect the incident
        ↓
Stop or contain the affected activity
        ↓
Protect people, data, systems, and evidence
        ↓
Classify severity
        ↓
Assign incident ownership
        ↓
Investigate scope and root cause
        ↓
Correct outputs, systems, data, prompts, models, and workflows
        ↓
Notify affected parties where appropriate
        ↓
Verify the correction
        ↓
Human reapproval
        ↓
Restore, restrict, redesign, or retire
        ↓
Monitor for recurrence
```

# Phase Four: Classify Severity

## 13. Minor Incident

Possible examples:

- Typographical error
- Broken noncritical link
- Formatting problem
- Incorrect nonmaterial metadata
- Low-impact internal output error
- Easily corrected issue with no meaningful harm

Typical response:

- Correct
- Document
- Verify
- Monitor

---

## 14. Material Incident

Possible examples:

- Incorrect statistic
- Misleading claim
- Incorrect attribution
- Customer information exposed to an unauthorized person
- Unapproved customer-facing automation
- Missing material disclosure
- Biased targeting
- Inaccessible customer experience
- Wrong audience or recipient
- Wrong version published

Typical response:

- Pause
- Investigate
- Correct
- Notify relevant stakeholders
- Reapprove
- Monitor

---

## 15. Critical Incident

Possible examples:

- Credential exposure
- Large-scale personal-data exposure
- Health, legal, or financial information exposure
- Cross-customer data leakage
- Dangerous recommendation
- Serious discriminatory outcome
- Deceptive impersonation
- Major false claim
- Ongoing autonomous harm
- Security compromise
- Legal or regulatory violation

Typical response:

- Immediate containment
- Executive escalation
- Privacy, security, legal, or compliance review
- Notification assessment
- System shutdown
- Formal incident response
- Possible retirement

- Data use is no longer permitted
- Tool is no longer approved
- Repeated incidents occur
- A safer replacement exists
- Business purpose no longer exists

## Important Principle

An AI incident rarely lives in only one place.

The visible mistake may appear in a post, email, chatbot response, recommendation, report, image, or customer record.

Its roots may reach into a stale source, careless prompt, unapproved file, weak permission boundary, overloaded reviewer, missing disclosure, broken automation gate, or model that changed while everyone was looking elsewhere.

Deleting the visible output is only the first shovel of dirt.

The organization must find every branch, every copy, every automated reuse, every connected system, and every control that failed.

AI may help organize timelines, compare versions, surface related assets, and prepare incident records.

A human must still protect the people affected, decide what happened, approve the correction, accept or reject the remaining risk, and determine whether the system deserves to return.
