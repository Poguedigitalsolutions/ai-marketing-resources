# Responsible AI Implementation Roadmap

Use this roadmap to turn responsible AI principles, policies, controls, review systems, and governance resources into an organized implementation program.

This roadmap is part of the **Brand Voice AI** methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC**.

> Responsible AI is not installed in one afternoon. It is built through a sequence of decisions, controls, owners, tests, approvals, habits, and reviews that gradually turn good intentions into dependable practice.

---

## Roadmap Purpose

The Responsible AI Implementation Roadmap helps organizations:

- Assess current AI use
- Identify policy gaps
- Assign governance ownership
- Inventory tools and vendors
- Inventory data sources
- Classify data
- Classify AI risks
- Prioritize high-risk activities
- Establish immediate safeguards
- Select responsible pilot projects
- Define human approval systems
- Train users
- Establish monitoring
- Establish audit processes
- Prepare for incidents
- Build documentation standards
- Create reapproval processes
- Define governance maturity stages
- Implement a 30-day plan
- Implement a 60-day plan
- Implement a 90-day plan
- Assign owners
- Track dependencies
- Establish approval gates
- Measure implementation progress
- Sustain responsible AI governance over time

This roadmap may be used by:

- Founder-led businesses
- Marketing teams
- Agencies
- Consultants
- Service businesses
- Nonprofits
- Educational organizations
- Healthcare organizations
- Government contractors
- Technology teams
- Operations teams
- Leadership teams
- Organizations beginning AI adoption
- Organizations already using AI without formal governance

## 6. Current-State Stop Conditions

Pause or restrict activity immediately when:

- Sensitive data is entering an unapproved tool
- Credentials have been entered into an AI system
- Customer-facing automation lacks human oversight
- Synthetic media may be deceptive
- A tool has uncontrolled write or delete access
- High-risk claims are being published without review
- Personal information may be exposed
- An AI system is making decisions outside approved boundaries
- The organization cannot identify the system owner
- An active incident may still be causing harm

# Phase Nine: Select a Responsible Pilot

## 26. Pilot Selection Criteria

Choose a pilot that:

- Has a clear business purpose
- Uses approved data
- Uses an approved tool
- Is low or moderate risk
- Is reversible
- Has a limited audience
- Has a human reviewer
- Has measurable outcomes
- Can be paused quickly
- Can generate useful learning

---

## 27. Avoid as First Pilots

Avoid beginning with:

- High-risk autonomous decisions
- Sensitive customer data
- Legal, medical, or financial claims
- Large-scale customer outreach
- Synthetic impersonation
- Uncontrolled agents
- Write or delete access
- High-impact eligibility decisions
- Systems without rollback

# Governance Maturity Model

## 55. Level One: Ad Hoc

Characteristics:

- AI use is largely undocumented
- Tools are selected individually
- Data rules are unclear
- Human approval is inconsistent
- Monitoring is minimal
- Incident response is informal

Priority:

- Discovery
- Containment
- Ownership
- Immediate safeguards

---

## 56. Level Two: Emerging

Characteristics:

- Initial policy exists
- Tool inventory exists
- Risk classification begins
- Some approval controls exist
- Training is limited
- Monitoring is developing

Priority:

- Standardization
- Role clarity
- Training
- Pilot governance

---

## 57. Level Three: Defined

Characteristics:

- Policy is approved
- Approved, restricted, and prohibited uses are documented
- Risk classification is consistent
- Tool and vendor reviews are standardized
- Human approval is documented
- Monitoring and incident processes exist

Priority:

- Reliability
- Auditability
- Cross-team adoption
- Reapproval discipline

---

## 58. Level Four: Managed

Characteristics:

- Monitoring is active
- Audits are scheduled
- Drift is detected
- Incidents are analyzed
- Corrective actions are tracked
- Tool and vendor changes trigger review
- Governance metrics inform decisions

Priority:

- Optimization
- Automation controls
- Better metrics
- Stronger assurance

---

## 59. Level Five: Optimized

Characteristics:

- Responsible AI is embedded in operations
- Governance is proactive
- Controls are risk-proportionate
- Monitoring is mature
- Audits influence strategy
- Lessons improve systems across the organization
- Unsafe systems are retired quickly

Priority:

- Continuous improvement
- Strategic innovation
- Shared learning
- Governance leadership

## Important Principle

Responsible AI implementation is not a race to produce the thickest policy binder.

The goal is to create a working system.

People should know which tools they may use.

They should know which data must stay outside.

They should know when human approval is required.

They should know how to stop a system, report an incident, correct an error, and ask for help.

The first thirty days build visibility.

The next thirty build controls.

The final thirty build rhythm.

After that, governance becomes less like an emergency construction site and more like part of the organization’s nervous system.

AI may help organize inventories, classify preliminary risks, compare documents, track tasks, and summarize findings.

A human must still decide the priorities, approve the controls, protect the people affected, and determine whether expansion is responsible.
