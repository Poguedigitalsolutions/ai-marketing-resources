# Campaign Plan Example

This completed example demonstrates how the [Campaign Planning Template](../templates/campaign-planning-template.md) can be used to turn customer research, journey insights, brand voice, and content strategy into a coordinated marketing campaign.

The fictional company, audience, results, and campaign details in this example are not based on a real client.

---

## Example Business

**Business name:** Northstar Operations Group

**Business type:** Veteran-owned operations and process-improvement consultancy

**Primary services:**

- Workflow improvement
- Standard operating procedure development
- Process documentation
- Team training
- Operational readiness assessments
- AI-assisted workflow planning

**Primary audience:** Owners and operations leaders of growing service-based businesses

---

## 1. Campaign Overview

**Campaign name:** Fix the Process Before the Tool

**Campaign type:** Educational lead-generation campaign

**Primary offer:** Operational Bottleneck Assessment

**Campaign duration:** 30 days

**Primary channels:**

- LinkedIn
- Email
- Company website
- Founder outreach
- Educational webinar

**Campaign owner:** Founder

**Campaign status:** Planned

---

## 2. Campaign Purpose

The campaign should help Northstar Operations Group:

- Educate business owners about operational bottlenecks
- Explain why software may not solve an undefined workflow
- Build trust through practical guidance
- Generate qualified assessment conversations
- Identify recurring customer problems
- Test messaging before expanding the offer
- Demonstrate responsible use of AI and automation

The campaign should create useful conversations rather than maximize lead volume.

---

## 3. Business Objective

### Primary Objective

Generate qualified conversations with owners and operations leaders who are experiencing repeated workflow problems.

### Secondary Objectives

- Increase awareness of the Operational Bottleneck Assessment
- Build an email list of relevant prospects
- Learn which operational problems create the strongest response
- Identify common objections
- Test the message “Fix the process before adding the tool”
- Produce reusable educational content

---

## 4. Campaign Goals

During the 30-day campaign, Northstar aims to:

- Generate 20 qualified assessment inquiries
- Schedule 10 discovery conversations
- Complete 5 Operational Bottleneck Assessments
- Add 75 relevant contacts to the email list
- Collect at least 15 direct descriptions of operational problems
- Identify the three most common bottleneck categories
- Document campaign lessons for future improvement

These figures are fictional planning targets and should not be represented as actual results.

- Interest in software or automation

## 7. Customer Journey Stage

### Primary Stage

Solution Exploration

### Secondary Stages

- Problem Awareness
- Provider Evaluation

The campaign is designed for people who know something is wrong but may not yet understand whether the solution is:

- Better software
- Process documentation

- Automation
- Consulting

## 8. Customer Problems

The campaign should address problems such as:

- Every decision returns to the owner
- Employees receive inconsistent instructions
- Work slows down between departments
- The same mistakes keep happening
- Critical knowledge lives in one person’s head
- Procedures are outdated
- Software tools do not solve the real problem
- Approvals are unclear
- Automation creates more confusion
- Customers experience inconsistent service

---

## 9. Trigger Events

The audience may become ready to act after:

- A key employee resigns
- A major customer complains
- A deadline is missed
- The company opens another location
- Revenue grows faster than operational capacity
- Employee turnover increases
- An automation project fails
- An audit identifies weak controls
- The owner becomes unable to step away
- A manager discovers multiple versions of the same process

---

## 10. Core Campaign Message

> Fix the process before adding the tool.

Technology should support a clear workflow.

Before purchasing software or building automation, define:

- What starts the process
- Who owns each step
- What information is required
- Where decisions occur
- Who approves the result
- What happens when the normal path fails

---

## 11. Supporting Messages

### Message 1: Repeated mistakes may indicate a process problem

When several people make the same mistake, inspect the system before blaming the people.

### Message 2: Automation increases speed, not clarity

A flawed process may fail faster after automation.

### Message 3: Ownership matters

A workflow remains unreliable when no one owns the handoff, approval, or exception.

### Message 4: Start small

The business does not need to redesign every process at once.

### Message 5: Human approval still matters

High-impact decisions, sensitive communications, and exceptions should remain subject to human review.

---

## 12. Campaign Offer

### Operational Bottleneck Assessment

A focused assessment designed to help the customer identify where work slows down, repeats, disappears, or returns to the owner.

### Possible Deliverables

- Discovery conversation
- Current workflow review
- Bottleneck identification
- Ownership and handoff review
- Risk summary
- Priority recommendations
- Suggested next steps

### Offer Positioning

The assessment is not presented as:

- A complete transformation
- A guaranteed cost-saving program
- A replacement for employees
- A software implementation
- A legal, financial, or compliance audit

It is a structured starting point for understanding the real operational problem.

---

## 13. Value Proposition

Northstar Operations Group helps growing service businesses identify operational bottlenecks, clarify ownership, and design practical workflows before investing in more software or automation.

The value lies in helping the customer:

- See the current process clearly
- Identify the source of friction
- Reduce unnecessary complexity
- Make better technology decisions
- Protect important human approval points
- Choose the next improvement deliberately

---

## 14. Lead Magnet

### Title

Operational Bottleneck Quick Check

### Format

Downloadable checklist

### Purpose

Help business owners identify warning signs in one workflow.

### Checklist Questions

- Does work frequently wait for one person?
- Are employees using different instructions?
- Do tasks return for missing information?
- Are approvals unclear?
- Does the owner answer the same questions repeatedly?
- Are exceptions handled inconsistently?
- Is software creating duplicate work?
- Is one employee holding critical knowledge?
- Does the process fail during busy periods?
- Is anyone responsible for keeping the process updated?

### Lead Magnet Call to Action

> Find the step where work waits, repeats, or disappears.

---

- Company name

Collect only information necessary for the campaign.

## 19. Email Sequence

### Email 1: Deliver the Checklist

**Subject:** Your Operational Bottleneck Quick Check

Purpose:

- Deliver the resource
- Explain how to use it
- Encourage the reader to choose one workflow

Call to action:

> Review one process that repeatedly slows down.

### Email 2: People Problem or Process Problem?

Purpose:

- Reduce employee blame
- Introduce system-level thinking
- Encourage observation

Call to action:

> Look for instructions, ownership, and handoff gaps.

### Email 3: Do Not Automate Confusion

Purpose:

- Explain the risk of automating an undefined workflow
- Reinforce the central message

Call to action:

> Write down what should happen before choosing the tool.

### Email 4: Five Questions Every Workflow Should Answer

Questions:

1. What starts the process?
2. Who owns each step?
3. What information is required?
4. Who approves the result?
5. What happens when the normal path fails?

Call to action:

> Use the five questions to review one workflow.

### Email 5: Assessment Invitation

Purpose:

- Invite qualified readers to discuss a real bottleneck
- Keep the message low-pressure

Call to action:

> Schedule an Operational Bottleneck Assessment.

## 26. CRM Routing

### Lead Categories

- Checklist downloader
- Webinar registrant
- Webinar attendee
- Assessment inquiry
- Qualified opportunity
- Referral partner
- Not currently qualified

### Routing Rules

- Checklist downloads enter the nurture sequence
- Webinar registrations receive event reminders
- Assessment inquiries create a human review task
- Qualified opportunities are assigned to the founder
- Unqualified leads remain in educational communication only when consent exists
- Opt-outs are suppressed immediately

### Human Review Requirement

No lead should be labeled as a sales-qualified opportunity solely by AI without a person reviewing the available context.

## 30. Risk Register

### Risk: Unqualified Leads

Safeguard:

Use clear audience language and qualification questions.

### Risk: Sounding Anti-Technology

Safeguard:

Explain that tools are valuable when they support a defined process.

### Risk: Overpromising Results

Safeguard:

Describe the assessment deliverables without guaranteeing outcomes.

### Risk: Weak Consent

Safeguard:

Use clear opt-in language and immediate suppression after opt-out.

### Risk: Generic AI Content

Safeguard:

Require founder examples, original judgment, and human voice review.

### Risk: Privacy Exposure

Safeguard:

Use fictional examples and collect only necessary information.

### Risk: Excessive Automation

Safeguard:

Keep human approval before sales qualification and important communication.

### Risk: Employee-Blaming Language

Safeguard:

Inspect systems, instructions, handoffs, workload, and ownership before assigning responsibility.

### Risk: Campaign Fatigue

Safeguard:

Limit frequency and avoid repetitive content.

### Risk: Broken Links or Forms

Safeguard:

Test before launch and verify live versions.

- Label assumptions

- Document source links
- Confirm permissions

## 35. Attribution Limits

The campaign should not assume that one post, email, or webinar caused a sale.

Consider:

- Previous brand awareness
- Referrals
- Existing relationships
- Multiple content interactions
- Offline conversations
- Timing
- Business urgency
- Other marketing activity

Report attribution honestly and note uncertainty.

---

## 36. Post-Campaign Review Questions

Ask:

- Did the campaign reach the intended audience?
- Which message produced the strongest relevant response?
- Which asset generated qualified conversations?
- Which questions appeared repeatedly?
- Which objections remained unresolved?
- Did the campaign sound like the brand?
- Was the offer clear?
- Did the automation work correctly?
- Were consent and privacy protected?
- Did any content require correction?
- Which assets should be refreshed?
- Which assets should be reused?
- Which assets should be retired?
- What should change before the next campaign?

- Continue

- Revise
- Narrow the audience
- Change the offer
- Update the messaging
- Improve the landing page
- Adjust the email sequence
- Add new evidence
- Pause
- Retire

The decision should be documented and approved.
