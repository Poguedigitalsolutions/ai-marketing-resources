# Where Is Work Slowing Down?

Repeated mistakes, missed handoffs, delayed approvals, and owner overload may point to a workflow problem rather than a people problem.

Use this quick check to examine one process in your business.

The goal is not to redesign everything.

The goal is to find the step where work waits, repeats, disappears, or returns to the owner.

> Fix the process before adding the tool.

---

## How to Use This Quick Check

Choose one workflow that regularly causes frustration.

Examples include:

- Customer onboarding
- Scheduling
- Estimates and proposals
- Sales follow-up
- Order processing
- Service delivery
- Invoice approval
- Hiring
- Employee onboarding
- Customer complaints
- Content approval
- Marketing campaign launch

Answer each question based on how the workflow operates today.

Do not answer based on how the process is supposed to work.

- **0 points:** No, this is not currently a problem

## How to Interpret Your Score

### 0–15: Generally Stable

The workflow appears relatively clear, but individual weak points may still deserve attention.

Review any item scored **2**.

One serious weakness can matter even when the overall score is low.

### 16–30: Emerging Friction

The workflow contains recurring uncertainty or inconsistency.

Choose the highest-scoring section and investigate it first.

### 31–50: Significant Bottleneck Risk

The workflow likely creates delays, rework, confusion, or unnecessary owner involvement.

Document the current process before adding more software or automation.

### 51–65: High Operational Friction

The process may depend heavily on workarounds, individual memory, unclear approvals, or repeated intervention.

A structured workflow review is recommended.

### 66–80: Immediate Review Needed

The workflow may create serious operational, customer-experience, privacy, financial, or reputational risk.

Pause high-impact automation or process expansion until a qualified person reviews the workflow.

---

## Do Not Rely on the Total Score Alone

The total score provides direction, not a diagnosis.

Pay special attention to any item involving:

- Customer harm
- Privacy
- Confidential information
- Financial decisions
- Legal or regulatory requirements
- Safety
- Missing approval
- Unauthorized automation
- Repeated incidents
- Critical knowledge held by one person

A single high-risk issue may require immediate attention even when the overall score is low.

## Five Questions Every Workflow Should Answer

Use these questions to begin improving the process.

### 1. What starts the process?

Identify the event, request, form, deadline, customer action, or approval that begins the work.

### 2. Who owns each step?

Assign a person or role to every important action.

### 3. What information is required?

Identify what must be available before work may continue.

### 4. Who approves the result?

Define which decisions require human authorization.

### 5. What happens when the normal path fails?

Document exceptions, escalation, correction, and recovery.

## Questions to Ask Your Team

Use neutral questions.

- Where does this process become unclear?
- Which step takes longer than it should?
- What information is usually missing?
- Which instructions conflict?
- Where do you rely on memory?
- Which task returns for correction most often?
- Which approval causes the longest delay?
- What happens when the normal process fails?
- Which workaround do people use?
- What would make this workflow easier to follow?

The goal is to understand the system before assigning blame.

---

## Before You Add Automation

Confirm that:

- [ ] The current workflow has been observed
- [ ] The desired workflow has been documented
- [ ] Each step has an owner
- [ ] Required information is defined
- [ ] Decision rules are clear
- [ ] Human approval points are documented
- [ ] Exceptions have a path
- [ ] Failure and recovery steps exist
- [ ] Privacy and confidentiality have been reviewed
- [ ] Someone owns monitoring and correction
- [ ] The automation can be paused
- [ ] The final design has human approval

Do not automate confusion.

---

## Privacy Guidance

Do not place sensitive information into public AI tools, shared documents, screenshots, or public repositories.

Protect:

- Customer records
- Employee information
- Passwords and credentials
- Financial information
- Health information
- Legal information
- Proprietary processes
- Private communications
- Confidential performance data
- Internal system details

Use fictional, anonymized, or properly authorized examples when discussing the workflow outside the organization.
