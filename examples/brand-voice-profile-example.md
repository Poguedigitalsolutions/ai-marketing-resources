# Brand Voice Profile Example

This completed example shows how the [Brand Voice Profile Template](../templates/brand-voice-profile-template.md) can be used to document a clear, practical, and human-centered brand voice.

The fictional company in this example is not a real client.

---

## Example Business

**Business name:** Northstar Operations Group

**Business type:** Veteran-owned operations and process-improvement consultancy

**Primary services:**

- Workflow improvement
- Standard operating procedure development
- Team training
- Process documentation
- Operational readiness assessments
- AI-assisted workflow planning

**Business model:** Consulting, workshops, and implementation support

**Primary market:** Small and midsize service-based businesses

---

## 1. Brand Identity

### Brand Summary

Northstar Operations Group helps service-based businesses replace unclear, inconsistent, and overly complicated processes with practical systems their teams can understand and use.

The company combines military-informed readiness principles with approachable business consulting.

The brand should feel disciplined without becoming rigid, confident without becoming arrogant, and practical without becoming dull.

### Mission

To help organizations build clearer systems, stronger teams, and more dependable operations.

### Vision

A business environment where people are supported by clear processes rather than trapped inside confusing ones.

### Core Purpose

Help people do good work with less unnecessary friction.

### Brand Promise

Northstar Operations Group will leave clients with processes they understand, systems they can maintain, and decisions they can explain.

---

## 2. Core Values

### Clarity

People should understand what is expected, why it matters, and what happens next.

### Readiness

Strong organizations prepare before pressure arrives.

### Accountability

Responsibilities, approvals, and outcomes should have clear owners.

### Practicality

A process is only valuable when people can use it in real conditions.

### Respect

Systems should support people rather than treat them as replaceable parts.

### Continuous Improvement

Processes should be reviewed, tested, corrected, and improved over time.

---

## 3. Founder Perspective

The founder is a former military logistics specialist who spent years working in environments where unclear instructions, missing equipment, or weak handoffs could create serious consequences.

After leaving military service, the founder noticed that many small businesses suffered from similar operational problems:

- Important knowledge lived only in someone’s head
- Employees received inconsistent instructions
- Processes changed without documentation
- Automation was added before the workflow was understood
- Teams were blamed for problems caused by unclear systems

The founder believes most operational failures are not caused by lazy people.

They are often caused by unclear expectations, weak communication, missing ownership, and systems that grew without deliberate design.

---

## 4. Audience

### Primary Audience

Owners and operations leaders of service-based businesses with approximately 10 to 100 employees.

### Common Industries

- Professional services
- Home services
- Healthcare administration
- Training organizations
- Veteran-owned businesses
- Local and regional service companies
- Small government contractors

### Audience Characteristics

The primary audience is often:

- Responsible for too many decisions
- Frustrated by inconsistent execution
- Concerned about employee performance
- Dependent on tribal knowledge
- Interested in automation
- Unsure where the real bottleneck exists
- Tired of tools that create more complexity

### What the Audience Wants

They want:

- Clear processes
- Fewer repeated mistakes
- Better handoffs
- More consistent customer experiences
- Easier employee onboarding
- Less dependence on one key person
- Automation that supports the business
- Confidence that work will be completed correctly

### What the Audience Fears

They may fear:

- Losing control
- Wasting money on consultants
- Creating unnecessary bureaucracy
- Overcomplicating simple work
- Replacing employees with automation
- Exposing internal weaknesses
- Changing too much too quickly
- Becoming dependent on expensive software

---

## 5. Positioning

### Positioning Statement

Northstar Operations Group helps growing service businesses identify operational bottlenecks, document critical knowledge, and build practical workflows that improve consistency without burying teams in bureaucracy.

### Differentiators

Northstar Operations Group:

- Begins with the real workflow rather than the software
- Includes the people who perform the work
- Separates process problems from people problems
- Builds human approval into important decisions
- Documents systems in plain language
- Tests recommendations under realistic conditions
- Focuses on adoption, not merely delivery
- Treats automation as a tool rather than the goal

### What the Brand Does Not Claim

The brand should not claim that:

- Every process should be automated
- AI can replace experienced employees
- One system works for every company
- Operational improvement happens instantly
- Software alone will solve leadership problems
- A consultant can understand the business without listening to employees

The brand personality should be:

- Calm
- Capable
- Direct
- Prepared
- Observant
- Practical
- Respectful
- Steady
- Curious
- Human

The brand should not feel:

- Aggressive
- Militaristic
- Cold
- Corporate
- Overly technical
- Condescending
- Alarmist
- Trend-chasing
- Robotic
- Sales-heavy

## 7. Voice Characteristics

### Clear

Use direct language and explain unfamiliar terms.

Preferred:

> The handoff fails because no one owns the final approval.

Avoid:

> There appears to be a cross-functional accountability misalignment within the execution environment.

### Practical

Connect ideas to real actions, decisions, and consequences.

Preferred:

> Before adding automation, write down what should happen, who approves it, and what happens when something goes wrong.

Avoid:

> Businesses should embrace transformational automation ecosystems.

### Confident

Make clear recommendations when the evidence supports them.

Preferred:

> Fix the process before purchasing another tool.

Avoid:

> This may potentially be something you might want to consider exploring.

### Respectful

Do not blame employees without understanding the system.

Preferred:

> When several people make the same mistake, inspect the process before judging the people.

Avoid:

> Your employees keep failing because they are not committed.

### Human

Recognize emotion, pressure, uncertainty, and the realities of work.

Preferred:

> A process can look perfect on paper and still collapse during a busy Monday morning.

Avoid:

> Human variables should be removed from operational execution.

### Prepared

Anticipate failure paths, exceptions, ownership, and recovery.

Preferred:

> A workflow is incomplete until it explains what happens when the normal path fails.

Avoid:

> Once implemented, the process should operate automatically.

## 8. Tone

The brand voice remains consistent, but the tone may change based on the situation.

### Educational Content

Tone should be:

- Helpful
- Clear
- Curious
- Encouraging
- Evidence-aware

Example:

> A bottleneck is not always where the work stops. Sometimes it is where decisions wait, information disappears, or responsibility becomes unclear.

### Sales Content

Tone should be:

- Confident
- Specific
- Low-pressure
- Outcome-focused

Example:

> We help you find the process slowing the business down, document what should happen, and build a system your team can actually use.

### Client Onboarding

Tone should be:

- Reassuring
- Organized
- Transparent
- Collaborative

Example:

> We will begin by observing the current process. We are not looking for someone to blame. We are looking for where information, decisions, and ownership become unclear.

### Corrective Communication

Tone should be:

- Direct
- Calm
- Factual
- Solution-oriented

Example:

> The current workflow allows a request to move forward without final approval. We recommend pausing that step until an approval checkpoint is added.

### Crisis or Incident Communication

Tone should be:

- Serious
- Concise
- Accountable
- Free of speculation

Example:

> The automated message was sent to the wrong customer segment. The campaign has been paused, the cause is being reviewed, and affected records are being identified.

## 9. Language Preferences

### Preferred Words and Phrases

Use language such as:

- Clear
- Practical
- Ready
- Reliable
- Ownership
- Handoff
- Bottleneck
- Workflow
- Process
- Decision point
- Approval
- Human review
- Failure path
- Recovery
- Readiness
- Documented
- Tested
- Maintainable
- Real conditions
- What happens next
- Who owns this step?
- Fix the process before adding the tool
- Build systems people can use
- Prepare before pressure arrives

### Words and Phrases to Avoid

Avoid language such as:

- Revolutionary
- Game-changing
- Effortless
- Fully autonomous
- Set it and forget it
- Eliminate the human element
- Guaranteed results
- Instant transformation
- Replace your team
- Ten times your productivity overnight
- Foolproof
- Bulletproof
- Dominate your market
- Crush the competition
- AI-powered synergy
- Seamless transformation
- Unlock exponential growth

### Jargon Guidance

Operational and technical terms may be used when they are necessary, but they should be explained.

Example:

> A decision point is a place in the workflow where the next action depends on specific information, approval, or conditions.

Do not use jargon to create the appearance of expertise.

---

## 10. Sentence Style and Rhythm

Preferred sentence style:

- Mostly short and medium-length sentences
- Occasional longer sentences for explanation
- Clear transitions
- Active voice
- Direct questions
- Strategic repetition when reinforcing a principle
- Strong standalone lines for emphasis

Example:

> Do not automate confusion.
>
> First, define the process.
>
> Then identify the decision points, approvals, and failure paths.
>
> Only after that should you decide what technology belongs in the workflow.

Avoid:

- Dense corporate paragraphs
- Long strings of abstract nouns
- Excessive parenthetical statements
- Repetitive sentence openings
- Artificially dramatic fragments
- Overuse of exclamation marks
- Generic AI-generated summaries

---

## 11. Storytelling Guidance

Stories should show:

- A recognizable operational problem
- The human consequences
- The hidden system issue
- The turning point
- The practical lesson
- The action the audience can take

### Preferred Story Structure

1. Introduce the real situation
2. Show the visible problem
3. Reveal the underlying process issue
4. Explain what changed
5. Connect the lesson to the audience
6. Offer a practical next step

### Example Story Opening

> The owner believed the scheduling team kept making careless mistakes.
>
> But when we mapped the process, we discovered three different versions of the scheduling instructions. Each employee had been trained correctly. They had simply been trained on different systems.

### Storytelling Boundaries

Do not:

- Invent customer stories
- Exaggerate results
- Remove important context
- Identify clients without permission
- Present reconstructed dialogue as an exact quotation
- Use employee mistakes as entertainment
- Turn operational problems into shame-based marketing

When details are changed for privacy, state that clearly.

---

## 12. Evidence and Claims

Claims should be supported by:

- Documented client results
- Approved customer statements
- Operational records
- Process measurements
- Reliable research
- Clearly labeled experience or opinion

### Evidence Labels

Use language such as:

- Our review found
- The available records show
- Based on the current workflow
- In our experience
- The team reported
- The evidence suggests
- This is a working assumption
- This still requires verification

### Claims to Avoid

Avoid claims such as:

- This process will work for every business
- Automation always reduces costs
- Employees resist change because they are lazy
- AI will eliminate operational errors
- The system guarantees compliance
- This approach removes all risk

---

## 13. Calls to Action

Calls to action should be useful, specific, and low-pressure.

Preferred:

- Identify the step where work waits the longest.
- Ask your team where information usually disappears.
- Map the process before buying another tool.
- Choose one workflow to document this week.
- Review who owns the final approval.
- Tell us where your process slows down.
- Schedule an operational readiness conversation.

Avoid:

- Act now before it is too late!
- Stop losing money immediately!
- Your competitors are already ahead!
- Book now or get left behind!
- This one secret will transform your business!

---

## 14. Channel-Specific Guidance

### Website

The website should be:

- Clear
- Credible
- Structured
- Outcome-focused
- Easy to scan

Use direct descriptions of services, processes, and expected outcomes.

### LinkedIn

LinkedIn content may be:

- More conversational
- Story-based
- Reflective
- Educational
- Opinionated when supported by experience

Posts should connect operational ideas to leadership, employees, customer experience, or responsible AI.

### Email

Email should feel:

- Personal
- Useful
- Respectful of time
- Easy to respond to

Avoid excessive promotional frequency and manufactured urgency.

### Proposals

Proposals should be:

- Specific
- Organized
- Transparent
- Clear about responsibilities
- Clear about assumptions
- Clear about what is not included

### Training Materials

Training should be:

- Step-by-step
- Practical
- Scenario-based
- Free of unnecessary jargon
- Designed for real workplace conditions

### Automated Messages

Automated messages should:

- Clearly state their purpose
- Avoid pretending to be personally written when they are not
- Use only approved customer data
- Include escalation paths
- Remain subject to human review
- Be paused when errors occur

## 15. AI-Assisted Content Guidance

AI may assist with:

- Organizing research
- Comparing documents
- Drafting outlines
- Summarizing approved source material
- Generating first drafts
- Creating variations
- Repurposing content
- Identifying missing information
- Documenting workflows
- Preparing review checklists

AI must not:

- Invent customer evidence
- Fabricate quotations
- Make unsupported claims
- expose confidential information
- Approve high-risk communications
- Replace subject-matter review
- Impersonate the founder deceptively
- Publish without authorization
- Create false certainty
- Hide material limitations

### Required Human Review

A person must review:

- Facts
- Sources
- Quotations
- Client references
- Privacy
- Permissions
- Brand voice
- Operational recommendations
- Legal or regulatory claims
- Automated communications
- Final publication

## 16. Authenticity Risks

### Becoming Too Militaristic

Risk:

The veteran background could cause the brand to overuse military terminology or sound commanding.

Safeguard:

Use readiness and accountability principles without turning every business problem into a battlefield metaphor.

### Becoming Too Corporate

Risk:

Operational language may become abstract and impersonal.

Safeguard:

Use real workplace examples, plain language, and human consequences.

### Sounding Like Generic AI

Risk:

Content may become polished but interchangeable.

Warning signs include:

- Repetitive three-part lists
- Empty inspirational conclusions
- Excessive use of “unlock,” “leverage,” or “transform”
- Unnecessary summaries
- Overly balanced statements with no clear position
- Sentences that could belong to any consulting company

Safeguard:

Add founder judgment, specific examples, practical recommendations, and original language.

### Overpromising Automation

Risk:

The brand may sound as though AI or software can solve leadership, process, or accountability problems automatically.

Safeguard:

Repeat the principle:

> Fix the process before adding the tool.

### Blaming Employees

Risk:

Process failures may be presented as individual performance problems.

Safeguard:

Investigate instructions, ownership, tools, training, workload, and handoffs before assigning blame.

## 17. Approved Brand Examples

### Approved Example 1

> Your team may not have a performance problem.
>
> They may have three different versions of the process.
>
> Before you retrain the people, inspect the system they were trained to follow.

### Approved Example 2

> Automation should not make a bad process move faster.
>
> First define what should happen.
>
> Then decide what a person must review, what a system may handle, and what should happen when something goes wrong.

### Approved Example 3

> A reliable workflow answers five questions:
>
> What starts the process?
>
> Who owns each step?
>
> What information is required?
>
> Who approves the result?
>
> What happens when the normal path fails?

### Approved Example 4

> The goal is not to remove people from the process.
>
> The goal is to remove unnecessary confusion from their work.

---

## 18. Unapproved Brand Examples

### Unapproved Example 1

> Revolutionize your business with our game-changing AI automation solutions!

Why it fails:

- Generic
- Inflated
- Tool-centered
- Unsupported
- Does not reflect the brand’s practical voice

### Unapproved Example 2

> Eliminate human error and automate your entire operation effortlessly.

Why it fails:

- Makes an impossible promise
- Treats people as the problem
- Ignores risk and oversight
- Encourages irresponsible automation

### Unapproved Example 3

> Your employees are costing you money because they refuse to follow the process.

Why it fails:

- Blames people without evidence
- Creates shame
- Ignores system causes
- Conflicts with the brand’s respect value

### Content Requiring Standard Review

- Blog posts
- Social media posts
- Educational emails
- General website updates
- Training handouts
- Internal documentation

Standard review should confirm:

- Accuracy

- Privacy
- Source integrity

- Legal claims

## 20. Brand Voice Scorecard

Review each item on a scale of 1 to 5.

### Clarity

Does the message use direct, understandable language?

### Practicality

Does the message help someone make a decision or take action?

### Confidence

Does the message make a clear recommendation without exaggeration?

### Respect

Does the message treat employees, customers, and readers fairly?

### Human Quality

Does the message acknowledge real conditions, emotions, and consequences?

### Evidence

Are important claims supported or clearly labeled?

### Brand Distinctiveness

Could this message only come from Northstar Operations Group, or could it belong to any consulting company?

### Human Accountability

Is it clear what a person must review, approve, or decide?

A public-facing message should normally score at least 4 in every category before approval.
