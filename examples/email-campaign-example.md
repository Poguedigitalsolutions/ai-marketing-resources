# Email Campaign Example

This completed example demonstrates how the [Email Campaign Development Prompt](../prompts/email-campaigns.md) and [Email Development Workflow](../workflows/email-development-workflow.md) can be used to create a practical, human-centered nurture sequence.

The fictional company, audience, offer, and campaign details in this example are not based on a real client.

---

## Example Business

**Business name:** Northstar Operations Group

**Business type:** Veteran-owned operations and process-improvement consultancy

**Primary offer:** Operational Bottleneck Assessment

**Lead magnet:** Operational Bottleneck Quick Check

**Primary audience:** Owners and operations leaders of growing service-based businesses

---

## 1. Campaign Overview

**Campaign name:** Fix the Process Before the Tool

**Sequence type:** Lead-magnet delivery and educational nurture

**Number of emails:** Five

**Primary objective:** Help qualified readers identify workflow problems and decide whether an Operational Bottleneck Assessment would be useful.

**Sequence owner:** Founder

**Drafting support:** AI-assisted

**Final approver:** Founder

**Status:** Example only

---

## 2. Audience

The sequence is intended for people who:

- Downloaded the Operational Bottleneck Quick Check
- Gave valid consent to receive follow-up email
- Own or manage a growing service-based business
- Experience repeated workflow problems
- Are considering software, automation, training, hiring, or consulting
- Want clearer systems without unnecessary bureaucracy

---

## 3. Customer Journey Stage

**Primary stage:** Solution Exploration

**Secondary stages:**

- Problem Awareness
- Provider Evaluation

The reader knows something is slowing the business down but may not yet understand whether the real issue involves:

- Software
- Process design
- Ownership
- Training
- Communication
- Automation
- Several overlapping causes

---

## 4. Sequence Goals

The email sequence should:

- Deliver the promised resource immediately
- Explain how to use it
- Reduce employee-blaming language
- Teach the reader to inspect the workflow
- Explain the risks of premature automation
- Introduce five workflow questions
- Invite qualified readers to schedule an assessment
- Produce useful replies and customer language
- Respect consent and communication preferences

---

## 5. Core Message

> Fix the process before adding the tool.

Technology can support a clear workflow.

It cannot independently resolve unclear ownership, missing information, weak handoffs, inconsistent instructions, or undefined approval rules.

---

## 6. Voice and Tone

The sequence should sound:

- Clear
- Practical
- Calm
- Respectful
- Human
- Confident
- Low-pressure
- Evidence-aware

It should not sound:

- Aggressive
- Alarmist
- Overly technical
- Sales-heavy
- Anti-technology
- Militaristic
- Artificially personal
- Generic

---

## 7. Personalization Rules

Approved personalization may include:

- First name
- Company name when provided directly
- Role
- Resource downloaded
- Webinar registration status
- Explicitly submitted operational concern

Do not infer or fabricate:

- Revenue
- Employee performance
- Financial condition
- Internal business problems
- Sensitive personal traits
- Buying intent
- Emotional state

Do not insert personalization that makes an automated message appear to be a personal one-to-one note.

# Email 1: Deliver the Checklist

## Purpose

Deliver the Operational Bottleneck Quick Check and help the reader choose one workflow to review.

## Timing

Immediately after valid form submission.

## Subject Line Options

- Your Operational Bottleneck Quick Check
- Start with the step where work slows down
- Here is your workflow bottleneck checklist

## Preview Text

Use this checklist to identify where work waits, repeats, disappears, or returns to you.

## Email Body

Hi {{first_name}},

Here is your **Operational Bottleneck Quick Check**:

[Download the Operational Bottleneck Quick Check](#)

You do not need to review your entire business today.

Choose one workflow that repeatedly:

- Slows down
- Produces mistakes
- Requires repeated follow-up
- Returns to the owner
- Creates confusion between teams

Then use the checklist to look for missing information, unclear ownership, weak handoffs, inconsistent instructions, and undefined approvals.

The goal is not to assign blame.

The goal is to understand what the process is asking people to do.

Start with one question:

> Where does work wait, repeat, or disappear?

That answer usually gives you a useful place to begin.

John  
Northstar Operations Group

# Email 2: People Problem or Process Problem?

## Purpose

Help the reader inspect the system before deciding that employees are the sole cause of repeated problems.

## Timing

Two days after Email 1.

## Subject Line Options

- Is this a people problem or a process problem?
- Before you retrain the team, inspect the workflow
- Repeated mistakes may be telling you something

## Preview Text

When several people make the same mistake, the process deserves a closer look.

## Email Body

Hi {{first_name}},

When one person makes a mistake, individual performance may need attention.

When several people make the same mistake, inspect the process too.

Look for questions such as:

- Are employees using the same instructions?
- Is the current version easy to find?
- Does everyone understand who owns the next step?
- Are approvals clearly defined?
- What happens when information is missing?
- Are exceptions handled consistently?

A team cannot follow a process that exists differently in every manager’s head.

Before adding another training session, observe what people were actually taught to do.

You may discover that the problem is not effort.

It may be competing instructions, unclear ownership, missing information, or a handoff that no one fully owns.

A useful question for your team is:

> Where does this process become unclear?

Listen before you solve.

John  
Northstar Operations Group

## Primary Call to Action

Ask one employee where the process becomes unclear.

## Reply Prompt

Reply with the step that causes the most confusion.

## Human Review Requirements

Confirm that the email:

- Does not excuse genuine misconduct
- Does not blame employees or managers
- Avoids unsupported claims
- Preserves a balanced view
- Uses approved reply handling

# Email 3: Do Not Automate Confusion

## Purpose

Explain why software and automation should follow process definition.

## Timing

Three days after Email 2.

## Subject Line Options

- Do not automate confusion
- Automation makes the current process move faster
- Define the workflow before choosing the tool

## Preview Text

A flawed process does not become clear simply because it becomes automatic.

## Email Body

Hi {{first_name}},

Automation can save time.

It can move information, trigger reminders, route tasks, update records, and report exceptions.

But automation does not automatically create clarity.

Before automating a workflow, define:

1. What starts the process
2. Who owns each step
3. What information is required
4. Where decisions occur
5. Who approves the result
6. What happens when the normal path fails

Without those answers, automation may simply move confusion faster.

That can lead to:

- Incorrect routing
- Duplicate work
- Missing approvals
- Confusing notifications
- Customer frustration
- More manual cleanup

Technology should support the process.

It should not substitute for understanding it.

Before purchasing another tool, map one real workflow as it operates today.

Not the ideal version.

The version people actually use.

John  
Northstar Operations Group

## Primary Call to Action

Map one current workflow before choosing new software.

## Optional Resource Link

[Review the Customer Journey Mapping Framework](../frameworks/customer-journey-mapping.md)

## Human Review Requirements

Verify:

- No anti-technology framing
- No guaranteed outcome claims
- No unsupported statistics
- Accurate description of automation limits
- Approved resource links

# Email 4: Five Questions Every Workflow Should Answer

## Purpose

Give the reader a compact framework for reviewing any process.

## Timing

Three days after Email 3.

## Subject Line Options

- Five questions every workflow should answer
- Can your process answer these five questions?
- A practical test for any workflow

## Preview Text

Clear workflows explain ownership, information, approvals, and failure paths.

## Email Body

Hi {{first_name}},

A usable workflow should answer five basic questions.

### 1. What starts the process?

A request, purchase, form, customer action, deadline, approval, or event should clearly trigger the work.

### 2. Who owns each step?

Every important action needs a responsible person or role.

### 3. What information is required?

Missing information creates delays, rework, and unnecessary follow-up.

### 4. Who approves the result?

Important decisions should not move forward without clear authorization.

### 5. What happens when the normal path fails?

A workflow is incomplete when it explains only the ideal scenario.

It should also explain:

- Who handles exceptions
- When work pauses
- Who receives an alert
- How errors are corrected
- When escalation is required

Choose one workflow and write down your answers.

Any blank answer may reveal a bottleneck.

John  
Northstar Operations Group

## Primary Call to Action

Use the five questions to review one workflow today.

## Secondary Call to Action

Reply with the question your current process cannot answer clearly.

## Human Review Requirements

Verify:

- Questions match approved framework language
- Examples remain practical
- No industry-specific compliance claim is implied
- Reply handling is assigned to a person

# Email 5: Operational Bottleneck Assessment Invitation

## Purpose

Invite qualified readers to discuss a real operational problem without pressure.

## Timing

Four days after Email 4.

## Subject Line Options

- Where is work getting stuck?
- Ready to examine one workflow?
- An outside look at your operational bottleneck

## Preview Text

A focused assessment can help identify where information, ownership, decisions, or approvals become unclear.

## Email Body

Hi {{first_name}},

By now, you may have identified a workflow that:

- Repeatedly slows down
- Returns to you for decisions
- Produces inconsistent results
- Depends on one experienced employee
- Creates weak handoffs
- Has become harder after automation

Sometimes the hardest part is seeing the process clearly while you are still inside it.

The **Operational Bottleneck Assessment** is designed to help identify where work waits, repeats, disappears, or becomes unclear.

A typical assessment may include:

- A discovery conversation
- Review of one current workflow
- Bottleneck identification
- Ownership and handoff review
- Risk observations
- Priority recommendations
- Suggested next steps

The assessment is not a promise to transform the entire business overnight.

It is a structured place to begin.

[Schedule an Operational Bottleneck Assessment](#)

John  
Northstar Operations Group

## Primary Call to Action

[Schedule an Operational Bottleneck Assessment](#)

## Alternative Call to Action

Reply with a brief description of the workflow you are reviewing.

## Human Review Requirements

A person must review:

- Lead context
- Qualification
- Scheduling availability
- Offer scope
- Pricing
- Claims
- Follow-up response
- Any sensitive information shared by the prospect

No prospect should be classified as qualified solely through AI-generated scoring.

---

## 8. Consent Requirements

Before enrollment, confirm:

- The person submitted a valid email address
- Consent language was visible
- The communication purpose was clear
- Consent records are retained
- Unsubscribe is available
- Suppression rules are active
- Applicable platform and legal requirements have been reviewed

Do not add purchased, scraped, or unrelated contacts to this sequence.

---

## 9. Unsubscribe and Preference Language

Each email should include:

- Sender identification
- Business mailing information when required
- A working unsubscribe link
- Clear preference management when available

Example:

> You are receiving this email because you requested the Operational Bottleneck Quick Check. You may unsubscribe at any time using the link below.

Unsubscribe requests should be honored promptly.

---

## 10. Reply Management

Replies should be reviewed by a person.

Possible categories include:

- General question
- Operational problem description
- Assessment inquiry
- Referral
- Unsubscribe request
- Privacy request
- Complaint
- Technical problem
- Not currently interested

Sensitive or confidential information should not be copied into public systems or shared unnecessarily.

---

## 11. Automation Rules

Automation may:

- Deliver the checklist
- Send the approved sequence
- Apply campaign tags
- Record delivery status
- Create follow-up tasks
- Stop messages after unsubscribe
- Notify a person about replies
- Track approved performance data

Automation must not:

- Continue after opt-out
- Invent personal details
- Send an assessment proposal automatically
- Make final qualification decisions
- Generate unapproved promises
- Expose private information
- Respond autonomously to complaints or sensitive disclosures
- Change approved email content without review

---

## 12. Suppression Rules

Suppress or pause communication when:

- The person unsubscribes
- The email address hard-bounces
- A complaint is received
- Consent cannot be verified
- The person requests no further contact
- A privacy concern is raised
- The sequence contains a technical error
- The campaign is paused
- The contact becomes an active client and should enter a different communication path

---

## 16. Performance Review Questions

After the sequence runs, ask:

- Did the promised resource arrive correctly?
- Which email produced the most useful replies?
- Did the sequence reach the intended audience?
- Which operational problems appeared most often?
- Did any subject line overpromise?
- Did readers understand the difference between process and technology?
- Did the assessment invitation feel appropriate?
- Were unsubscribe and suppression rules reliable?
- Did any automation require correction?
- Which emails should be revised, retained, expanded, or retired?

---

## 17. Risks and Safeguards

### Risk: Artificial Personalization

Safeguard:

Use only directly provided information and approved fields.

### Risk: Excessive Follow-Up

Safeguard:

Limit the sequence to five useful messages and avoid pressure-heavy reminders.

### Risk: Employee-Blaming Language

Safeguard:

Inspect process, ownership, instructions, workload, and training before assigning responsibility.

### Risk: Overpromising Automation

Safeguard:

Explain that automation supports a defined workflow and still requires oversight.

### Risk: Weak Consent

Safeguard:

Use clear opt-in language, retained consent records, and immediate suppression.

### Risk: Generic AI Writing

Safeguard:

Require founder judgment, practical examples, brand review, and final human approval.

### Risk: Sensitive Replies

Safeguard:

Route replies to a person and avoid unnecessary copying or automated analysis.
