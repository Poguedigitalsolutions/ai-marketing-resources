# Customer Journey Map Example

This completed example demonstrates how the [Customer Journey Mapping Framework](../frameworks/customer-journey-mapping.md) can be used to connect customer needs, emotions, touchpoints, content, ownership, automation, and human approval.

The fictional company and customer journey in this example are not based on a real client.

---

## Example Business

**Business name:** Northstar Operations Group

**Business type:** Veteran-owned operations and process-improvement consultancy

**Primary services:**

- Workflow improvement
- Standard operating procedure development
- Process documentation
- Team training
- Operational readiness assessments
- AI-assisted workflow planning

**Primary customer:** Owners and operations leaders of growing service-based businesses

---

## 1. Journey Objective

The purpose of this journey map is to understand how a business owner moves from recognizing an operational problem to becoming a satisfied client and potential advocate.

The map should help Northstar Operations Group:

- Identify customer questions
- Understand emotional friction
- Create useful content
- Improve handoffs
- Clarify ownership
- Add appropriate automation
- Preserve human review
- Measure progress
- Reduce avoidable confusion

---

## 2. Primary Customer

### Customer Segment

The Operations-Weary Owner

### Customer Situation

The customer runs a growing service business with approximately 10 to 75 employees.

The business has become too complex for informal processes, but many workflows still depend on memory, direct supervision, spreadsheets, chat messages, or one experienced employee.

### Primary Goal

Create a more dependable business that does not require constant owner intervention.

### Primary Fear

Adding more bureaucracy, software, or consulting expense without solving the actual problem.

---

## 3. Journey Stages

1. Problem Awareness
2. Solution Exploration
3. Provider Evaluation
4. Decision
5. Onboarding
6. Discovery and Assessment
7. Design and Approval
8. Implementation and Training
9. Adoption and Improvement
10. Advocacy

---

# Stage 1: Problem Awareness

## Customer Situation

The customer notices repeated mistakes, missed handoffs, owner overload, inconsistent customer experiences, or dependence on one key employee.

## Customer Goal

Understand why the problem keeps happening.

## Customer Questions

- Why does this issue keep returning?
- Is this a people problem or a process problem?
- Why do employees need me to answer the same questions?
- Why does work slow down between departments?
- Why did the software not fix the problem?
- Is the business simply growing too fast?

## Customer Emotions

- Frustrated
- Overwhelmed
- Concerned
- Defensive
- Tired
- Uncertain

## Customer Friction

- The problem may appear in several departments
- Employees may blame one another
- The owner may not have time to investigate
- No reliable process data may exist
- Symptoms may be confused with causes
- The customer may assume another tool is needed

## Touchpoints

- LinkedIn posts
- Search results
- Referrals
- Business-owner groups
- Industry conversations
- Email newsletters
- Website articles
- Short assessments

## Helpful Content

- How to identify an operational bottleneck
- Signs that a process is unclear
- People problem versus process problem
- Why repeated mistakes deserve system review
- Questions to ask before buying new software
- Warning signs of undocumented knowledge

## Suggested Call to Action

> Identify the step where work waits, repeats, or returns to the owner.

## Owner

Marketing

## Automation Opportunities

- Deliver an operational bottleneck checklist
- Send a short educational email sequence
- Route assessment results by problem type
- Recommend relevant articles

## Human Approval Points

- Public claims
- Customer examples
- Statistical claims
- Diagnostic language
- Any high-risk recommendation

## Success Measures

- Article engagement
- Assessment completions
- Checklist downloads
- Qualified website visits
- Replies describing a real operational problem

---

# Stage 2: Solution Exploration

## Customer Situation

The customer begins comparing possible solutions, including software, hiring, training, automation, process documentation, or consulting.

## Customer Goal

Determine what kind of solution is actually needed.

## Customer Questions

- Do we need better software?
- Should we hire an operations manager?
- Can AI document our processes?
- Is consulting necessary?
- Can we solve this internally?
- How much change will this require?
- Where should we begin?

## Customer Emotions

- Curious
- Skeptical
- Hopeful
- Cautious
- Confused

## Customer Friction

- Too many solution categories
- Conflicting advice
- Tool-centered marketing
- Unclear pricing
- Fear of unnecessary complexity
- Lack of internal agreement

## Touchpoints

- Comparison articles
- Webinars
- Consultations
- Case studies
- Diagnostic tools
- Referrals
- Vendor demonstrations
- Educational videos

## Helpful Content

- Fix the process before adding the tool
- When software is not the answer
- What a workflow consultant actually does
- How to document a process
- When automation helps
- What should remain human

## Suggested Call to Action

> Map one current workflow before choosing the solution.

## Owner

Marketing and Business Development

## Automation Opportunities

- Send content based on assessment results
- Offer a scheduling link
- Provide a pre-consultation questionnaire
- Tag leads by problem category

## Human Approval Points

- Lead qualification rules
- Automated recommendations
- Pricing statements
- Claims about expected outcomes

## Success Measures

- Consultation requests
- Webinar attendance
- Return website visits
- Content completion
- Qualified lead rate

# Stage 3: Provider Evaluation

## Customer Situation

The customer compares Northstar Operations Group with other consultants, software providers, internal options, or no action.

## Customer Goal

Decide whether the provider is credible, practical, and safe to work with.

## Customer Questions

- Will they understand our business?
- Will they create unnecessary bureaucracy?
- How much employee time will this require?
- What will we receive?
- How will confidential information be protected?
- Do they understand automation risk?
- Will they disappear after delivering documents?
- Can they work with our team?

## Customer Emotions

- Cautious
- Analytical
- Protective
- Interested
- Suspicious of inflated promises

## Customer Friction

- Generic consulting language
- Weak examples
- Unclear scope
- Lack of pricing transparency
- Fear of exposing internal problems
- Concern about employee resistance
- Uncertainty about return on investment

## Touchpoints

- Website service pages
- Completed examples
- Discovery calls
- Proposals
- Referrals
- Testimonials
- Case studies
- LinkedIn profile
- Privacy and process documentation

## Helpful Content

- Completed workflow examples
- Methodology explanation
- Privacy and confidentiality practices
- Human approval approach
- Project timeline
- Roles and responsibilities
- What the consultant will not do

## Suggested Call to Action

> Schedule an operational readiness conversation.

## Owner

Founder or Sales Lead

## Automation Opportunities

- Confirm meetings
- Send preparation instructions
- Deliver relevant examples
- Record lead source
- Create follow-up reminders

## Human Approval Points

- Proposal language
- Scope
- Pricing
- Client references
- Confidentiality commitments
- Final qualification

## Success Measures

- Proposal requests
- Discovery-call completion
- Proposal acceptance rate
- Time from first inquiry to decision
- Qualified opportunity rate

# Stage 4: Decision

## Customer Situation

The customer is deciding whether to move forward.

## Customer Goal

Reduce uncertainty and confirm that the engagement is worth the investment.

## Customer Questions

- What exactly is included?
- Who needs to participate?
- How long will this take?
- What happens if the scope changes?
- How will success be measured?
- What happens after the engagement?
- Who owns the final deliverables?

## Customer Emotions

- Hopeful
- Nervous
- Financially cautious
- Ready for clarity
- Concerned about disruption

## Customer Friction

- Internal approval delays
- Budget concerns
- Competing priorities
- Unclear responsibilities
- Fear of employee resistance
- Contract questions

## Touchpoints

- Proposal
- Statement of work
- Contract
- Follow-up call
- Internal review
- Procurement
- Legal review

## Helpful Content

- Clear scope
- Deliverable list
- Timeline
- Participation requirements
- Assumptions
- Exclusions
- Approval process
- Success measures

## Suggested Call to Action

> Approve the scope and identify the internal project owner.

## Owner

Founder, Sales, and Operations

## Automation Opportunities

- Send reminders
- Track proposal status
- Route contracts
- Create internal project records

## Human Approval Points

- Final scope
- Contract
- Pricing
- Legal terms
- Data access
- Project ownership

## Success Measures

- Contract completion
- Time to signature
- Scope clarity
- Reduced pre-project questions
- Internal owner assigned

- Slightly overwhelmed

- Unclear ownership
- Scheduling conflicts
- Access delays
- Confidentiality concerns
- Employee uncertainty

## Touchpoints

- Kickoff meeting
- Onboarding form
- Shared workspace
- Access request
- Project plan
- Communication schedule

## Helpful Content

- Onboarding checklist
- Project timeline
- Roles and responsibilities
- Confidentiality guidance
- Interview schedule
- File-submission instructions
- Escalation path

## Suggested Call to Action

> Complete the onboarding checklist and confirm the project team.

## Owner

Project Manager

## Automation Opportunities

- Send welcome materials
- Collect documents
- Schedule interviews
- Create task lists
- Send reminders
- Track missing inputs

## Human Approval Points

- Data access
- Confidential material
- Participant list
- Recording consent
- Project scope confirmation

## Success Measures

- Onboarding completion time
- Missing-input rate
- Attendance
- Access readiness
- Stakeholder clarity

# Stage 6: Discovery and Assessment

## Customer Situation

Northstar observes current workflows, interviews employees, reviews documents, and identifies bottlenecks.

## Customer Goal

Help the consultant understand how work actually happens.

## Customer Questions

- Are we being judged?
- Will employee comments remain private?
- What problems will be uncovered?
- Will this create more work?
- What happens if departments disagree?
- How will findings be verified?

## Customer Emotions

- Exposed
- Defensive
- Curious
- Hopeful
- Concerned

## Customer Friction

- Conflicting accounts
- Missing documentation
- Employee fear
- Incomplete records
- Hidden workarounds
- Different process versions

## Touchpoints

- Interviews
- Observation
- Document review
- Process-mapping sessions
- Surveys
- System review
- Interim findings

## Helpful Content

- Interview expectations
- Confidentiality explanation
- Evidence-classification method
- Current-state maps
- Finding summaries
- Research limitations

## Suggested Call to Action

> Review the current-state findings and identify anything missing or incorrect.

## Owner

Consultant and Client Project Owner

## Automation Opportunities

- Schedule interviews
- Organize notes
- Tag evidence
- Track open questions
- Prepare draft summaries

## Human Approval Points

- Evidence interpretation
- Participant privacy
- Quotations
- Findings
- Contradictions
- Sensitive conclusions

## Success Measures

- Interview completion
- Evidence coverage
- Open-question resolution
- Stakeholder review
- Current-state approval

# Stage 7: Design and Approval

## Customer Situation

Northstar develops a future-state workflow, roles, approvals, procedures, and recommendations.

## Customer Goal

Approve a practical system that the team can use.

## Customer Questions

- Will this work in real conditions?
- Who owns each step?
- What happens when the normal path fails?
- Which parts should be automated?
- How much training is required?
- What will change for employees?
- How will exceptions be handled?

## Customer Emotions

- Engaged
- Cautious
- Optimistic
- Concerned about disruption
- Ready to decide

## Customer Friction

- Stakeholder disagreement
- Overly complex design
- Unclear exceptions
- Automation enthusiasm
- Approval delays
- Weak ownership

## Touchpoints

- Future-state map
- Draft procedures
- Review sessions
- Decision logs
- Automation recommendations
- Risk review
- Approval meetings

## Helpful Content

- Workflow diagrams
- Role definitions
- Decision points
- Approval checkpoints
- Failure paths
- Risk summary
- Implementation plan

## Suggested Call to Action

> Approve the workflow, assign owners, and confirm the implementation sequence.

## Owner

Consultant, Client Project Owner, and Department Leaders

## Automation Opportunities

- Track revisions
- Route approvals
- Maintain version history
- Send review reminders
- Record decisions

## Human Approval Points

- Final workflow
- Roles
- Automation
- Customer communications
- Privacy controls
- Exceptions
- Implementation plan

## Success Measures

- Approval cycle time
- Number of unresolved issues
- Stakeholder agreement
- Risk signoff
- Final design approval

# Stage 8: Implementation and Training

## Customer Situation

The approved workflow is introduced, tested, documented, and taught.

## Customer Goal

Adopt the new process without disrupting service.

## Customer Questions

- What changes today?
- Where are the instructions?
- Who answers questions?
- What happens if the process fails?
- How will feedback be handled?
- Is the old process still allowed?

## Customer Emotions

- Curious
- Resistant
- Hopeful
- Anxious
- Overloaded

## Customer Friction

- Habit
- Conflicting instructions
- Training gaps
- Technical issues
- Incomplete access
- Fear of accountability
- Parallel old processes

## Touchpoints

- Training
- SOPs
- Job aids
- Pilot tests
- Team meetings
- Help desk
- Manager coaching
- Feedback forms

## Helpful Content

- Quick-reference guides
- Step-by-step procedures
- Scenario exercises
- Escalation instructions
- Frequently asked questions
- Change summaries

## Suggested Call to Action

> Use the new process, record failures, and report unclear steps.

## Owner

Client Project Owner and Department Managers

## Automation Opportunities

- Assign training
- Track completion
- Send reminders
- Collect feedback
- Log issues
- Monitor adoption

## Human Approval Points

- Training content
- Go-live decision
- Automation activation
- Exception handling
- Incident response

## Success Measures

- Training completion
- Adoption rate
- Error rate
- Help requests
- Workflow completion time
- Employee feedback

# Stage 9: Adoption and Improvement

## Customer Situation

The new workflow is operating and must be monitored, corrected, and maintained.

## Customer Goal

Keep the process useful and prevent it from becoming outdated.

## Customer Questions

- Is the process working?
- Are employees using it?
- What should be improved?
- Are customers seeing better results?
- When should the documentation be updated?
- Who owns maintenance?

## Customer Emotions

- Relieved
- Analytical
- Watchful
- Proud
- Concerned about drift

## Customer Friction

- Old habits returning
- Documentation drift
- Untracked exceptions
- Weak ownership
- New software changes
- Missing performance data

## Touchpoints

- Performance reports
- Review meetings
- Employee feedback
- Customer feedback
- Audit logs
- Improvement backlog
- Refresher training

## Helpful Content

- Performance review
- Issue log
- Change-control process
- Updated SOPs
- Refresher materials
- Improvement recommendations

## Suggested Call to Action

> Review performance, approve corrections, and schedule the next workflow review.

## Owner

Client Process Owner

## Automation Opportunities

- Schedule reviews
- Monitor metrics
- Flag exceptions
- Send refresh reminders
- Track document versions

## Human Approval Points

- Process changes
- Metric interpretation
- Automation updates
- Corrective actions
- Retirement decisions

## Success Measures

- Reduced errors
- Faster completion
- Fewer repeated questions
- Lower owner intervention
- Better customer feedback
- Documentation currency
- Employee adoption

## 4. Cross-Journey Bottlenecks

Common bottlenecks include:

- Slow approvals
- Missing information
- Unclear ownership
- Weak handoffs
- Confidentiality concerns
- Internal disagreement
- Lack of evidence
- Overly complex recommendations
- Poor follow-up
- Automation without review

---

## 5. Cross-Journey Human Approval Points

Human review is required for:

- Public claims
- Customer quotations
- Client identification
- Pricing
- Contracts
- Confidential information
- Research findings
- Workflow recommendations
- Automated communications
- Go-live decisions
- Corrective actions
- Case studies
- Testimonials

---

## 6. Cross-Journey Automation Principles

Automation may support:

- Scheduling
- Reminders
- Form delivery
- Lead routing
- Document collection
- Task creation
- Approval routing
- Version tracking
- Performance monitoring
- Review scheduling

Automation should not:

- Invent findings
- Approve high-risk decisions
- Publish customer information
- Activate important workflows without authorization
- Hide uncertainty
- Remove necessary human judgment

---

## 7. Journey Measurement

Recommended measures include:

- Time between stages
- Conversion rate
- Drop-off rate
- Number of unanswered questions
- Approval delays
- Customer satisfaction
- Employee adoption
- Error reduction
- Owner intervention
- Repeat engagement
- Referral rate

Metrics should be interpreted with context.

A faster journey is not always a better journey if speed removes necessary review or creates risk.

---

## 8. Journey Improvement Questions

During review, ask:

- Where does the customer wait?
- Where does information disappear?
- Where does responsibility become unclear?
- Where do customers repeat the same question?
- Where does fear or uncertainty increase?
- Where is automation helpful?
- Where is human review essential?
- Which touchpoints build trust?
- Which touchpoints create friction?
- Which stage lacks useful content?
- Which handoff is most likely to fail?
