# Email Campaign Development Prompt

Use this prompt to build clear, useful, brand-aligned email campaigns without relying on generic urgency, artificial intimacy, exaggerated claims, or relentless selling.

This resource is part of the **Brand Voice AI** methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC**.

> A good email should feel like a relevant conversation arriving at the right time.

---

## Purpose

This prompt helps turn an audience need, business objective, article, offer, event, story, or customer insight into a structured email campaign.

The goal is to create emails that:

- Sound recognizable
- Respect the reader
- Serve a clear purpose
- Build trust over time
- Provide useful information
- Support the customer journey
- Make the next step easy to understand
- Preserve human review and judgment

AI can help organize, draft, compare, and improve the campaign.

The sender remains responsible for every message.

## Before You Begin

Gather the information that should guide the campaign.

Useful inputs include:

- Brand voice guidance
- Customer avatar research
- Customer journey information
- The offer
- The campaign goal
- A lead magnet
- An article
- Customer questions
- Sales objections
- Testimonials
- Case studies
- Product details
- Event information
- Pricing
- Deadlines
- Frequently asked questions
- Previous campaign results
- Compliance requirements
- A desired call to action

Do not ask AI to invent testimonials, customer results, statistics, quotations, deadlines, scarcity, guarantees, credentials, or personal experiences.

Do not upload private customer data, passwords, protected health information, financial information, or confidential records.

## Master Prompt

Copy the prompt below into your preferred AI assistant.

Replace the bracketed sections with your information.

```text
You are helping me develop a useful, ethical, and brand-aligned email campaign.

Your job is to organize the campaign strategy, map the reader journey, draft the emails, test the messaging, and improve clarity.

Do not invent facts, testimonials, customer results, statistics, quotations, pricing, deadlines, scarcity, guarantees, credentials, personal experiences, or legal claims.

Do not use manipulative urgency, false intimacy, guilt, pressure tactics, or exaggerated promises.

Separate confirmed information from interpretation.

When information is missing, label the gap rather than filling it with assumptions.

SENDER OR BRAND:

[Enter the person, company, or brand sending the emails.]

BRAND VOICE:

[Paste the brand voice summary, voice rules, examples, signature language, voice dos, and voice don’ts.]

CAMPAIGN TYPE:

[Welcome sequence, nurture sequence, launch, event promotion, onboarding, re-engagement, newsletter, follow-up, or another type.]

CAMPAIGN GOAL:

[Describe the primary business or communication objective.]

TARGET AUDIENCE:

[Describe the intended recipients.]

AUDIENCE SITUATION:

[Describe what the recipients are experiencing when the campaign begins.]

CUSTOMER GOAL:

[What are the recipients trying to accomplish?]

CUSTOMER PROBLEM:

[What problem, frustration, concern, delay, or opportunity makes the campaign relevant?]

CUSTOMER JOURNEY STAGE:

[Unaware, problem aware, solution aware, offer aware, decision, onboarding, retention, advocacy, or another stage.]

DESIRED READER OUTCOME:

[What should the reader understand, feel, believe, or do by the end of the sequence?]

OFFER OR RESOURCE:

[Describe the product, service, event, lead magnet, article, community, or next step being presented.]

VALUE PROPOSITION:

[Explain the practical value of the offer or resource.]

KEY BENEFITS:

[List the supported benefits.]

FEATURES OR DELIVERABLES:

[List what is included.]

PROOF:

[Add testimonials, case studies, credentials, results, demonstrations, examples, or other evidence that may be used.]

KNOWN OBJECTIONS:

[List the questions, doubts, concerns, risks, or barriers that may delay action.]

CUSTOMER LANGUAGE:

[Paste real customer phrases, questions, complaints, comments, emails, or interview excerpts.]

PERSONAL STORY OR EXPERIENCE:

[Describe any true story that should be included.]

CAMPAIGN LENGTH:

[Enter the number of emails.]

CAMPAIGN TIMING:

[Describe when the emails should be sent.]

DEADLINE OR TIME-SENSITIVE INFORMATION:

[Include only real dates, limits, availability, or deadlines.]

PRIMARY CALL TO ACTION:

[Describe the main action the reader should take.]

SECONDARY CALL TO ACTION:

[Describe an optional lower-commitment action.]

DESTINATION LINK:

[Add the relevant link.]

LEGAL OR COMPLIANCE REQUIREMENTS:

[List required disclosures, privacy considerations, unsubscribe requirements, regulated claims, or internal rules.]

WORDS, CLAIMS, OR STYLES TO AVOID:

[List language that feels generic, exaggerated, manipulative, overly promotional, false, corporate, or inconsistent with the brand.]

Using only the information supplied, create an Email Campaign Development Report with the following sections:

1. Information and Evidence Review

Separate the supplied information into:

- Confirmed facts
- Supported benefits
- Personal opinions
- Interpretations
- Claims requiring verification
- Missing information
- Information that should not be used
- Compliance concerns

Do not begin drafting until the distinction is clear.

2. Campaign Summary

State:

- Campaign type
- Primary audience
- Reader situation
- Campaign goal
- Main offer or resource
- Desired reader outcome
- Primary CTA
- Campaign length
- Recommended tone

3. Reader Journey

Map the reader’s likely progression through the campaign.

For each stage, include:

- What the reader may be thinking
- What the reader may be feeling
- What question needs to be answered
- What information should be provided
- What level of commitment is appropriate
- What should not be asked too early

4. Campaign Strategy Options

Create three possible campaign strategies.

For each strategy, include:

- Strategy name
- Central approach
- Emotional emphasis
- Educational emphasis
- Sales emphasis
- Best use case
- Risks or weaknesses

Recommend the strongest strategy and explain why.

5. Campaign Sequence

Build the complete email sequence.

For each email, include:

- Email number
- Purpose
- Reader journey stage
- Main message
- Supporting point
- Objection addressed
- Story or evidence used
- Primary CTA
- Secondary CTA
- Connection to the previous email
- Preparation for the next email

6. Subject Line Options

Create ten subject line options for each email across these styles:

- Direct
- Curiosity-based
- Benefit-focused
- Question-based
- Story-based
- Problem-focused
- Personal
- Practical
- Short
- Searchable or descriptive

Avoid clickbait, false urgency, vague bait, and unsupported promises.

7. Preview Text Options

Create five preview text options for each email.

The preview text should extend the subject line rather than repeat it.

8. Email Drafts

Write the full campaign.

Each email should include:

- Subject line
- Preview text
- Greeting
- Opening
- Main body
- Relevant proof, story, or explanation
- Primary CTA
- Optional secondary CTA
- Closing
- Sender name
- Optional P.S.

The emails should:

- Preserve the supplied brand voice
- Use readable paragraphs
- Respect the reader’s attention
- Avoid unnecessary jargon
- Avoid excessive exclamation points
- Avoid manufactured familiarity
- Avoid unsupported certainty
- Avoid repetitive calls to action
- Avoid sounding like every email is the final chance
- Make the next step clear
- Provide value even when the reader does not buy

9. Welcome Sequence Version

When relevant, create a welcome sequence containing:

Email 1: Welcome and expectation setting

- Confirm what the reader signed up for
- Deliver the promised resource
- Explain what they can expect
- Make one simple next step clear

Email 2: Problem and perspective

- Show understanding of the reader’s situation
- Introduce the sender’s point of view
- Avoid rushing into a sale

Email 3: Story and credibility

- Share a relevant true story
- Connect the experience to the reader’s problem
- Explain why the work matters

Email 4: Practical value

- Teach a useful framework, idea, checklist, or process
- Demonstrate how the sender thinks

Email 5: Invitation

- Present the next step
- Explain who it is for
- Explain who it may not be for
- Use a proportionate CTA

10. Nurture Sequence Version

When relevant, create a nurture sequence that balances:

- Education
- Stories
- Customer questions
- Objection handling
- Useful resources
- Case studies
- Behind-the-scenes thinking
- Soft invitations
- Direct offers

Explain the role of each email in building trust.

11. Launch Sequence Version

When relevant, create a launch sequence containing:

- Problem recognition
- New possibility
- Offer introduction
- Demonstration
- Proof
- Objection handling
- Frequently asked questions
- Deadline reminder
- Final reminder
- Post-launch follow-up

Only use deadlines and scarcity that were explicitly supplied.

12. Re-Engagement Sequence Version

When relevant, create a respectful re-engagement sequence containing:

- Recognition of the silence
- A useful reason to reconnect
- Preference options
- A relevant resource
- A clear choice to remain subscribed
- A graceful exit

Do not use guilt, shame, or emotional pressure.

13. Onboarding Sequence Version

When relevant, create a customer onboarding sequence containing:

- Welcome
- Immediate next step
- Access instructions
- Expectations
- Support information
- Early success milestone
- Common mistakes
- Progress check
- Feedback request
- Next-stage guidance

The sequence should reduce uncertainty and help the customer reach value quickly.

14. Story Integration Review

Evaluate every story used in the campaign.

For each story, explain:

- Why it belongs
- Which email should contain it
- What lesson it supports
- Whether it feels connected or forced
- Whether additional context is needed
- Whether it risks making the sender the hero instead of the reader

15. Objection Map

Create a table containing:

- Objection
- Evidence it exists
- Reader concern beneath the objection
- Best email for addressing it
- Recommended response
- Proof needed
- Risk of addressing it too aggressively

16. Trust-Building Review

Evaluate how the campaign builds trust through:

- Transparency
- Consistency
- Relevance
- Evidence
- Specificity
- Personal experience
- Useful education
- Clear expectations
- Human access
- Respect for reader choice

Identify where trust may be weakened.

17. Brand Voice Review

Review the campaign for:

- Tone
- Vocabulary
- Rhythm
- Sentence length
- Warmth
- Authority
- Storytelling
- Signature language
- Voice dos
- Voice don’ts
- Generic AI phrasing
- Excessive polish
- Overpromotion
- Unnatural transitions

Identify emails or passages that do not sound recognizable.

18. Generic Email Language Audit

Highlight phrases that sound:

- Generic
- Corporate
- Manipulative
- Overly polished
- Artificial
- Predictable
- Sales-heavy
- Emotionally empty
- Urgent without reason
- Detached from the brand

Rewrite them using more natural language.

19. Ethical Persuasion Review

Evaluate whether the campaign uses:

- False urgency
- Artificial scarcity
- Fear without context
- Guilt
- Shame
- Pressure
- Exaggerated outcomes
- Unsupported guarantees
- Hidden conditions
- Manipulative personalization
- Manufactured intimacy
- Repeated emotional escalation

Rewrite any problematic sections.

20. Call-to-Action Review

Evaluate each CTA for:

- Clarity
- Timing
- Effort required
- Relevance
- Tone
- Reader readiness
- Relationship to the email
- Proportionality

Create alternative CTAs for readers who are:

- Curious
- Comparing options
- Not ready to buy
- Ready to speak
- Ready to purchase
- Already customers

21. Factuality and Compliance Review

Create a table containing:

- Claim
- Source supplied
- Verification status
- Compliance concern
- Risk level
- Recommended revision or action

Do not invent legal or regulatory conclusions.

Flag areas that require professional review.

22. Mobile Readability Review

Review the emails for mobile reading.

Check:

- Paragraph length
- Line length
- Heading use
- Link visibility
- CTA placement
- Scannability
- Repetition
- Visual clutter
- Excessive bolding
- Long introductions

Suggest improvements.

23. Personalization Guidance

Recommend ethical personalization using available information.

Separate:

- Useful personalization
- Optional segmentation
- Unnecessary personalization
- Sensitive data that should not be used
- Personalization that may feel intrusive

Do not invent customer details.

24. Segmentation Options

Create campaign variations for relevant segments such as:

- New subscriber
- Existing customer
- Past customer
- Warm lead
- Cold lead
- Event attendee
- Community member
- Referral
- Engaged reader
- Inactive subscriber

Only include segments supported by the campaign context.

25. Testing Plan

Create a testing plan for:

- Subject lines
- Preview text
- Opening
- CTA wording
- CTA placement
- Email length
- Story versus education
- Send timing
- Number of emails
- Plain text versus designed format

For each test, include:

- Variable
- Hypothesis
- What should remain unchanged
- Success metric
- Minimum observation period
- Interpretation warning

Do not treat a small sample as definitive.

26. Campaign Measurement Plan

Recommend appropriate measures such as:

- Delivery rate
- Open rate
- Click rate
- Reply rate
- Conversion rate
- Unsubscribe rate
- Spam complaints
- Resource downloads
- Booked calls
- Purchases
- Customer activation
- Revenue
- Qualitative replies

Explain what each metric can and cannot reveal.

27. Follow-Up Reply Templates

Create replies for:

- Interested reader
- Reader with a question
- Reader with an objection
- Reader asking for pricing
- Reader asking for more information
- Reader who is not ready
- Reader who wants to unsubscribe
- Existing customer
- Potential referral partner
- Reader sharing a personal story

The replies should sound human and continue the conversation naturally.

28. Campaign Repurposing Plan

Create a plan for turning the campaign into:

- LinkedIn posts
- X posts
- Facebook posts
- Instagram captions
- Short videos
- A blog article
- A FAQ page
- A lead magnet
- A webinar outline
- A Skool community discussion
- A customer onboarding resource

29. Final Campaign Summary

Finish with:

- Campaign name
- Audience
- Goal
- Number of emails
- Recommended timing
- Primary message
- Main offer
- Primary CTA
- Most important objection
- Primary trust builder
- Biggest campaign risk
- One-sentence campaign strategy

30. Human Review Questions

Finish by asking:

- Does every email have a clear purpose?
- Does the sequence respect the reader’s journey?
- Does the campaign sound like the sender?
- Are all stories true?
- Are all claims supported?
- Is the offer represented accurately?
- Are deadlines and limits real?
- Is the campaign helpful before it becomes promotional?
- Does each CTA match the reader’s readiness?
- Is the language respectful?
- Has manipulative pressure been removed?
- Are legal and compliance concerns flagged?
- Has confidential information been excluded?
- Would I be comfortable receiving these emails?
- Would I defend every claim publicly?
```

## Recommended Follow-Up Questions

### Follow-Up 1: Test the Sequence

```text
Review the full email sequence. Identify emails that repeat the same purpose, move too quickly, ask for too much commitment, or fail to advance the reader journey. Recommend a stronger sequence.
```

### Follow-Up 2: Remove Generic Email Language

```text
Identify every phrase that sounds generic, automated, overly polished, manipulative, or copied from common marketing emails. Explain why it feels unnatural and rewrite it in the supplied brand voice.
```

### Follow-Up 3: Strengthen the Welcome

```text
Review the first email. Does it clearly confirm what the reader signed up for, deliver the promised value, establish expectations, and provide one simple next step? Rewrite it if necessary.
```

### Follow-Up 4: Audit the Calls to Action

```text
Review every call to action in the campaign. Identify where the CTA appears too early, feels too aggressive, lacks clarity, or does not match the reader’s stage. Create better alternatives.
```

### Follow-Up 5: Challenge the Claims

```text
Create a claim-by-claim audit of the campaign. Separate confirmed facts, supported benefits, opinions, assumptions, and statements requiring verification. Do not allow unsupported certainty.
```

### Follow-Up 6: Improve the Subject Lines

```text
Evaluate the subject lines for clarity, relevance, credibility, curiosity, and brand fit. Remove clickbait, vague bait, false urgency, and exaggerated promises. Create stronger alternatives.
```

### Follow-Up 7: Build a Plain-Text Version

```text
Rewrite the campaign as natural plain-text emails from one person to another. Preserve the strategy and brand voice while removing unnecessary formatting and promotional language.
```

### Follow-Up 8: Create the Automation Map

```text
Map the campaign as an automation workflow. Include the entry trigger, timing, decision points, segments, actions, exit conditions, human approval checkpoints, and follow-up paths.
```

## Human Review Checklist

Before activating the campaign, confirm:

- Is the audience clearly defined?
- Does every email have one primary purpose?
- Is the sequence ordered logically?
- Does the campaign sound recognizable?
- Are the subject lines honest?
- Are all stories true?
- Are all facts supported?
- Are benefits represented accurately?
- Are deadlines and limits real?
- Does the campaign provide value?
- Does the sender respect the reader’s choice?
- Is the CTA appropriate for the journey stage?
- Has generic AI language been removed?
- Has false urgency been removed?
- Has artificial intimacy been removed?
- Are compliance concerns addressed?
- Is unsubscribe access clear?
- Has confidential information been excluded?
- Does the campaign work on mobile?
- Would the sender be comfortable receiving it?
- Would the sender defend every message publicly?

## Important Principle

An email sequence should not feel like a hallway where every door leads to the same sales pitch.

Each email should move the relationship forward by helping the reader understand something, solve something, decide something, or take a reasonable next step.

Trust compounds.

Pressure corrodes.
