# Marketing Automation Workflow Prompt

Use this prompt to design responsible marketing automations that connect customer needs, business goals, content, communication, data, and human judgment.

This resource is part of the **Brand Voice AI** methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC**.

> Automation should remove unnecessary repetition without removing responsibility.

---

## Purpose

This prompt helps transform a marketing or customer-communication process into a structured automation workflow.

The goal is to define:

- Why the automation exists
- Who should enter it
- What triggers it
- What information it requires
- What messages it sends
- Which decisions it makes
- Where human approval is required
- What happens when something fails
- How customers can pause or leave
- How results are measured
- How the workflow is reviewed and improved

AI can help map, draft, test, and document the automation.

The business remains responsible for every action the system takes.

## Automation Principles

A responsible automation should:

- Serve a clear customer or business purpose
- Use only necessary information
- Respect consent and communication preferences
- Avoid false urgency and pressure
- Preserve brand voice
- Provide a clear human contact path
- Include failure and escalation procedures
- Stop when the customer exits or becomes ineligible
- Avoid repeating messages unnecessarily
- Protect confidential information
- Record important decisions
- Require human approval for sensitive actions
- Be reviewed regularly

## Master Prompt

Copy the prompt below into your preferred AI assistant.

Replace the bracketed sections with your information.

```text

Your job is to map the process, define entry and exit conditions, identify data requirements, design actions and decision branches, establish human approval checkpoints, document failure paths, and create a testing and measurement plan.

- Customer data
- Consent
- Legal requirements
- Technical capabilities
- Integrations
- Pricing
- Deadlines
- Customer behavior
- Business policies
- Performance results
- Access permissions
- Software features
- Claims
- Messages already approved by the business

Separate:

- Confirmed facts
- Current process
- Proposed improvements
- Assumptions
- Technical unknowns
- Compliance questions
- Data risks
- Decisions requiring human approval

Do not assume automation is appropriate simply because a task is repetitive.

Recommend human involvement when the situation includes:

- Sensitive information
- High-value decisions
- Complaints
- Refunds
- Pricing exceptions
- Legal concerns
- Medical or financial information
- Customer distress
- Public accusations
- Security issues
- Complex recommendations

[Enter the business, company, project, or brand.]

AUTOMATION NAME:

[Enter a clear working name.]

## Recommended Follow-Up Questions

### Follow-Up 1: Challenge the Automation

```text
Review the workflow and identify every step that may be automated merely because it is technically possible. Recommend where human judgment would better serve the customer.
```

### Follow-Up 2: Audit Consent and Data

```text
Create a field-by-field review of the data used by this automation. Identify its source, purpose, consent, sensitivity, retention, access, and whether it is truly necessary.
```

### Follow-Up 3: Test Every Branch

```text
Create a complete test plan covering entry, eligibility, exclusions, consent, personalization, timing, decision branches, CRM actions, human handoffs, failures, pauses, exits, and opt-outs.
```

### Follow-Up 4: Improve the Human Handoff

```text
Review every point where a person may need to take over. Identify the trigger, information transferred, assigned owner, response time, customer expectation, and failure escalation.
```

### Follow-Up 5: Reduce Customer Pressure

```text
Review the messages, timing, frequency, urgency, and calls to action. Remove repetition, pressure, artificial scarcity, manipulative personalization, and unnecessary escalation.
```

### Follow-Up 6: Create the Implementation Plan

```text
Turn this workflow into a phased implementation plan with owners, tools, dependencies, tests, approvals, pilot steps, launch criteria, and rollback procedures.
```

### Follow-Up 7: Create the Automation Diagram

```text
Create a clear text-based workflow diagram showing triggers, conditions, actions, branches, delays, approvals, handoffs, failures, pauses, and exits.
```
