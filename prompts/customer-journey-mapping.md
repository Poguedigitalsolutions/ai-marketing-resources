# Customer Journey Mapping Prompt

Use this prompt to map how customers move from first recognizing a problem through evaluation, purchase, onboarding, success, retention, and advocacy.

This resource is part of the **Brand Voice AI** methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC**.

> A customer journey is not a funnel people fall through. It is a sequence of questions, decisions, experiences, and trust.

---

## Purpose

This prompt helps organize customer research into a practical journey map.

The goal is to understand:

- What the customer is trying to accomplish
- What triggers their search for help
- What they think and feel at each stage
- What questions they need answered
- Which touchpoints influence their decision
- Where friction slows or stops progress
- What builds or weakens trust
- Which content helps at each stage
- Where human support is needed
- Where automation may be appropriate
- What success looks like after purchase
- What encourages retention, advocacy, and referral

AI can help organize evidence, identify gaps, and prepare the journey map.

The human remains responsible for validating the map with real customers.

## Journey Stages

The exact stages may vary by business.

A complete journey may include:

1. Unaware
2. Problem aware
3. Solution aware
4. Offer aware
5. Evaluation
6. Decision
7. Purchase
8. Onboarding
9. Early use
10. First success
11. Ongoing value
12. Renewal or repeat purchase
13. Advocacy
14. Referral
15. Re-engagement

Use only the stages that fit the supplied customer evidence and business model.

Do not force every customer into a perfectly linear path.

## Before You Begin

Gather evidence that reflects how customers actually behave.

Useful sources include:

- Customer interviews
- Discovery calls
- Sales calls
- Support conversations
- Surveys
- Reviews
- Testimonials
- CRM notes
- Website analytics
- Search data
- Email replies
- Social media comments
- Community discussions
- Lost-sale feedback
- Onboarding questions
- Customer success notes
- Cancellation feedback
- Renewal conversations
- Referral conversations
- Existing process documentation

Do not include private customer information, passwords, protected health information, confidential records, financial details, or anything you are not authorized to share.

## Master Prompt

Copy the prompt below into your preferred AI assistant.

Replace the bracketed sections with your information.

```text
You are helping me create an evidence-based customer journey map.

Your job is to organize the supplied customer research, identify the customer’s stages, questions, emotions, actions, touchpoints, friction, and trust needs, and recommend appropriate content and support.

Do not invent customer behavior, motivations, emotions, objections, demographics, purchase steps, results, or touchpoints.

Use only the information supplied.

Separate direct evidence, reasonable interpretation, assumptions, and unknowns.

When evidence is missing, identify the gap.

Do not force the journey into a perfectly linear funnel when the evidence suggests customers move backward, pause, compare, leave, return, or enter at different stages.

BUSINESS OR BRAND:

[Enter the business, company, project, or brand.]

PRODUCT OR SERVICE:

[Describe the offer, how it is delivered, and what the customer receives.]

TARGET CUSTOMER:

[Describe the customer group.]

CUSTOMER GOAL:

[Describe what the customer is trying to accomplish.]

CUSTOMER PROBLEM:

[Describe the problem, frustration, delay, risk, or unmet need.]

CUSTOMER TRIGGER:

[Describe the event, realization, deadline, failure, opportunity, or pressure that may begin the journey.]

CURRENT ALTERNATIVES:

[Describe what the customer currently does instead.]

CUSTOMER LANGUAGE:

[Paste real questions, phrases, complaints, objections, reviews, comments, emails, or interview statements.]

CUSTOMER EVIDENCE:

[Paste interview notes, survey responses, sales notes, support conversations, analytics findings, reviews, testimonials, or other evidence.]

KNOWN JOURNEY STAGES:

[List any stages already identified.]

KNOWN TOUCHPOINTS:

[List the website, social platforms, email, calls, events, referrals, communities, sales conversations, onboarding steps, support channels, or other interactions.]

KNOWN QUESTIONS:

[List questions customers ask before, during, and after purchase.]

KNOWN OBJECTIONS:

[List concerns, doubts, risks, delays, comparisons, or reasons customers do not move forward.]

KNOWN FRICTION:

[List confusing steps, delays, unclear information, technical problems, handoff problems, approval requirements, or customer complaints.]

TRUST BUILDERS:

[List known evidence, experiences, people, credentials, demonstrations, or support that increase trust.]

TRUST BREAKERS:

[List behaviors, messages, experiences, delays, or inconsistencies that weaken trust.]

SALES PROCESS:

[Describe the current lead, qualification, sales, proposal, and purchase process.]

ONBOARDING PROCESS:

[Describe what happens immediately after purchase.]

DELIVERY PROCESS:

[Describe how the customer receives the product, service, support, or experience.]

SUCCESS OUTCOME:

[Describe what successful use looks like.]

RETENTION OR RENEWAL PROCESS:

[Describe how customers continue, renew, upgrade, or buy again.]

REFERRAL OR ADVOCACY PROCESS:

[Describe how customers share, recommend, review, or refer.]

AVAILABLE CONTENT:

[List existing articles, emails, videos, lead magnets, case studies, FAQs, guides, webinars, or customer resources.]

AVAILABLE TECHNOLOGY:

[List CRM, email platform, automation tools, scheduling tools, customer portal, analytics, support systems, or other technology.]

DESIRED OUTPUT:

[Journey map, content plan, automation plan, customer experience improvements, sales process, onboarding system, or another deliverable.]
```

## Recommended Follow-Up Questions

### Follow-Up 1: Challenge the Journey

```text
Review the journey map and identify every stage, action, emotion, objection, or assumption that is not supported by direct customer evidence. Explain what research is needed.
```

### Follow-Up 2: Find the Friction

```text
Identify the ten highest-impact friction points in the journey. Rank them by customer impact, business impact, evidence strength, effort to fix, and urgency.
```

### Follow-Up 3: Build the Content Map

```text
Create a complete content plan for every journey stage. Include the customer question, content format, core message, CTA, channel, evidence required, and measurement.
```

### Follow-Up 4: Build the Automation Map

```text
Map the customer journey as an automation workflow. Include triggers, actions, timing, conditions, data requirements, human approval checkpoints, failure paths, and exit conditions.
```

### Follow-Up 5: Improve Onboarding

```text
Review the onboarding journey and identify where customers may feel confused, delayed, unsupported, or uncertain. Create a clearer path to first success.
```

### Follow-Up 6: Create the Interview Plan

```text
Create a customer interview process to validate this journey. Ask one question at a time and use each answer to select the next question.
```

### Follow-Up 7: Strengthen Retention

```text
Review the journey after first success. Identify missing education, check-ins, support, reporting, community, renewal, and advocacy opportunities.
```

## Important Principle

Customers do not experience departments, funnels, campaigns, and software systems.

They experience one continuous relationship.

Every handoff, message, delay, promise, and result becomes part of that relationship.
