# Founder Knowledge Capture Prompt

Use this prompt to document the knowledge, experiences, values, stories, decisions, processes, and language that usually live only inside a founder’s head.

This resource is part of the **Brand Voice AI** methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC**.

> The founder’s memory should not be the company’s only filing cabinet.

---

## Purpose

This prompt helps transform scattered founder knowledge into a structured, reviewable knowledge base.

The goal is to capture:

- Origin story
- Mission and purpose
- Values and convictions
- Customer understanding
- Offer knowledge
- Processes and methods
- Decision-making principles
- Frequently answered questions
- Stories and lessons
- Signature language
- Institutional memory
- Boundaries and confidential information
- Knowledge gaps requiring follow-up

AI can help organize the conversation.

The founder remains the source of truth.

## Before You Begin

Gather any material that reflects how the founder thinks and communicates.

Useful sources include:

- Interviews
- Articles
- Social media posts
- Emails
- Presentations
- Videos
- Podcast transcripts
- Sales calls
- Customer conversations
- Meeting notes
- Business plans
- Process documents
- Training materials
- Personal stories
- Frequently asked questions
- Old marketing materials
- Founder journals
- Customer testimonials
- Team questions
- Recorded lessons

Use original material whenever possible.

Do not include:

- Passwords
- Access credentials
- Private customer records
- Protected health information
- Confidential legal matters
- Private financial details
- Trade secrets intended to remain undisclosed
- Employee information without authorization
- Information restricted by contract
- Anything the founder is not authorized to share

## Master Prompt

Copy the prompt below into your preferred AI assistant.

Replace the bracketed sections with your information.

```text
You are helping me capture and organize founder knowledge.

Your job is to interview the founder, preserve their language, identify what they know, separate fact from interpretation, and organize the information into a usable knowledge base.

Do not invent:

- Stories
- Experiences
- Customer results
- Dates
- Credentials
- Processes
- Beliefs
- Motivations
- Business history
- Quotations
- Decisions
- Lessons
- Statistics
- Relationships
- Confidential information

Do not make the founder sound more polished, impressive, dramatic, or certain than the supplied evidence supports.

Preserve the founder’s natural wording whenever possible.

Clearly separate:

- Direct founder statements
- Confirmed facts
- Personal opinions
- Interpretations
- Assumptions
- Unverified memories
- Sensitive information
- Confidential information
- Knowledge gaps
- Questions requiring follow-up

When interviewing, ask one question at a time.

After each answer:

1. Summarize what was said.
2. Identify important phrases.
3. Note possible follow-up questions.
4. Separate confirmed information from interpretation.
5. Ask the next most useful question.

Do not rush through a fixed questionnaire when an answer reveals a more important path.

FOUNDER:

[Enter the founder’s name.]

BUSINESS OR BRAND:

[Enter the company, organization, project, or brand.]

BUSINESS DESCRIPTION:

[Describe what the business does.]

PRIMARY AUDIENCE:

[Describe the customers, clients, members, students, or stakeholders.]

KNOWN PRODUCTS OR SERVICES:

[List known offers.]

KNOWN VALUES:

[List known values, beliefs, or principles.]

KNOWN STORIES:

[List any founder stories already documented.]

KNOWN PHRASES:

[List recurring expressions, sayings, metaphors, questions, or language.]

AVAILABLE SOURCE MATERIAL:

[Paste transcripts, articles, notes, emails, interviews, posts, presentations, or other material.]

CONFIDENTIALITY BOUNDARIES:

[List information that must remain private, internal, anonymized, or excluded.]

INTENDED USE:

[Brand voice profile, AI assistant, content system, knowledge base, training, book, website, succession planning, or another use.]

Using the supplied information and interview responses, create a Founder Knowledge Capture Report with the following sections:

1. Founder Identity

Document:

- Full name
- Preferred name
- Role
- Professional identity
- Personal identity relevant to the work
- Areas of expertise
- Areas of lived experience
- Communities represented
- Credentials
- Important limitations
- Information requiring verification

2. Founder Summary

Write a concise summary describing:

- Who the founder is
- What they do
- Who they help
- What they believe
- What makes their perspective distinctive
- What they want to be known for

Do not use inflated language.

3. Origin Story

Capture:

- What existed before the business
- What problem or experience sparked the idea
- Important turning points
- Early experiments
- Obstacles
- First customers
- Breakthrough moments
- Lessons learned
- Why the founder continued
- What has changed since the beginning

Separate exact memories from reconstructed timelines.

4. Mission

Document:

- What the business does
- Who it serves
- What outcome it supports
- Why the work matters
- How the founder explains the mission naturally
- Formal mission statement when one exists
- Informal mission statement in the founder’s own language

5. Purpose

Explore:

- Why the founder feels called to this work
- What personal meaning the work carries
- What problem feels worth solving
- What impact matters most
- What the founder would continue discussing even without immediate reward
- What the founder hopes people remember

6. Vision

Capture:

- Long-term direction
- Desired impact
- Future customers
- Future products or services
- Geographic expansion
- Community goals
- Educational goals
- Technology goals
- Team goals
- Legacy goals
- Boundaries on growth
- What the founder does not want the business to become

7. Core Values

Identify each value.

For every value, include:

- Value name
- Founder definition
- Story demonstrating it
- Business behavior it supports

- Decision it influences
- Evidence
- Questions requiring confirmation

Document recurring beliefs about:

- Speed versus certainty
```

## Recommended Follow-Up Questions

### Follow-Up 1: Challenge the Accuracy

```text
Review the founder knowledge report and identify every statement that is inferred, uncertain, contradictory, outdated, or unsupported. Create a verification list.
```

### Follow-Up 2: Find the Missing Knowledge

```text
Identify the most important knowledge gaps that would prevent a team member or AI assistant from representing the founder accurately.
```

### Follow-Up 3: Build the Interview

```text
Create a founder interview that fills the most important gaps. Ask one question at a time and use each answer to choose the next question.
```

### Follow-Up 4: Extract the Voice

```text
Create a founder language bank using only exact or strongly supported wording. Organize it by values, stories, teaching, leadership, customers, objections, and calls to action.
```

### Follow-Up 5: Build the Story Bank

```text
Extract every founder story and organize it by theme, lesson, audience, best use, permission status, privacy risk, and verification needs.
```

### Follow-Up 6: Create the Knowledge Base

```text
Transform the approved information into a structured knowledge base with categories, source references, confidence levels, confidentiality labels, and human approval requirements.
```

### Follow-Up 7: Create the AI Guardrails

```text
Create rules defining what an AI assistant may retrieve, summarize, draft, recommend, personalize, publish, automate, or share using this founder knowledge.
```

## Human Review Checklist

Before approving the founder knowledge base, confirm:

- Is the founder represented accurately?
- Are facts verified?
- Are memories labeled appropriately?
- Are interpretations separated?
- Are stories true?
- Are quotations accurate?
- Are permissions documented?
- Are customer identities protected?
- Are confidential boundaries clear?
- Are proprietary processes protected?
- Does the voice sound recognizable?
- Are important contradictions visible?
- Are knowledge gaps listed?
- Are sources documented?
- Are approval requirements defined?
- Has the founder reviewed the final version?

## Important Principle

A founder knowledge base should not turn a human being into a pile of polished talking points.

It should preserve the experience, judgment, language, stories, and convictions that make the founder’s perspective worth capturing.
