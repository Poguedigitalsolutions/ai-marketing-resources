# Social Media Content Prompt

Use this prompt to transform one meaningful idea, article, story, offer, or customer insight into platform-specific social content while preserving the original brand voice.

This resource is part of the **Brand Voice AI** methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC**.

> One idea should not become ten copies. It should become ten useful conversations.

---

## Purpose

This prompt helps create social media content that is adapted rather than merely duplicated.

The goal is to preserve:

- The original idea
- The brand voice
- The intended audience
- The human perspective
- The platform context
- The purpose of the message

AI can help reshape the material.

The human remains responsible for the meaning.

## Before You Begin

Gather the source material you want to transform.

Useful inputs include:

- An article
- A newsletter
- A personal story
- A customer question
- A lesson learned
- A framework
- A business observation
- A product or service
- An event
- A case study
- A video transcript
- Research notes
- A quotation
- A conversation
- A call to action

Do not ask AI to invent personal stories, customer results, quotations, statistics, or experiences.

---

## Master Prompt

Copy the prompt below into your preferred AI assistant.

Replace the bracketed sections with your information.

```text
You are helping me create platform-specific social media content in my authentic brand voice.

Your job is to preserve the central idea while adapting the format, length, opening, pacing, emotional tone, and call to action for each platform.

Do not produce identical posts with only minor wording changes.

Do not invent facts, stories, customer results, quotations, statistics, credentials, or experiences.

Separate supplied facts from interpretation.

AUTHOR OR BRAND:

[Enter the person, company, or brand.]

BRAND VOICE:

[Paste the brand voice summary, voice rules, signature language, voice dos, voice don’ts, or examples.]

SOURCE MATERIAL:

[Paste the article, story, transcript, idea, notes, framework, offer, or original content.]

CORE IDEA:

[State the main point in one or two sentences.]

TARGET AUDIENCE:

[Describe the people the content should reach.]

AUDIENCE PROBLEM OR INTEREST:

[Describe the problem, question, desire, concern, or opportunity that makes the idea relevant.]

CONTENT GOAL:

[Choose awareness, education, engagement, authority, community growth, lead generation, event promotion, offer promotion, or another goal.]

DESIRED AUDIENCE RESPONSE:

[What should the audience think, feel, say, or do?]

PLATFORMS:

[List the platforms you want content created for.]

PERSONAL STORY OR EXPERIENCE:

[Include any true story or experience that should be used.]

CUSTOMER LANGUAGE:

[Paste real questions, phrases, objections, comments, or expressions from the audience.]

KEY POINTS TO INCLUDE:

[List the most important ideas.]

WORDS, CLAIMS, OR STYLES TO AVOID:

[List anything that feels exaggerated, generic, manipulative, overly promotional, corporate, false, or inconsistent with the brand.]

CALL TO ACTION:

[Describe the desired next step.]

LINK OR DESTINATION:

[Add the article, resource, community, event, product, or website link if applicable.]

Using only the supplied information, create a Social Media Content Report with the following sections:

1. Source Summary

State:

- The central idea
- The intended audience
- The primary value
- The strongest emotional or practical tension
- The desired response
- The most important supporting point

2. Content Angle Options

Create five possible angles.

For each angle, include:

- Angle name
- Main hook
- Intended emotional effect
- Best platform
- Why it may work
- Risk or weakness

Recommend the strongest angle.

3. Hook Library

Create fifteen hooks across these categories:

- Direct statement
- Question
- Contrarian observation
- Personal story
- Problem recognition
- Surprising lesson
- Practical promise
- Customer language
- Curiosity
- Short declaration

Avoid clickbait and unsupported promises.

4. Platform Strategy

For each requested platform, explain:

- Reader or viewer behavior
- Recommended length
- Opening style
- Formatting
- Tone
- CTA style
- Link placement
- Best content format
- Common mistake to avoid

5. LinkedIn Post

Create:

- One thought-leadership post
- One personal-story post
- One educational post
- One conversation-starting post

Use readable paragraph spacing.

Avoid excessive hashtags, manufactured vulnerability, and generic professional language.

6. X Content

Create:

- Five standalone posts
- One five-post thread
- One question post
- One concise quote-style post

Each post should remain clear without depending on unexplained context.

7. Facebook Post

Create:

- One conversational post
- One story-driven post
- One community-engagement post

The writing should feel natural and personal rather than copied from LinkedIn.

8. Instagram Caption

Create:

- One short caption
- One medium caption
- One story-based caption
- One carousel caption

Include a natural CTA.

Do not overload the caption with hashtags.

9. TikTok or Short-Video Script

Create a 30-to-60-second script containing:

- Opening hook
- Spoken body
- Visual or movement suggestion
- On-screen text
- Closing line
- CTA

The script should sound natural when spoken aloud.

10. YouTube Community Post

Create:

- One discussion question
- One educational post
- One article or video promotion post

11. Skool Community Post

Create:

- One teaching post
- One discussion prompt
- One challenge or action post
- One resource-sharing post

Encourage meaningful participation rather than shallow reactions.

12. Content Variations

Create versions using these approaches:

- Story-first
- Problem-first
- Lesson-first
- Question-first
- Framework-first
- Contrarian
- Practical
- Reflective

13. Brand Voice Review

Evaluate all content for:

- Voice consistency
- Natural language
- Sentence rhythm
- Signature themes
- Emotional authenticity
- Excessive polish
- Generic AI phrasing
- Overpromotion
- Platform fit
- Human judgment

Identify any posts that do not sound recognizable.

14. Generic Language Audit

Highlight phrases that sound:

- Corporate
- Predictable
- Artificial
- Inflated
- Promotional
- Overly polished
- Emotionally empty
- Detached from the original idea

Replace them with more natural alternatives.

15. Platform Adaptation Audit

Explain whether each version was truly adapted for the platform.

Identify:

- Posts that feel copied
- Hooks that do not fit
- CTAs that feel unnatural
- Length problems
- Formatting problems
- Tone mismatches

16. Engagement Questions

Create ten questions that could generate meaningful responses.

The questions should:

- Be easy to understand
- Invite experience or opinion
- Relate directly to the content
- Avoid manufactured controversy
- Avoid yes-or-no answers when possible

17. Comment Strategy

Create:

- Three replies to supportive comments
- Three replies to thoughtful disagreement
- Three replies to questions
- Three follow-up questions
- One response to a potential customer
- One response to someone asking for the resource

The replies should continue the conversation without sounding automated.

18. CTA Options

Create five calls to action:

- Comment CTA
- Conversation CTA
- Resource CTA
- Community CTA
- Service CTA

Each CTA should match the content goal and remain proportionate.

19. Hashtag Guidance

Recommend:

- Whether hashtags are useful for each platform
- How many to use
- What kinds to select
- Which broad or generic hashtags to avoid

Do not invent trending hashtags.

20. Publishing Sequence

Create a seven-day publishing sequence using the source material.

For each day, include:

- Platform
- Content angle
- Format
- Goal
- CTA
- Relationship to the previous post

The sequence should feel connected without repeating the same post.

21. Human Review Questions

Finish by asking:

- Does this sound like me?
- Is the core idea still intact?
- Has anything been exaggerated?
- Are the stories true?
- Are the claims supported?
- Is each post appropriate for its platform?
- Does the CTA feel natural?
- Would I respond to this content?
- Is the post useful without requiring a sale?
- Am I comfortable defending the message publicly?
```

## Recommended Follow-Up Questions

### Follow-Up 1: Remove Repetition

```text
Compare all versions and identify where the wording, structure, hooks, or calls to action feel repetitive. Rewrite those sections so each platform version has a distinct purpose and rhythm.
```

### Follow-Up 2: Strengthen Platform Fit

```text
Review each post for platform fit. Explain which content feels naturally written for the platform and which content still feels copied from somewhere else. Rewrite the weak versions.
```

### Follow-Up 3: Restore the Human Voice

```text
Identify language that sounds generic, automated, overly polished, or emotionally empty. Rewrite it using the supplied brand voice, personal perspective, and natural sentence rhythm.
```

### Follow-Up 4: Improve Engagement

```text
Strengthen the posts so they encourage meaningful conversation rather than shallow reactions. Create better questions, stronger discussion prompts, and more natural replies.
```

### Follow-Up 5: Create a Repurposing Calendar

```text
Turn this source material into a 30-day content calendar. Vary the platforms, formats, angles, and calls to action while preserving the original idea and brand voice.
```

---

## Human Review Checklist

Before publishing, confirm:

- Does each post preserve the central idea?
- Does the content sound recognizable?
- Is every personal story true?
- Are customer examples accurate?
- Are factual claims supported?
- Is each version genuinely adapted?
- Does the hook match the content?
- Is the CTA proportionate?
- Has generic AI language been removed?
- Does the content provide value?
- Is the tone appropriate?
- Does the content respect the audience?
- Has confidential information been excluded?
- Would the author post this without embarrassment?
- Does the content invite a real conversation?

---

## Important Principle

Repurposing should not produce a row of content clones.

Each platform has its own room temperature, pace, and social rhythm.

The idea remains the same.

The expression should adapt.
