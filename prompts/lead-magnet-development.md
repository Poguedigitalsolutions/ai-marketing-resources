# Lead Magnet Development Prompt

Use this prompt to create a useful, evidence-aware lead magnet that solves a specific audience problem, demonstrates how the brand thinks, and naturally leads readers toward an appropriate next step.

This resource is part of the **Brand Voice AI** methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC**.

> A strong lead magnet should deliver a real win, not merely decorate an email form.

---

## Purpose

This prompt helps transform customer research, expertise, articles, frameworks, stories, or processes into a practical resource.

The goal is to create something the audience can use immediately.

A strong lead magnet should:

- Address one recognizable problem
- Promise a specific and credible outcome
- Match the audience’s level of awareness
- Be easy to understand
- Provide practical value
- Preserve the brand voice
- Demonstrate expertise without overwhelming the reader
- Lead naturally to the next step
- Respect the reader’s time and trust

AI can help organize, draft, test, and improve the resource.

The creator remains responsible for the claims, examples, recommendations, and final approval.

## Before You Begin

Gather the information that should guide the lead magnet.

Useful inputs include:

- Customer avatar research
- Brand voice guidance
- Customer questions
- Sales objections
- Interview notes
- Reviews
- Testimonials
- Frequently asked questions
- Articles
- Frameworks
- Processes
- Checklists
- Templates
- Examples
- Case studies
- Lessons learned
- Research sources
- Service information
- Community information
- A desired call to action

Do not ask AI to invent customer results, testimonials, statistics, quotations, credentials, research findings, case studies, or personal experiences.

Do not include confidential customer data, private business records, passwords, protected health information, financial information, or anything you are not authorized to share.

## Master Prompt

Copy the prompt below into your preferred AI assistant.

Replace the bracketed sections with your information.

```text
You are helping me develop a useful, credible, and brand-aligned lead magnet.

Your job is to identify the strongest concept, match it to a specific audience problem, recommend the most useful format, develop the content, create the supporting promotion, and test whether the resource delivers real value.

Do not invent facts, statistics, quotations, testimonials, customer results, credentials, research findings, case studies, deadlines, scarcity, or personal experiences.

Do not create an inflated promise simply to increase sign-ups.

Separate confirmed information, interpretation, assumptions, and unknowns.

When the supplied information is insufficient, identify what is missing.

CREATOR OR BRAND:

[Enter the person, company, or brand.]

BRAND VOICE:

[Paste the brand voice summary, examples, signature language, voice rules, voice dos, and voice don’ts.]

TARGET AUDIENCE:

[Describe the specific people the lead magnet should help.]

AUDIENCE AWARENESS LEVEL:

[Unaware, problem aware, solution aware, offer aware, or another relevant stage.]

AUDIENCE SITUATION:

[Describe what is happening in the audience’s work, business, life, or decision process.]

PRIMARY PROBLEM:

[Describe the specific problem the lead magnet should address.]

DESIRED QUICK WIN:

[What useful result should the reader achieve shortly after using the resource?]

LONGER-TERM GOAL:

[What larger outcome does this quick win support?]

CUSTOMER LANGUAGE:

[Paste real questions, frustrations, comments, objections, search terms, interview statements, or phrases used by the audience.]

SOURCE MATERIAL:

[Paste articles, frameworks, processes, notes, research, examples, transcripts, checklists, or other content that may support the lead magnet.]

KNOWN FACTS:

[List information that may be treated as confirmed.]

CLAIMS REQUIRING VERIFICATION:

[List statistics, dates, claims, quotations, or recommendations that must be checked.]

EXPERTISE OR EXPERIENCE:

[Describe relevant credentials, experience, stories, lessons, or methods that may be used.]

OFFER OR NEXT STEP:

[Describe the service, product, consultation, community, course, article, or resource that the lead magnet may naturally introduce.]

PRIMARY CALL TO ACTION:

[Describe the desired next step after the reader uses the resource.]

SECONDARY CALL TO ACTION:

[Describe a lower-commitment alternative.]

DELIVERY METHOD:

[PDF, webpage, email course, spreadsheet, template, video companion, interactive tool, or another format.]

DESIRED LENGTH:

[Enter the intended page count, word count, number of questions, number of steps, or estimated completion time.]

DESIGN OR ACCESSIBILITY REQUIREMENTS:

[List font size, reading level, mobile use, printable format, visual needs, accessibility needs, or brand requirements.]

WORDS, CLAIMS, OR STYLES TO AVOID:

[List language that feels generic, exaggerated, manipulative, overly promotional, false, corporate, technical, or inconsistent with the brand.]

Using only the supplied information, create a Lead Magnet Development Report with the following sections:

1. Evidence and Information Review

Separate the supplied material into:

- Confirmed facts
- Supported expertise
- Personal opinion
- Interpretation
- Assumptions requiring confirmation
- Claims requiring verification
- Missing information
- Confidential or unsuitable material
- Information that should not be used publicly

Do not begin drafting until these distinctions are clear.

2. Audience-Problem Alignment

State:

- Primary audience
- Awareness level
- Current situation
- Main problem
- Immediate frustration
- Desired quick win
- Longer-term goal
- Likely objection
- Reason the audience would exchange contact information for this resource

Evaluate whether the problem is specific enough.

3. Lead Magnet Concept Options

Create ten possible lead magnet concepts.

For each concept, include:

- Working title
- Format
- Audience problem addressed
- Quick win promised
- Estimated completion time
- Why the concept may be valuable
- Relationship to the next offer
- Risk or weakness

Recommend the strongest concept and explain why.

4. Format Recommendation

Compare the most appropriate formats, such as:

- Checklist
- Guide
- Worksheet
- Assessment
- Scorecard
- Template
- Prompt collection
- Workbook
- Email course
- Resource list
- Roadmap
- Decision tool

For each format, explain:

- Why it fits
- How much effort the reader must invest
- How quickly the reader may experience value
- How much content is required
- Design complexity
- Best delivery method
- Potential weakness

Recommend one primary format.

5. Value Proposition

Create:

- One-sentence value proposition
- One short elevator description
- One landing-page description
- One social-media description
- One email description

The promise should be useful, specific, credible, and proportionate.

6. Title Options

Create twenty title options across these styles:

- Direct
- Benefit-focused
- Problem-focused
- Question-based
- Checklist
- Quick-start
- Framework
- Assessment
- Scorecard
- Roadmap
- Curiosity-based
- Professional
- Search-friendly

Avoid exaggerated outcomes, vague promises, and clickbait.

7. Subtitle Options

Create ten subtitles that clarify:

- Who the resource is for
- What problem it addresses
- What the reader will receive
- How quickly it can be used
- What outcome it supports

8. Content Scope

Define what the lead magnet should include and exclude.

Include:

- Primary topic
- Supporting topics
- Required explanations
- Required examples
- Required actions
- Boundaries
- Information better saved for a paid offer
- Information that would overwhelm the reader
- Information requiring professional review

9. Reader Journey

Map the reader’s experience from discovery to completion.

Include:

- What attracts attention
- Why they sign up
- What they expect
- What they see first
- What they learn
- What they complete
- Where they may become confused
- Where they experience the quick win
- What they should do next

10. Lead Magnet Outline

Create a complete outline.

Include:

- Cover
- Title page
- Welcome
- Purpose
- Who the resource is for
- How to use it
- Core sections
- Exercises
- Examples
- Checklists
- Reflection questions
- Action steps
- Summary
- Next-step invitation
- About the creator
- Contact or resource links
- Disclaimer when appropriate

11. Opening Section

Write the opening section.

It should:

- Recognize the reader’s situation
- Explain the problem clearly
- Establish what the resource will help them do
- Set realistic expectations
- Explain how to use the resource
- Avoid beginning with a long biography or sales pitch

12. Core Content Draft

Write the complete lead magnet.

The content should:

- Preserve the supplied brand voice
- Use clear headings
- Use readable paragraphs
- Provide practical actions
- Explain unfamiliar terms
- Avoid unnecessary jargon
- Include examples only when supported
- Distinguish fact from opinion
- Avoid unsupported certainty
- Avoid repetitive advice
- Avoid padding the resource to appear more valuable
- Help the reader reach the promised quick win

13. Checklist Version

When appropriate, create a concise checklist containing:

- Preparation steps
- Required information
- Action steps
- Review steps
- Common mistakes
- Completion criteria
- Next action

Each checklist item should be specific enough to use.

14. Worksheet Version

When appropriate, create a worksheet containing:

- Instructions
- Questions
- Fill-in fields
- Reflection prompts
- Decision points
- Action planning
- Priority ranking
- Completion summary

Avoid asking vague questions that produce unusable answers.

15. Assessment or Scorecard Version

When appropriate, create an assessment containing:

- Clear purpose
- Instructions
- Scored questions
- Response scale
- Category definitions
- Scoring method
- Result ranges
- Interpretation guidance
- Recommended next steps
- Limitations of the assessment

Do not imply scientific validity unless evidence supports it.

16. Template Version

When appropriate, create a reusable template containing:

- Placeholder instructions
- Example entries
- Required fields
- Optional fields
- Completion guidance
- Review criteria
- Common errors
- Final-use instructions

17. Prompt Collection Version

When appropriate, create a prompt collection containing:

- Purpose of each prompt
- Required inputs
- Copyable prompt
- Customization guidance
- Expected output
- Follow-up prompt
- Human review questions
- Privacy warning

18. Quick-Win Test

Evaluate whether the lead magnet gives the reader a useful result.

Answer:

- What can the reader complete?
- What decision can the reader make?
- What confusion can the reader reduce?
- What mistake can the reader avoid?
- What result can the reader see?
- How long should this take?
- What may prevent completion?
- Is the promise fulfilled?

Recommend improvements if the quick win is weak.

19. Practical Value Audit

Identify every section that is:

- Actionable
- Educational
- Repetitive
- Too abstract
- Too advanced
- Too promotional
- Unnecessary
- Missing an example
- Missing an instruction
- Missing a completion criterion

Revise weak sections.

20. Brand Voice Review

Review the resource for:

- Tone
- Vocabulary
- Rhythm
- Warmth
- Authority
- Storytelling
- Signature language
- Voice dos
- Voice don’ts
- Generic AI phrasing
- Excessive polish
- Overpromotion
- Unnatural transitions

Identify passages that do not sound recognizable.

21. Generic Language Audit

Highlight wording that sounds:

- Generic
- Corporate
- Artificial
- Predictable
- Inflated
- Overly polished
- Promotional
- Emotionally empty
- Detached from the audience
- Detached from the creator

Rewrite those passages in the supplied brand voice.

22. Accuracy and Factuality Review

Create a table containing:

- Claim
- Source supplied
- Verification status
- Risk level
- Recommended action
- Whether the claim should remain, be revised, or be removed

Do not create sources that were not supplied.

23. Accessibility Review

Review the lead magnet for:

- Reading level
- Font size
- Heading structure
- Contrast
- Paragraph length
- Mobile readability
- Printability
- Link clarity
- Image descriptions
- Instructions
- Cognitive load
- Ability to use the resource without specialized knowledge

Recommend improvements.

24. Design Brief

Create a practical design brief containing:

- Visual objective
- Audience
- Tone
- Brand colors
- Typography guidance
- Cover concept
- Page layout
- Heading hierarchy
- Callout style
- Worksheet style
- Icon or illustration guidance
- Image needs
- Accessibility requirements
- Export formats
- Mobile and print considerations

Do not invent brand colors or visual assets that were not supplied.

25. Cover Copy

Create:

- Main title
- Subtitle
- Creator name
- Company or brand name
- Short benefit statement
- Optional edition or version
- Optional callout
- Footer text

26. Landing Page Copy

Create a landing page containing:

- Headline
- Subheadline
- Problem recognition
- Resource description
- What the reader receives
- Benefits
- Who it is for
- Who it may not be for
- Creator credibility
- Form heading
- Call-to-action button text
- Privacy reassurance
- Expectation setting
- Frequently asked questions
- Final CTA

Avoid exaggerated claims and artificial urgency.

27. Sign-Up Form Copy

Create:

- Form heading
- One-sentence description
- Name-field label
- Email-field label
- CTA button options
- Privacy statement
- Consent language
- Confirmation message
- Error message
- Existing-subscriber guidance

Flag any consent or compliance language that requires professional review.

28. Delivery Email

Write the lead magnet delivery email.

Include:

- Honest subject line
- Preview text
- Welcome
- Download or access link
- Instructions
- Expected completion time
- One practical suggestion
- Support contact
- Simple next step
- Sender name

The email should deliver the resource before introducing a sales message.

29. Follow-Up Email Sequence

Create a five-email follow-up sequence:

Email 1: Delivery and expectation setting

Email 2: Help the reader use the resource

Email 3: Address a common obstacle

Email 4: Share a relevant example or lesson

Email 5: Invite the reader to the next appropriate step

For each email, include:

- Purpose
- Subject line
- Preview text
- Main message
- CTA
- Timing

30. Thank-You Page

Create thank-you page copy containing:

- Confirmation
- Access instructions
- Inbox reminder
- Expected next email
- One immediate action
- Optional secondary resource
- Community invitation
- Contact guidance

Do not overwhelm the page with multiple competing offers.

31. Call-to-Action Options

Create calls to action for:

- Download
- Complete the worksheet
- Reply by email
- Join a community
- Read an article
- Watch a video
- Book a consultation
- Explore a service
- Share the resource
- Continue to another tool

Each CTA should match the reader’s readiness.

32. Promotion Plan

Create a promotion plan for:

- LinkedIn
- X
- Facebook
- Instagram
- YouTube
- TikTok
- Email
- Writing.io
- Substack
- Skool
- Website
- Direct outreach

For each channel, include:

- Content angle
- Hook
- Format
- CTA
- Link placement
- Frequency
- Common mistake to avoid

33. Social Promotion Posts

Create:

- Three LinkedIn posts
- Five X posts
- Two Facebook posts
- Three Instagram captions
- One short-video script
- One YouTube Community post
- One Skool post
- One article teaser

The posts should not all use the same hook or wording.

34. Content Upgrade Version

Explain how the lead magnet can serve as a content upgrade for:

- An article
- A newsletter
- A video
- A webinar
- A social post
- A podcast
- A workshop

Identify where the resource should be introduced and why.

35. Lead Qualification Guidance

Explain what the lead magnet may reveal about the reader.

Separate:

- Interest signals
- Readiness signals
- Problem severity
- Fit indicators
- Non-fit indicators
- Follow-up opportunities
- Information that should not be inferred

Do not treat a download as proof of purchase intent.

36. Measurement Plan

Recommend appropriate metrics, including:

- Landing-page visits
- Conversion rate
- Delivery rate
- Download rate
- Open rate
- Click rate
- Completion rate
- Reply rate
- Community joins
- Consultations booked
- Sales influenced
- Unsubscribe rate
- Spam complaints
- Qualitative feedback

Explain what each metric can and cannot reveal.

37. Improvement Questions

Create questions for gathering feedback:

- Was the resource easy to understand?
- Did it solve the promised problem?
- What section was most useful?
- What remained unclear?
- What was missing?
- Did the reader complete it?
- What action did they take?
- What resource would help next?

38. Repurposing Plan

Create a plan for turning the lead magnet into:

- An article series
- A LinkedIn carousel
- A workshop
- A webinar
- A short video series
- A podcast outline
- An email course
- A Skool classroom module
- A client onboarding tool
- A consultation framework
- A paid expanded resource

39. Final Lead Magnet Summary

Finish with:

- Lead magnet title
- Subtitle
- Target audience
- Main problem
- Quick win
- Format
- Estimated completion time
- Delivery method
- Primary CTA
- Follow-up sequence
- Relationship to the offer
- Primary trust builder
- Biggest risk
- One-sentence positioning statement

40. Human Review Questions

Finish by asking:

- Is the audience specific?
- Is the problem recognizable?
- Is the promised result realistic?
- Does the resource provide a genuine quick win?
- Is the content useful without purchasing anything?
- Does the resource sound like the creator?
- Are all stories true?
- Are all claims supported?
- Is the format appropriate?
- Are the instructions clear?
- Can the reader complete it?
- Is the next step natural?
- Has generic AI language been removed?
- Has unnecessary promotion been removed?
- Is private information protected?
- Would I exchange my email address for this?
- Would I feel satisfied after using it?
```

## Recommended Follow-Up Questions

### Follow-Up 1: Test the Value

```text
Evaluate whether this lead magnet provides enough practical value to justify asking for the reader’s contact information. Identify weak sections, missing actions, and places where the promise is not fully delivered.
```

### Follow-Up 2: Strengthen the Quick Win

```text
Identify the exact quick win the reader should achieve. Rewrite the resource so that the reader can reach that result with less confusion, less effort, and clearer completion criteria.
```

### Follow-Up 3: Reduce the Scope

```text
Review the lead magnet for unnecessary complexity. Identify content that should be removed, shortened, or saved for a larger paid resource so the lead magnet remains focused and usable.
```

### Follow-Up 4: Remove Generic AI Language

```text
Identify every phrase that sounds generic, artificial, overly polished, corporate, or promotional. Explain why it weakens the resource and rewrite it in the supplied brand voice.
```

### Follow-Up 5: Audit the Promise

```text
Compare the title, subtitle, landing-page promise, and actual content. Identify any mismatch between what is promised and what the resource delivers. Recommend corrections.
```

### Follow-Up 6: Improve the Landing Page

```text
Review the landing page for clarity, credibility, audience fit, friction, and trust. Rewrite weak sections without using exaggerated claims, false urgency, or manipulative language.
```

### Follow-Up 7: Create the Delivery Sequence

```text
Create a complete delivery and follow-up email sequence that helps the reader use the lead magnet, reach the promised quick win, and discover the next appropriate step.
```

### Follow-Up 8: Turn It Into a Challenge

```text
Transform this lead magnet into a five-day guided challenge. Create one practical action per day, a daily lesson, a reflection question, a completion check, and a natural final invitation.
```

## Human Review Checklist

Before publishing, confirm:

- Is the audience clearly defined?
- Does the resource solve one specific problem?
- Is the title accurate?
- Is the promise realistic?
- Is the quick win clear?
- Is the format appropriate?
- Are the instructions easy to follow?
- Is the resource useful without a purchase?
- Does it preserve the brand voice?
- Are all examples accurate?
- Are all claims supported?
- Are all sources represented honestly?
- Has filler been removed?
- Has generic AI language been removed?
- Has excessive promotion been removed?
- Is the next step proportionate?
- Is the landing page honest?
- Does the delivery email provide immediate access?
- Are accessibility needs addressed?
- Has confidential information been excluded?
- Would the creator defend every claim publicly?
- Would the intended reader feel that the resource respected their time?

## Important Principle

A lead magnet is not a brochure wearing a helpful hat.

It should solve a real problem, create a useful result, and give the reader a reason to trust the next conversation.
