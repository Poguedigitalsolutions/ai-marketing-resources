# Market Research Prompt

Use this prompt to investigate a market, customer segment, industry, trend, opportunity, or competitive landscape while clearly separating verified evidence from assumptions and interpretation.

This resource is part of the **Brand Voice AI** methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC**.

> Research should reduce uncertainty, not decorate assumptions with confident language.

---

## Purpose

This prompt helps organize market research into a practical decision-making report.

The goal is to understand:

- What market is being examined
- Who participates in it
- What customers are trying to accomplish
- What problems remain unresolved
- What alternatives already exist
- How competitors position themselves
- Which trends may matter
- Where opportunities or risks may exist
- What still requires verification

AI can assist with gathering, organizing, comparing, and interpreting information.

The human remains responsible for evaluating the evidence and making the decision.

## Before You Begin

Gather any available material that may help define the research.

Useful inputs include:

- Customer interviews
- Sales conversations
- Search trends
- Industry reports
- Government data
- Trade association reports
- Competitor websites
- Pricing pages
- Reviews
- Social media conversations
- Community discussions
- Research papers
- News coverage
- Market forecasts
- Existing customer data
- CRM notes
- Survey results
- Website analytics
- Previous research

Use current, credible, and relevant sources whenever possible.

Do not treat a single source, promotional claim, or AI-generated summary as sufficient evidence.

Do not include confidential customer data, passwords, private financial information, protected health information, or material you are not authorized to share.

## Master Prompt

Copy the prompt below into your preferred AI assistant.

Replace the bracketed sections with your information.

```text
You are helping me conduct structured market research.

Your job is to gather, organize, compare, and interpret relevant information without presenting assumptions as facts.

Use current and credible sources when research tools or internet access are available.

Prefer primary sources such as:

- Government agencies
- Regulatory bodies
- Academic research
- Trade associations
- Company filings
- Official company websites
- Original surveys
- Published datasets
- Direct customer evidence

Use reputable secondary sources when primary sources are unavailable.

Cite the source for factual claims.

Identify the publication date and relevant data period whenever possible.

When sources disagree, explain the disagreement.

Do not invent statistics, quotations, sources, market sizes, customer behavior, competitor information, pricing, trends, or forecasts.

When current information cannot be verified, label it clearly.

If internet or research access is unavailable, state that limitation and create a research plan rather than pretending the information is current.

BUSINESS OR PROJECT:

[Enter the business, brand, product, service, or idea.]

RESEARCH OBJECTIVE:

[Describe the decision this research should support.]

MARKET OR INDUSTRY:

[Define the market, industry, category, or field.]

PRODUCT OR SERVICE:

[Describe what is being offered or considered.]

TARGET CUSTOMER:

[Describe the believed customer segment.]

GEOGRAPHIC SCOPE:

[Local, regional, national, international, or a specific location.]

RESEARCH TIMEFRAME:

[Current market, last twelve months, five-year trend, historical comparison, or another period.]

CUSTOMER PROBLEM:

[Describe the problem or unmet need being investigated.]

CUSTOMER GOAL:

[Describe what the customer is trying to accomplish.]

KNOWN COMPETITORS OR ALTERNATIVES:

[List known companies, products, services, manual solutions, or substitutes.]

KNOWN FACTS:

[List information that may be treated as confirmed.]

ASSUMPTIONS TO TEST:

[List beliefs, hypotheses, or expectations that should be investigated.]

RESEARCH QUESTIONS:

[List the most important questions this research should answer.]

AVAILABLE SOURCE MATERIAL:

[Paste reports, notes, interviews, links, datasets, articles, reviews, or other evidence.]

BUDGET OR PRICE CONTEXT:

[Describe known pricing, customer budgets, or financial constraints.]

REGULATORY OR INDUSTRY CONSIDERATIONS:

[List known laws, regulations, standards, certifications, or professional requirements.]

DESIRED OUTPUT:

[Market overview, opportunity assessment, positioning recommendations, launch decision, content plan, or another deliverable.]

Using the supplied information and verifiable research, create a Market Research Report with the following sections:

1. Research Scope

State:

- Research objective
- Market definition
- Geographic scope
- Timeframe
- Target customer
- Product or service
- Primary decision being supported
- Important limitations

2. Source Review

Create a source table containing:

- Source
- Source type
- Publication date
- Data period
- Primary or secondary source
- Credibility
- Relevance
- Known limitation
- Link or citation

Separate supplied sources from newly researched sources.

3. Evidence Classification

Organize the information into:

- Verified facts
- Strongly supported findings
- Reasonable interpretations
- Assumptions requiring validation
- Conflicting evidence
- Important unknowns
- Outdated information
- Claims that should not be used

4. Market Definition

Define:

- Core market
- Adjacent markets
- Customer categories
- Product or service categories
- Geographic boundaries
- Relevant channels
- Important exclusions

Explain how the chosen definition affects the conclusions.

5. Market Size

When credible data is available, examine:

- Total addressable market
- Serviceable available market
- Serviceable obtainable market
- Current revenue
- Customer count
- Transaction volume
- Growth rate
- Geographic distribution

For every figure, include:

- Source
- Year
- Measurement method
- Currency
- Geographic scope
- Important limitations

Do not calculate false precision from weak evidence.

6. Market Growth and Direction

Analyze:

- Historical growth
- Current growth
- Forecast growth
- Major drivers
- Major constraints
- Structural changes
- Temporary changes
- Seasonal patterns
- Economic sensitivity

Separate observed trends from forecasts.

7. Customer Segments

Identify meaningful customer segments.

For each segment, include:

- Segment description
- Primary goal
- Main problem
- Buying context
- Budget sensitivity
- Decision process
- Preferred channel
- Current alternative
- Evidence supporting the segment
- Research still needed

Do not invent demographic details.

8. Customer Needs

Identify:

- Functional needs
- Emotional needs
- Professional needs
- Social needs
- Convenience needs
- Risk-reduction needs
- Support needs
- Information needs

Separate directly stated needs from interpretation.

9. Jobs to Be Done

Explain what customers are trying to accomplish.

Include:

- Practical job
- Emotional job
- Social job
- Trigger situation
- Desired progress
- Current obstacle
- Existing workaround
- Successful outcome

10. Customer Language

Create a voice-of-customer language bank using supplied or researched customer evidence.

Organize exact wording into:

- Problem language
- Goal language
- Frustration language
- Objection language
- Comparison language
- Trust language
- Purchase language
- Outcome language

Do not rewrite customer language into marketing language.

11. Demand Signals

Evaluate available signals such as:

- Search behavior
- Customer inquiries
- Review volume
- Community discussions
- Product adoption
- Sales activity
- Funding
- Job postings
- Event attendance
- Social engagement
- Competitor expansion
- Regulatory changes

Explain what each signal can and cannot prove.

12. Competitor Landscape

Identify:

- Direct competitors
- Indirect competitors
- Substitute solutions
- Manual alternatives
- Free alternatives
- Internal solutions
- Doing nothing

For each competitor or alternative, include:

- Name
- Offer
- Target customer
- Positioning
- Price when publicly available
- Distribution channel
- Strengths
- Weaknesses
- Evidence
- Important unknowns

13. Competitive Positioning Map

Compare competitors across relevant dimensions such as:

- Price
- Complexity
- Speed
- Personalization
- Human support
- Automation
- Specialization
- Ease of use
- Audience
- Delivery model
- Trust
- Results

Explain why the chosen dimensions matter.

14. Competitor Messaging

Analyze competitor messaging for:

- Headline
- Value proposition
- Primary promise
- Audience
- Problem addressed
- Benefits
- Proof
- CTA
- Tone
- Repeated language
- Differentiators
- Unsupported claims

Do not copy competitor language.

15. Pricing Landscape

When public information is available, compare:

- Entry price
- Standard price
- Premium price
- Subscription model
- Project pricing
- Hourly pricing
- Free tier
- Trial
- Contract terms
- Add-on costs
- Discount structure
- Pricing transparency

Identify where pricing information is missing or outdated.

16. Buying Process

Map the customer’s likely buying process:

- Trigger
- Initial research
- Problem definition
- Comparison
- Internal discussion
- Budget approval
- Risk evaluation
- Purchase
- Onboarding
- Renewal
- Referral

For each stage, identify the customer’s likely question.

17. Decision Criteria

Identify what customers may evaluate, including:

- Price
- Credibility
- Experience
- Ease of use
- Speed
- Support
- Security
- Flexibility
- Customization
- Values alignment
- Proof
- Reputation
- Integration
- Training
- Risk

Separate confirmed criteria from likely criteria.

18. Barriers to Entry

Analyze barriers such as:

- Capital
- Technology
- Expertise
- Distribution
- Brand recognition
- Customer trust
- Regulation
- Certifications
- Partnerships
- Data access
- Intellectual property
- Switching costs
- Network effects
- Customer acquisition cost

19. Regulatory and Compliance Landscape

Identify relevant:

- Laws
- Regulations
- Licensing
- Certifications
- Industry standards
- Privacy requirements
- Advertising rules
- Accessibility requirements
- Procurement rules
- Professional restrictions

Do not provide legal conclusions.

Flag matters requiring professional review.

20. Technology Landscape

Analyze technologies affecting the market, including:

- Artificial intelligence
- Automation
- Cloud platforms
- Data systems
- Customer platforms
- Communication tools
- Analytics
- Integration requirements
- Security
- Emerging technologies

Separate current adoption from speculation.

21. Trend Analysis

Identify trends across:

- Customer behavior
- Technology
- Regulation
- Pricing
- Competition
- Distribution
- Workforce
- Culture
- Demographics
- Economics
- Content
- Sales

For each trend, include:

- Evidence
- Timeframe
- Likely impact
- Confidence level
- Risk of overinterpretation

22. Opportunity Areas

Identify possible opportunities based on evidence.

For each opportunity, include:

- Customer segment
- Unresolved problem
- Existing alternatives
- Evidence of demand
- Competitive intensity
- Potential differentiation
- Required capabilities
- Main risk
- Validation needed

23. Market Gaps

Identify gaps such as:

- Underserved segments
- Poor customer experience
- Weak education
- Inconsistent quality
- High complexity
- Lack of personalization
- Missing integrations
- Poor support
- Geographic gaps
- Pricing gaps
- Trust gaps
- Accessibility gaps

Do not treat the absence of evidence as proof of opportunity.

24. Risk Analysis

Evaluate:

- Market risk
- Demand risk
- Competitive risk
- Pricing risk
- Regulatory risk
- Technology risk
- Operational risk
- Reputation risk
- Customer-acquisition risk
- Timing risk
- Concentration risk
- Evidence risk

For each risk, include likelihood, impact, evidence, and mitigation.

25. SWOT Analysis

Create an evidence-based SWOT analysis.

Separate:

- Strengths supported by internal evidence
- Weaknesses supported by internal evidence
- Opportunities supported by market evidence
- Threats supported by market evidence

Do not use generic filler.

26. Strategic Options

Create three to five strategic options.

For each option, include:

- Description
- Target segment
- Customer problem
- Offer
- Differentiation
- Required resources
- Time to test
- Evidence supporting it
- Main risk
- Validation method

27. Positioning Recommendations

Recommend:

- Primary audience
- Category
- Problem addressed
- Value proposition
- Differentiator
- Proof required
- Messaging emphasis
- Language to avoid
- Best initial channel
- Best initial offer

Clearly distinguish recommendations from facts.

28. Validation Plan

Create a practical research and validation plan using:

- Customer interviews
- Surveys
- Landing-page tests
- Outreach
- Pilot programs
- Pre-sales
- Content response
- Search analysis
- Competitive monitoring
- Pricing tests
- Partnership conversations
- Prototype testing

For each method, include:

- Question being tested
- Participant
- Method
- Success signal
- Failure signal
- Minimum evidence needed
- Bias to avoid

29. Customer Interview Questions

Create twenty customer interview questions.

The questions should explore:

- Current situation
- Recent trigger
- Problem severity
- Existing alternatives
- Previous attempts
- Decision criteria
- Budget
- Trust
- Desired outcome
- Language
- Purchase process

Use open-ended questions and ask for specific examples.

30. Survey Questions

Create a concise survey containing:

- Qualification questions
- Current behavior
- Problem frequency
- Problem severity
- Current solution
- Satisfaction
- Desired outcome
- Purchase interest
- Price context
- Optional follow-up permission

Avoid leading questions.

31. Research Gaps

List unanswered questions.

For each gap, include:

- Why it matters
- Best source
- Research method
- Required freshness
- Decision affected
- Priority

32. Opportunity Scorecard

Create a scorecard using criteria such as:

- Customer pain
- Evidence of demand
- Market growth
- Competitive intensity
- Differentiation
- Ability to deliver
- Revenue potential
- Cost to enter
- Time to validate
- Strategic fit
- Regulatory risk
- Customer access

Explain the scoring system and its limitations.

33. Content Opportunities

Create content ideas based on research findings:

- Educational articles
- Comparison guides
- FAQs
- Lead magnets
- Social posts
- Email campaigns
- Webinars
- Workshops
- Case studies
- Customer tools
- Community discussions

34. Final Market Summary

Finish with:

- Market examined
- Target segment
- Primary customer problem
- Strongest demand signal
- Leading alternative
- Most relevant competitor
- Main market trend
- Strongest opportunity
- Biggest risk
- Most important unknown
- Recommended validation step
- Confidence level
- One-sentence market conclusion

35. Human Review Questions

Finish by asking:

- Are the sources credible?
- Is the information current?
- Are market boundaries clear?
- Are facts separated from interpretation?
- Are assumptions labeled?
- Are customer needs supported?
- Are competitors represented accurately?
- Are prices current?
- Are trends supported by evidence?
- Are risks acknowledged?
- Are recommendations proportionate to the evidence?
- What still needs to be verified?
- Would I make a significant decision based on this evidence?
```

## Recommended Follow-Up Questions

### Follow-Up 1: Challenge the Evidence

```text
Review the market research and identify every conclusion that relies on weak, outdated, indirect, promotional, or incomplete evidence. Explain what stronger evidence is needed.
```

### Follow-Up 2: Test the Market Definition

```text
Challenge the way the market has been defined. Identify whether it is too broad, too narrow, or missing important adjacent markets, substitutes, or customer segments.
```

### Follow-Up 3: Verify the Opportunity

```text
Evaluate the strongest identified opportunity. Separate evidence of customer pain, evidence of demand, evidence of willingness to pay, and evidence that this business can deliver the solution.
```

### Follow-Up 4: Build the Interview Plan

```text
Create a customer interview plan to test the most important assumptions. Ask one question at a time and use each answer to choose the next question.
```

### Follow-Up 5: Compare the Competitors

```text
Create a competitor comparison table using only verifiable information. Include positioning, audience, offer, pricing, proof, strengths, weaknesses, and important unknowns.
```

### Follow-Up 6: Create the Validation Sprint

```text
Turn the research gaps into a fourteen-day validation sprint. Include daily actions, interview targets, tests, evidence requirements, and decision checkpoints.
```

## Human Review Checklist

Before using the research, confirm:

- Is the market clearly defined?
- Are sources current?
- Are sources credible?
- Are primary sources prioritized?
- Are publication dates visible?
- Are customer claims supported?
- Are competitor claims supported?
- Are prices current?
- Are assumptions labeled?
- Are disagreements between sources explained?
- Are market-size limitations disclosed?
- Are trends separated from forecasts?
- Are opportunities based on evidence?
- Are risks included?
- Are legal questions flagged?
- Is further validation required?
- Would the decision change if one major assumption is wrong?

---

## Important Principle

Market research does not eliminate uncertainty.

It gives uncertainty a shape, a source, and a next question.

The best report is not the one that sounds most certain.

It is the one that helps someone make the next decision with their eyes open.
