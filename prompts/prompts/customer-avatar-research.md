# Customer Avatar Research Prompt

Use this prompt to build an evidence-based customer avatar without relying on stereotypes, vague assumptions, or invented psychology.

This resource is part of the **Brand Voice AI** methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC**.

> Better marketing begins with better understanding.

---

## Purpose

This prompt helps organize what you know about a customer into a practical research profile.

The goal is not to create an imaginary “perfect customer.”

The goal is to understand:

- Who the customer is
- What they are trying to accomplish
- What is slowing them down
- What they have already tried
- What they believe
- What they fear
- What influences their decisions
- What language they naturally use
- What would help them trust an offer

---

## Best Sources

Use real customer evidence whenever possible.

Helpful sources include:

- Customer interviews
- Sales calls
- Discovery calls
- Support conversations
- Surveys
- Reviews
- Testimonials
- Emails
- Social media comments
- Search queries
- Community discussions
- Frequently asked questions
- Lost-sale feedback
- Website analytics
- CRM notes
- Customer success stories

Do not include private customer information, passwords, protected health information, confidential records, or anything you are not authorized to share.

## Master Prompt

Copy the prompt below into your preferred AI assistant.

Replace the bracketed sections with your information.

```text
You are helping me research and document a practical customer avatar.

Your job is not to invent a fictional personality or fill gaps with stereotypes.

Use only the information I provide.

Separate direct evidence, reasonable interpretation, and unknown information.

When evidence is weak or missing, say so clearly.

BUSINESS OR BRAND:

[Enter the business, company, or brand name.]

PRODUCT OR SERVICE:

[Describe the offer, what it provides, and how it is delivered.]

PRIMARY CUSTOMER:

[Describe the customer group you currently believe is the best fit.]

GEOGRAPHIC OR MARKET CONTEXT:

[Describe the location, market, industry, or community if relevant.]

CUSTOMER GOAL:

[What is the customer trying to accomplish?]

CUSTOMER PROBLEM:

[What problem, frustration, delay, risk, or unmet need are they experiencing?]

CURRENT ALTERNATIVES:

[What are they currently doing instead of buying this offer?]

KNOWN OBJECTIONS:

[List the reasons customers hesitate, delay, decline, or choose another option.]

CUSTOMER LANGUAGE:

[Paste phrases, questions, complaints, reviews, emails, comments, or interview statements written in the customer’s own words.]

CUSTOMER EVIDENCE:

[Paste interview notes, survey responses, sales-call notes, testimonials, reviews, support questions, or other relevant evidence.]

PURCHASE CONTEXT:

[Describe how, when, where, and why the customer begins looking for help.]

DECISION INFLUENCES:

[Describe the people, information, experiences, risks, or pressures that influence the decision.]

SUCCESS OUTCOME:

[Describe what success looks like after the customer uses the offer.]

Using only the supplied information, create a Customer Avatar Research Report with the following sections:

1. Avatar Summary

Write a concise description of the customer, their situation, their main goal, and the problem they are trying to solve.

2. Evidence Quality

Separate the information into:

- Directly supported by evidence
- Reasonable interpretation
- Assumptions requiring confirmation
- Important unknowns

3. Customer Situation

Describe the customer’s current circumstances, responsibilities, pressures, and relevant environment.

Do not add demographic details unless they are supplied or clearly supported.

4. Primary Goals

Identify the customer’s most important:

- Functional goals
- Emotional goals
- Professional goals
- Social goals
- Short-term goals
- Long-term goals

5. Problems and Frustrations

Identify the practical and emotional problems the customer experiences.

For each problem, explain:

- What happens
- Why it matters
- What it may cost them
- What evidence supports the conclusion

6. Jobs to Be Done

Explain what the customer is trying to accomplish when they seek this kind of product or service.

Include:

- The task they need completed
- The progress they want to make
- The situation that creates urgency
- The result they are hoping to achieve

7. Trigger Events

Identify events or moments that may cause the customer to begin looking for help.

Examples may include:

- A missed opportunity
- A deadline
- A business change
- A painful failure
- A new responsibility
- A financial pressure
- A customer complaint
- A competitive threat
- A personal realization

Only include triggers supported by the supplied information.

8. Current Alternatives

Describe what the customer does before purchasing.

Include:

- Doing nothing
- Solving the problem manually
- Using free resources
- Hiring someone else
- Using another product
- Asking friends or colleagues
- Delaying the decision

Explain why each alternative may feel safer, easier, or more familiar.

9. Barriers and Objections

Identify the customer’s likely barriers, including:

- Price
- Time
- Trust
- Risk
- Complexity
- Fear of change
- Lack of urgency
- Unclear value
- Previous disappointment
- Internal approval
- Competing priorities

Separate confirmed objections from inferred objections.

10. Decision Criteria

Explain what the customer may need to see, understand, or believe before purchasing.

Include factors such as:

- Credibility
- Demonstrated results
- Ease of use
- Personal attention
- Speed
- Price
- Support
- Experience
- Security
- Flexibility
- Values alignment

11. Customer Language

Identify the exact words, phrases, questions, complaints, and expressions customers use.

Organize the language into:

- Problem language
- Goal language
- Emotional language
- Objection language
- Trust language
- Purchase language

Do not rewrite the customer’s language into marketing language.

12. Emotional Landscape

Describe the emotions associated with:

- The current problem
- Previous failed attempts
- Searching for help
- Evaluating options
- Making a purchase
- Achieving success

Clearly identify which emotions are directly stated and which are interpretations.

13. Customer Journey

Map the likely journey across these stages:

- Unaware
- Problem aware
- Solution aware
- Offer aware
- Decision
- Onboarding
- Early success
- Long-term success
- Advocacy or referral

For each stage, include:

- What the customer may be thinking
- What they may be feeling
- What question they need answered
- What type of content or support may help

14. Trust Builders

Identify what could increase confidence, such as:

- Case studies
- Testimonials
- Demonstrations
- Samples
- Clear pricing
- Guarantees
- Credentials
- Transparent processes
- Personal stories
- Referrals
- Proof of experience
- Human access and support

15. Trust Breakers

Identify messaging or behavior that may reduce trust, including:

- Exaggerated claims
- Generic promises
- Pressure tactics
- Hidden costs
- Unclear language
- Unproven results
- Overly technical explanations
- Lack of personal attention
- Inconsistent communication

16. Messaging Opportunities

Create practical messaging guidance for:

- Website copy
- Social media
- Email
- Articles
- Lead magnets
- Sales conversations
- Customer onboarding

For each channel, explain what message should be emphasized and why.

17. Content Questions

List the questions the customer is likely to ask before making a decision.

Organize them into:

- Problem questions
- Solution questions
- Comparison questions
- Price questions
- Trust questions
- Implementation questions
- Results questions

18. Interview Questions

Create fifteen customer interview questions that would help confirm or correct the avatar.

The questions should encourage detailed stories rather than yes-or-no answers.

19. Avatar Quick Reference

Finish with a concise reference card containing:

- Primary customer
- Main goal
- Main problem
- Trigger event
- Current alternative
- Biggest objection
- Primary emotional concern
- Main decision criterion
- Desired outcome
- Most important customer phrase
- One-sentence avatar summary

Do not present assumptions as facts.

Do not invent demographic details, motivations, fears, or behaviors that are not supported by the supplied information.
```

## Recommended Follow-Up Questions

### Follow-Up 1: Challenge the Assumptions

```text
Which parts of this customer avatar are supported by direct evidence, and which parts are assumptions that still need to be tested?
```

### Follow-Up 2: Identify Missing Research

```text
What important information is missing from this customer avatar, and what research method would best help me obtain it?
```

### Follow-Up 3: Create the Interview

```text
Create a customer discovery interview that will help validate this avatar. Ask one question at a time, wait for my answer, and use each response to choose the next question.
```

### Follow-Up 4: Extract Customer Language

```text
Review the supplied customer evidence and create a voice-of-customer language bank. Preserve the customer’s wording and organize it by problems, goals, emotions, objections, questions, and desired outcomes.
```

---

## Human Review Checklist

Before approving the avatar, confirm:

- Is it based on real evidence?
- Are assumptions clearly labeled?
- Does it avoid stereotypes?
- Does it preserve the customer’s actual language?
- Are the customer’s goals specific?
- Are the frustrations connected to real consequences?
- Are the objections believable?
- Are decision criteria supported?
- Does the journey reflect how customers actually buy?
- Are important unknowns acknowledged?
- Could this avatar guide content and sales conversations?
- Would a real customer recognize themselves in it?

---

## Important Principle

A customer avatar should be a research tool, not a cardboard character.

It should become more accurate as new interviews, sales conversations, reviews, and customer results are added.
