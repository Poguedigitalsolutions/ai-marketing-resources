# Article Development Prompt

Use this prompt to develop thoughtful, evidence-aware articles that preserve the writer’s brand voice while using AI for research organization, outlining, drafting, and refinement.

This resource is part of the **Brand Voice AI** methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC**.

> Open Big → AI Does the Heavy Lifting → Close Big

---

## Purpose

This prompt helps turn an idea, observation, story, research source, or business lesson into a complete article.

The goal is not to ask AI to produce generic content from a topic.

The goal is to combine:

- A clear human perspective
- Relevant evidence
- Personal stories
- Brand voice
- Practical value
- Strong structure
- Human review

AI can help prepare and organize the work.

The writer remains responsible for the message.

This prompt can be used for:

## Before You Begin

Gather any material that may help develop the article.

Useful inputs include:

- The original idea
- Personal experiences
- Customer questions
- Research notes
- Source links
- Quotes
- Statistics
- Industry observations
- Previous posts
- Interview notes
- Examples
- Contrasting viewpoints
- A desired call to action

Do not ask the AI to invent facts, quotations, research findings, customer stories, or personal experiences.

---

## Master Prompt

Copy the prompt below into your preferred AI assistant.

Replace the bracketed sections with your information.

```text
You are helping me develop a thoughtful article in my authentic brand voice.

Your role is to organize the idea, identify the strongest angle, develop the structure, help draft the article, and improve clarity.

Do not invent facts, statistics, quotations, personal experiences, customer stories, research findings, sources, or credentials.

Separate supplied facts from interpretation.

When information requires verification, label it clearly.

The article should follow this structure:

OPEN BIG

Begin with a strong human problem, recognizable tension, surprising observation, meaningful question, or personal moment.

AI DOES THE HEAVY LIFTING

Use the middle of the article to organize evidence, explain the idea, compare options, identify patterns, teach the framework, and give practical guidance.

CLOSE BIG

End with human judgment, personal meaning, a memorable lesson, a challenge, a story, or a clear next step.

AUTHOR OR BRAND:

[Enter the name of the writer, company, or brand.]

BRAND VOICE:

[Paste a brand voice summary, voice rules, examples, or communication guidance.]

ARTICLE PLATFORM:

[LinkedIn, Writing.io, Substack, website, newsletter, or another platform.]

ARTICLE TOPIC:

[Describe the subject.]

CORE IDEA:

[State the main point in one or two sentences.]

WHY THIS MATTERS:

[Explain why the audience should care.]

TARGET AUDIENCE:

[Describe the intended reader.]

READER PROBLEM:

[What problem, fear, question, frustration, or opportunity does the article address?]

DESIRED READER OUTCOME:

[What should the reader understand, feel, believe, or do after reading?]

PERSONAL STORY OR EXPERIENCE:

[Describe any personal experience that should be included.]

CUSTOMER OR BUSINESS EXAMPLE:

[Describe any relevant example that may be used.]

SOURCE MATERIAL:

[Paste research notes, links, quotations, statistics, articles, transcripts, reports, or supporting information.]

KNOWN FACTS:

[List facts that may be treated as confirmed.]

INFORMATION REQUIRING VERIFICATION:

[List claims, dates, statistics, quotations, or assumptions that must be checked.]

KEY PHRASES OR IDEAS TO INCLUDE:

[List important language, themes, concepts, frameworks, or quotations.]

WORDS, CLAIMS, OR STYLES TO AVOID:

[List language that feels generic, exaggerated, promotional, false, overly technical, or inconsistent with the brand.]

DESIRED LENGTH:

[Enter an approximate word count or article length.]

CALL TO ACTION:

[Describe what the reader should do next.]

Using only the supplied information, create an Article Development Report with the following sections:

1. Source and Evidence Review

Separate the supplied material into:

- Confirmed information
- Personal opinion
- Interpretation
- Claims requiring verification
- Missing information
- Material that should not be used without additional support

Do not begin drafting until this distinction is clear.

2. Article Purpose

State:

- The central purpose
- The main reader
- The reader’s problem
- The desired outcome
- The article’s primary argument

3. Article Angle Options

Create five possible article angles.

For each angle, include:

- Working title
- Central argument
- Opening approach
- Why the angle may resonate
- Risks or weaknesses

Recommend the strongest angle and explain why.

4. Thesis

Write one clear thesis statement.

The thesis should express the article’s primary argument without sounding academic, inflated, or generic.

5. Headline Options

Create ten headline options across these styles:

- Direct
- Curiosity-based
- Problem-focused
- Contrarian
- Story-driven
- Question-based
- Practical
- Thought leadership
- Emotional
- Search-friendly

Do not use clickbait or make promises the article cannot support.

6. Subtitle Options

Create five subtitle or deck options that clarify the article’s value.

7. Opening Options

Create three possible openings:

- A personal story opening
- A problem or tension opening
- A provocative question or observation opening

Each opening should establish why the article matters.

8. Article Structure

Build a detailed outline using:

OPEN BIG

Identify:

- The opening tension
- The human problem
- The reason the reader should continue
- The transition into the main idea

AI DOES THE HEAVY LIFTING

Identify:

- The primary explanation
- Supporting evidence
- Frameworks
- Examples
- Contrasting ideas
- Practical guidance
- Questions the reader may have
- Logical transitions

CLOSE BIG

Identify:

- The final lesson
- The human judgment
- The personal meaning
- The call to action
- The final memorable line

9. First Draft

Write the full article.

The article should:

- Follow the approved structure
- Preserve the supplied brand voice
- Use clear language
- Avoid unnecessary jargon
- Include personal experience where relevant
- Distinguish fact from opinion
- Avoid unsupported certainty
- Provide practical value
- Use readable paragraphs
- Avoid repetitive conclusions
- End with a strong human-centered close

10. Brand Voice Review

Review the draft for:

- Tone
- Vocabulary
- Rhythm
- Sentence length
- Storytelling
- Emotional range
- Signature language
- Voice dos
- Voice don’ts
- Generic AI phrasing
- Excessive polish
- Unnatural transitions

Identify passages that do not sound consistent with the supplied voice.

11. Generic Language Audit

Highlight language that sounds:

- Generic
- Corporate
- Overly polished
- Predictable
- Inflated
- Promotional
- Artificial
- Emotionally empty
- Repetitive
- Detached from the writer

Suggest more natural alternatives.

12. Factuality Review

Create a table with:

- Claim
- Source supplied
- Verification status
- Risk level
- Recommended action

Do not create sources that were not supplied.

13. Story Integration Review

Evaluate whether the article uses personal stories effectively.

Explain:

- Whether the story supports the article
- Whether it feels connected or forced
- Whether more context is needed
- Whether the lesson is clear
- Whether the story should appear earlier or later

14. Practical Value Review

Identify what the reader can:

- Understand
- Apply
- Question
- Change
- Discuss
- Try immediately

Recommend improvements if the article remains too abstract.

15. Closing Options

Create three possible closings:

- Reflective
- Action-oriented
- Story-driven

Each closing should reinforce the article’s central message without simply repeating the introduction.

16. Call-to-Action Options

Create five calls to action:

- Conversation CTA
- Comment CTA
- Resource CTA
- Community CTA
- Service CTA

The calls to action should feel natural and proportionate to the article.

17. Platform Adaptation

Explain how the article should be adapted for:

- LinkedIn
- Writing.io
- Substack

For each platform, recommend:

- Headline approach
- Opening length
- Paragraph style
- Article length
- CTA style
- Link strategy
- Engagement strategy

18. Article Teaser

Create three short promotional posts that encourage readers to open the full article.

Each teaser should:

- Introduce the tension
- Avoid summarizing the entire article
- Give the reader a reason to click
- Preserve the brand voice
- Avoid exaggerated claims

19. Repurposing Plan

Create a repurposing plan containing:

- Five short social posts
- Three quote graphics
- One email introduction
- One LinkedIn teaser
- One discussion question
- One short video concept
- One carousel outline
- One community post
- One lead magnet idea

20. Final Human Review Questions

Finish by asking:

- Is the central idea genuinely mine?
- Does the article sound recognizable?
- Are the facts supported?
- Are interpretations clearly distinguished?
- Does the story belong in the article?
- Does the article provide practical value?
- Is the opening strong enough?
- Does the middle remain focused?
- Does the close feel human?
- Would I be comfortable defending every claim publicly?
```

---

## Recommended Follow-Up Questions

### Follow-Up 1: Strengthen the Opening

```text
Review the opening of this article. Does it create enough tension, curiosity, or emotional relevance to make the intended reader continue? Suggest three stronger openings without changing the central idea.
```

### Follow-Up 2: Remove Generic AI Language

```text
Identify every sentence that sounds generic, overly polished, predictable, corporate, or AI-generated. Explain why it feels unnatural and suggest a more human alternative.
```

### Follow-Up 3: Test the Argument

```text
Challenge the central argument of this article. Identify weak assumptions, missing evidence, alternative explanations, and questions a skeptical reader may raise.
```

### Follow-Up 4: Strengthen the Human Close

```text
Review the ending. Rewrite it so the article closes with stronger human judgment, personal meaning, and a memorable final line without becoming sentimental or repetitive.
```

### Follow-Up 5: Repurpose the Article

```text
Repurpose this article for LinkedIn, Writing.io, Substack, email, and short-form social media. Preserve the central idea and brand voice while adapting the format for each channel.
```

---

## Human Review Checklist

Before publishing, confirm:

- Is the central idea clear?
- Does the article sound like the writer?
- Are all factual claims supported?
- Are sources represented accurately?
- Are personal stories true?
- Are opinions labeled appropriately?
- Does the opening earn attention?
- Does the middle provide useful substance?
- Does the close leave the reader with something meaningful?
- Is the call to action natural?
- Has generic AI language been removed?
- Has confidential information been excluded?
- Would the writer defend the article publicly?
- Does the article contribute something beyond recycled advice?

## Important Principle

AI can help organize the research, test the argument, develop the structure, and prepare the draft.

The human must supply the perspective, judgment, experience, and final approval.
