# Competitive Analysis Prompt

Use this prompt to compare competitors, substitute solutions, and customer alternatives using verifiable public evidence rather than assumptions, imitation, or surface-level feature lists.

This resource is part of the **Brand Voice AI** methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC**.

> Competitive research should sharpen your direction, not turn your brand into an echo.

---

## Purpose

This prompt helps organize competitive research into a practical decision-making report.

The goal is to understand:

- Who serves the same audience
- What problems competitors claim to solve
- How competing offers are structured
- How each company positions itself
- What proof supports its claims
- How customers describe their experiences
- What alternatives exist beyond direct competitors
- Where meaningful gaps may exist
- What should be verified before making a decision

AI can help gather, organize, compare, and interpret public information.

The human remains responsible for evaluating the evidence, respecting intellectual property, and deciding how the findings should influence strategy.

## Competitor Categories

A complete analysis may include:

### Direct Competitors

Businesses offering a similar solution to a similar customer.

### Indirect Competitors

Businesses solving the same underlying problem through a different type of offer.

### Substitute Solutions

Products, services, tools, or behaviors customers may use instead.

### Do-It-Yourself Alternatives

Manual processes, templates, free tools, internal staff, or self-education.

### Internal Alternatives

The customer assigns the work to an employee, department, contractor, or existing system.

### The Status Quo

The customer delays action, accepts the problem, or does nothing.

The status quo may be the strongest competitor.

## Ethical Research Guidelines

Use publicly available and properly authorized information.

Appropriate sources may include:

- Official company websites
- Public pricing pages
- Product documentation
- Public demonstrations
- Company filings
- Press releases
- Public case studies
- Customer reviews
- Public social media
- Public interviews
- Trade publications
- Government records
- Publicly available job postings
- Public webinars
- Published research
- Public app marketplaces
- Public community discussions

Do not:

- Misrepresent your identity
- Seek confidential information
- Access private systems
- Circumvent access controls
- Copy proprietary material
- Reproduce protected content
- Publish personal customer information
- Present rumors as facts
- Treat anonymous claims as confirmed evidence
- Contact employees under false pretenses

## Master Prompt

Copy the prompt below into your preferred AI assistant.

Replace the bracketed sections with your information.

```text
You are helping me conduct a structured and ethical competitive analysis.

Your job is to gather, organize, compare, and interpret publicly available information without presenting assumptions as facts.

Use current and credible sources when internet or research access is available.

Prefer primary sources such as:

- Official company websites
- Public pricing pages
- Product documentation
- Company filings
- Official announcements
- Public demonstrations
- Published case studies
- Regulatory records
- Public datasets
- Direct customer evidence

Use reputable secondary sources when primary sources are unavailable.

Cite the source for factual claims.

Include publication dates, access dates, and relevant time periods whenever possible.

When information cannot be verified, label it clearly.

When sources disagree, describe the disagreement.

Do not invent competitors, prices, features, customer results, quotations, statistics, market share, partnerships, credentials, reviews, or strategic conclusions.

Do not copy competitor language for use as original brand messaging.

If internet or research access is unavailable, state the limitation and create a research plan rather than pretending the information is current.

BUSINESS OR PROJECT:

[Enter the business, brand, product, service, or project.]

RESEARCH OBJECTIVE:

[Describe the decision this analysis should support.]

BUSINESS OFFER:

[Describe the product, service, program, platform, or solution.]

TARGET CUSTOMER:

[Describe the intended customer.]

CUSTOMER PROBLEM:

[Describe the problem, frustration, delay, risk, or unmet need being addressed.]

CUSTOMER GOAL:

[Describe what the customer is trying to accomplish.]

GEOGRAPHIC SCOPE:

[Local, regional, national, international, or a specific location.]

INDUSTRY OR CATEGORY:

[Describe the market or category.]

KNOWN DIRECT COMPETITORS:

[List known direct competitors.]

KNOWN INDIRECT COMPETITORS:

[List known indirect competitors.]

KNOWN SUBSTITUTE SOLUTIONS:

[List tools, manual processes, free options, internal solutions, or alternatives.]

KNOWN FACTS:

[List information that may be treated as confirmed.]

ASSUMPTIONS TO TEST:

[List beliefs or hypotheses that should be investigated.]

IMPORTANT COMPARISON CRITERIA:

[List factors such as price, speed, specialization, support, automation, customization, accessibility, security, or results.]

AVAILABLE SOURCE MATERIAL:

[Paste links, notes, reports, reviews, screenshots, pricing information, or other evidence.]

BRAND POSITIONING:

[Describe how the business currently presents itself.]

BRAND VOICE:

[Paste the brand voice summary, positioning guidance, voice rules, or examples.]

DESIRED OUTPUT:

[Positioning recommendations, pricing comparison, offer strategy, content opportunities, sales guidance, or another deliverable.]

Using only supplied information and verifiable public evidence, create a Competitive Analysis Report with the following sections:

- Access date
- Primary or secondary source

- Relevance
- Limitation
- Link or citation

Separate supplied sources from newly researched sources.

4. Evidence Classification

Organize findings into:

- Verified facts
- Strongly supported findings
- Reasonable interpretations
- Assumptions requiring confirmation
- Conflicting information
- Outdated information
- Important unknowns
- Claims that should not be used

5. Competitor Profiles

Create a profile for each competitor containing:

- Company name
- Website
- Location
- Year founded when verified
- Ownership or company structure when relevant
- Primary audience
- Customer problem addressed
- Main offer
- Delivery model
- Pricing when public
- Positioning
- Primary promise
- Features
- Benefits
- Proof
- Credentials
- Customer support
- Sales process
- Distribution channels
- Strengths
- Weaknesses
- Important unknowns
- Sources

6. Offer Comparison

Compare each competitor’s offer across:

- Core product or service
- Deliverables
- Scope
- Format
- Customization
- Access
- Support
- Training
- Implementation
- Duration
- Contract structure
- Cancellation terms
- Guarantees
- Add-ons
- Customer responsibilities
- Exclusions

Do not infer unavailable details.

7. Audience Comparison

For each competitor, identify:

- Stated audience
- Implied audience
- Industry focus
- Business size
- Customer sophistication
- Geographic focus
- Awareness level
- Budget level when supported

- Evidence

- Positioning strengths
- Positioning weaknesses

- Customer-acquisition risk
- Reputation risk
- Legal risk

43. Human Review Questions

- Are direct and indirect alternatives included?
- Is the status quo included?

- Are prices current?
- Are claims verified?
```

## Recommended Follow-Up Questions

### Follow-Up 1: Challenge the Competitor List

```text
Review the competitor list and identify missing direct competitors, indirect competitors, substitutes, do-it-yourself options, internal alternatives, free alternatives, and status-quo behaviors.
```

### Follow-Up 2: Verify the Evidence

```text
Identify every competitive conclusion based on weak, outdated, promotional, indirect, or incomplete evidence. Explain what stronger evidence is needed.
```

### Follow-Up 3: Test the Differentiation

```text
Evaluate the proposed differentiation. Determine whether it is meaningful to customers, supported by a real capability, difficult to copy, and clearly distinct from competitor messaging.
```

### Follow-Up 4: Compare the Offers

```text
Create a side-by-side offer comparison using only verifiable public information. Include audience, problem, deliverables, pricing, support, proof, strengths, weaknesses, and important unknowns.
```

### Follow-Up 5: Analyze Customer Reviews

```text
Analyze the available competitor reviews. Separate repeated praise, repeated complaints, isolated experiences, expectations, support issues, value perceptions, and language customers use when comparing options.
```

### Follow-Up 6: Create the Monitoring System

```text
Create a monthly competitive-monitoring process using lawful public sources. Include what to check, where to check it, who should review it, and what changes should trigger strategic discussion.
```

### Follow-Up 7: Build the Positioning Options

```text
Create five positioning options that respond to the competitive landscape without copying competitor language. Explain the customer relevance, proof required, risk, and validation method for each.
```

## Human Review Checklist

Before using the analysis, confirm:

- Are direct competitors included?
- Are indirect competitors included?
- Are substitutes included?
- Is the status quo included?
- Are public sources cited?
- Are dates recorded?
- Are prices current?
- Are claims verified?
- Are reviews represented fairly?
- Are facts separated from interpretation?
- Are competitors’ strengths acknowledged?
- Are weaknesses supported by evidence?
- Are gaps validated?
- Does the differentiation matter to customers?
- Can the business actually deliver the differentiator?
- Has competitor language been avoided?
- Has confidential information been excluded?
- Have intellectual-property boundaries been respected?
- Would the business defend the analysis publicly?

## Important Principle

Competitive analysis is not a scavenger hunt for ideas to copy.

It is a navigational chart.

It shows where others are sailing, where customers are still stranded, and where your capabilities may support a course of your own.
