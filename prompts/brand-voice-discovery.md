# Brand Voice Discovery Prompt

Use this prompt to help identify the language, personality, values, stories, and communication patterns that make a person or organization sound distinctive.

This resource is part of the **Brand Voice AI** methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC**.

> AI should amplify human expression, not replace it.

---

## Purpose

This prompt helps transform scattered information about a founder, business, or personal brand into a structured brand voice profile.

The goal is not to manufacture a personality.

The goal is to recognize, document, and preserve the voice that already exists.

---

## Before You Begin

Gather several examples of the person or organization communicating naturally.

Useful examples include:

- Articles
- Social media posts
- Emails
- Videos
- Podcast transcripts
- Presentations
- Sales messages
- Website copy
- Customer conversations
- Personal stories
- Interview responses

Use original, unedited material whenever possible.

Do not include passwords, private customer information, protected health information, confidential business data, or anything you are not authorized to share.

---

## Master Prompt

Copy the prompt below into your preferred AI assistant.

Replace the bracketed sections with your information.

```text
You are helping me discover and document an authentic brand voice.

Your job is not to invent a new personality or make the writing sound more impressive. Your job is to study the information I provide and identify the recurring patterns that make this person or organization sound distinctive.

Analyze the material carefully and separate direct evidence from interpretation.

BRAND OR PERSON:

[Enter the name of the person, company, or brand.]

BUSINESS OR PROFESSIONAL CONTEXT:

[Describe what they do, whom they serve, and what they want to be known for.]

AUDIENCE:

[Describe the people they communicate with.]

COMMUNICATION GOALS:

[Describe what the communication should help the audience understand, feel, believe, or do.]

VALUES:

[List known values, principles, beliefs, or commitments.]

VOICE EXAMPLES:

[Paste several examples of natural writing, speech, stories, emails, articles, posts, or transcripts.]

WORDS OR PHRASES THEY OFTEN USE:

[List any known expressions, sayings, metaphors, questions, or recurring language.]

WORDS, PHRASES, OR STYLES TO AVOID:

[List language that feels false, generic, exaggerated, overly formal, manipulative, or inconsistent with the brand.]

STORIES OR EXPERIENCES THAT SHAPE THE VOICE:

[Describe important personal, professional, cultural, educational, military, family, or business experiences.]

Using only the information provided, create a Brand Voice Discovery Report with the following sections:

1. Voice Summary

Write a clear paragraph describing how the voice sounds, what it communicates, and how it should make the audience feel.

2. Core Voice Characteristics

Identify five to seven defining voice characteristics.

For each characteristic, include:

- A short name
- A description
- Evidence from the supplied material
- Guidance for reproducing it authentically

3. Tone Range

Explain how the voice should adapt across:

- Educational content
- Thought leadership
- Social media
- Sales communication
- Customer support
- Personal storytelling
- Serious or sensitive topics

4. Language Patterns

Identify recurring patterns such as:

- Sentence length
- Vocabulary
- Rhythm
- Questions
- Storytelling habits
- Humor
- Metaphors
- Emotional language
- Calls to action
- Transitions
- Opening styles
- Closing styles

5. Signature Language

List phrases, expressions, themes, or verbal habits that appear distinctive.

Do not invent signature phrases that are not supported by the supplied material.

6. Voice Dos

Create a practical list of behaviors writers and AI systems should follow.

7. Voice Don’ts

Create a practical list of language, tone, and behaviors to avoid.

8. Brand Beliefs

Identify the recurring beliefs, principles, or convictions expressed through the voice.

Clearly label anything that is an interpretation rather than a direct statement.

9. Storytelling Profile

Describe:

- The kinds of stories used
- How stories usually begin
- How lessons are revealed
- How personal the stories become
- How stories connect to business or professional ideas

10. Before-and-After Examples

Create three examples showing:

- A generic sentence
- A revised sentence in this brand voice
- A brief explanation of the changes

11. Authenticity Risks

Identify situations where the voice could become:

- Too polished
- Too promotional
- Too generic
- Too technical
- Too emotional
- Too informal
- Inconsistent with the stated values

12. Brand Voice Quick Reference

Finish with a concise reference card containing:

- Voice in three words
- Primary audience
- Desired emotional effect
- Five voice rules
- Five things to avoid
- Three signature themes
- One sentence describing the voice

Do not claim certainty when the evidence is limited.

When the supplied examples are insufficient, explain what additional material would improve the analysis.
