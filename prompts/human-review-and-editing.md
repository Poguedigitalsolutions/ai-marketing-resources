# Human Review and Editing Prompt

Use this prompt to review AI-assisted content before it is published, sent, presented, automated, or used to make a business decision.

This resource is part of the **Brand Voice AI** methodology developed by **John M. Pogue** and **Pogue Digital Solutions, LLC**.

> AI can prepare the work. A human must remain responsible for the message.

---

## Purpose

This prompt creates a structured human-review checkpoint for:

- Accuracy
- Brand voice
- Clarity
- Relevance
- Unsupported claims
- Source quality
- Privacy
- Confidentiality
- Ethics
- Accessibility
- Platform fit
- Customer impact
- Calls to action
- Final approval

The goal is not to make content sound more polished simply because AI can rewrite it.

The goal is to determine whether the content is:

- True
- Useful
- Recognizable
- Responsible
- Appropriate
- Ready for its intended audience

## Review Levels

Use the review level that matches the risk of the content.

### Level 1: Light Review

Appropriate for low-risk internal drafts and informal content.

Check:

- Clarity
- Grammar
- Brand voice
- Formatting
- Basic accuracy

### Level 2: Standard Review

Appropriate for public marketing content, articles, newsletters, and customer communication.

Check:

- Accuracy
- Sources
- Brand voice
- Audience relevance
- Claims
- Privacy
- CTA
- Platform fit

### Level 3: High-Risk Review

Appropriate for content involving:

- Legal matters
- Medical or health information
- Financial information
- Public policy
- Government
- Employment
- Safety
- Regulated industries
- Customer data
- Confidential information
- Major business commitments
- Public accusations
- Sensitive personal stories
- Automated decisions

This level may require qualified professional review.

AI should not replace legal, medical, financial, compliance, security, or other specialized professional judgment.

## Before You Begin

Gather the material needed for review.

Useful inputs include:

- The full draft
- Original source material
- Brand voice profile
- Customer avatar
- Intended platform
- Intended audience
- Supporting links
- Research notes
- Approved claims
- Known facts
- Required disclaimers
- Privacy requirements
- Accessibility requirements
- Call to action
- Publication deadline
- Internal approval rules
- Previous approved examples

Do not assume a claim is accurate merely because it sounds plausible.

Do not assume a source is credible merely because it appears in the draft.

## Master Prompt

Copy the prompt below into your preferred AI assistant.

Replace the bracketed sections with your information.

```text
You are assisting with a structured human review of AI-assisted content.

Your role is to identify issues, organize the review, explain risks, and recommend revisions.

You do not have final approval authority.

Do not silently rewrite the entire draft before explaining what is wrong.

Do not invent facts, sources, quotations, customer results, credentials, legal conclusions, medical conclusions, financial conclusions, statistics, personal experiences, or permissions.

Separate:

- Confirmed facts
- Supported interpretation
- Personal opinion
- Assumptions
- Claims requiring verification
- Missing information
- High-risk content
- Content requiring professional review

When evidence is unavailable, say so.

When a claim cannot be verified, recommend removing it, qualifying it, or obtaining a source.

When the content involves legal, medical, financial, safety, employment, regulatory, privacy, or other high-risk matters, identify the need for qualified professional review.

CONTENT CREATOR OR BRAND:

[Enter the person, company, or brand.]

CONTENT TYPE:

[Article, social post, email, landing page, proposal, report, script, presentation, customer message, or another type.]

CONTENT PURPOSE:

[Describe what the content is intended to accomplish.]

TARGET AUDIENCE:

[Describe the intended reader, viewer, listener, or recipient.]

PLATFORM OR CHANNEL:

[LinkedIn, Writing.io, Substack, email, website, Skool, YouTube, internal document, or another channel.]

DESIRED AUDIENCE RESPONSE:

[What should the audience understand, feel, believe, discuss, or do?]

BRAND VOICE:

[Paste the brand voice summary, examples, signature language, voice rules, voice dos, and voice don’ts.]

APPROVED FACTS:

[List facts already verified.]

APPROVED CLAIMS:

[List claims the organization has approved for use.]

SUPPORTING SOURCES:

[Paste links, citations, research notes, transcripts, reports, datasets, or other evidence.]

REQUIRED LANGUAGE:

[List disclosures, disclaimers, terminology, legal language, citations, accessibility text, or internal wording that must remain.]

PROHIBITED LANGUAGE:

[List words, claims, topics, promises, tones, or confidential information that must not appear.]

PRIVACY OR CONFIDENTIALITY REQUIREMENTS:

[List customer privacy, internal confidentiality, contractual, or data-handling requirements.]

ACCESSIBILITY REQUIREMENTS:

[List reading level, font, contrast, captions, alternative text, formatting, or usability requirements.]

RISK LEVEL:

[Light, standard, high-risk, or unknown.]

DRAFT TO REVIEW:

[Paste the complete draft.]

Using only the supplied information, create a Human Review and Editing Report with the following sections:

1. Review Summary

Provide:

- Content type
- Purpose
- Audience
- Platform
- Risk level
- Overall readiness
- Primary strengths
- Primary concerns
- Recommended action

Choose one readiness status:

- Ready
- Ready with minor edits
- Requires substantial revision
- Requires fact verification
- Requires professional review
- Not suitable for publication

2. Content Purpose Review

Evaluate whether the draft:

- Has one clear purpose
- Addresses the intended audience
- Provides relevant value
- Supports the desired response
- Maintains focus
- Contains unnecessary material
- Makes the next step clear

3. Audience Fit Review

Evaluate:

- Audience knowledge level
- Audience needs
- Audience language
- Audience concerns
- Audience expectations
- Cultural sensitivity
- Emotional appropriateness
- Accessibility
- Risk of misunderstanding
- Risk of alienating the intended reader

4. Evidence Classification

Separate all material into:

- Verified facts
- Facts supplied without verification
- Personal experience
- Opinion
- Interpretation
- Assumption
- Prediction
- Recommendation
- Quotation
- Statistical claim
- Marketing claim
- High-risk claim
- Unknown

5. Claim-by-Claim Audit

Create a table containing:

- Exact claim
- Claim type
- Source supplied
- Verification status
- Confidence level
- Risk level
- Context needed
- Recommended action

Recommended actions may include:

- Keep
- Cite
- Qualify
- Rewrite
- Verify
- Remove
- Refer for professional review

6. Source Review

For every source, evaluate:

- Source name
- Source type
- Author or organization
- Publication date
- Relevant data period
- Primary or secondary source
- Credibility
- Relevance
- Possible bias
- Whether the draft represents it accurately
- Whether the source is current enough
- Citation needed

7. Quotation Review

For every quotation, confirm:

- Exact wording supplied
- Speaker or source
- Context
- Permission when relevant
- Accuracy
- Attribution
- Risk of changing the meaning
- Whether quotation marks are appropriate

Do not manufacture quotations.

8. Statistics Review

For every statistic, identify:

- Statistic
- Source
- Publication date
- Data period
- Sample
- Geography
- Definition
- Calculation method when known
- Context
- Limitation
- Whether the draft overstates the finding

9. Factuality Review

Identify:

- Incorrect statements
- Unsupported statements
- Outdated statements
- Ambiguous claims
- Missing context
- False precision
- Causal claims without evidence
- Predictions presented as certainty
- Marketing claims presented as fact
- Conclusions stronger than the evidence

10. Hallucination Risk Review

Identify any content that may have been invented or inferred by AI, including:

- Sources
- Dates
- Names
- Titles
- Statistics
- Quotations
- Customer examples
- Case studies
- Credentials
- Product features
- Pricing
- Legal requirements
- Research findings
- Personal stories
- URLs
- Partnerships
- Awards
- Results

Explain how each item should be verified.

11. Brand Voice Review

Evaluate the draft for:

- Tone
- Vocabulary
- Rhythm
- Sentence length
- Warmth
- Authority
- Humor
- Storytelling
- Signature language
- Values
- Voice dos
- Voice don’ts
- Recognizability
- Emotional authenticity
- Excessive polish
- Generic AI language
- Overly formal language
- Overly casual language

12. Generic AI Language Audit

Highlight wording that sounds:

- Generic
- Predictable
- Corporate
- Inflated
- Artificial
- Overly polished
- Emotionally empty
- Repetitive
- Vague
- Detached from the creator
- Detached from the audience
- Unnaturally balanced
- Filled with unnecessary transitions

For each phrase, explain why it feels artificial and offer a more natural alternative.

13. Common AI Pattern Audit

Check for:

- Repeated three-part lists
- Excessive headings
- Repetitive conclusions
- Restating the introduction
- Overuse of “not only”
- Overuse of “in today’s world”
- Empty motivational language
- Excessive rhetorical questions
- Manufactured vulnerability
- Unnecessary disclaimers
- Repeated sentence structures
- Overuse of colons
- Predictable “the future is…” statements
- Forced metaphors
- Unsupported certainty
- Excessive symmetry

14. Clarity Review

Evaluate:

- Main idea
- Sentence clarity
- Paragraph clarity
- Pronoun references
- Terminology
- Jargon
- Acronyms
- Transitions
- Instructions
- Calls to action
- Ambiguity
- Missing definitions
- Overly complex explanations

15. Structure Review

Evaluate:

- Opening
- Sequence
- Headings
- Paragraph order
- Supporting evidence
- Examples
- Transitions
- Repetition
- Closing
- CTA placement

Identify material that should be:

- Moved
- Combined
- Split
- Shortened
- Expanded
- Removed

16. Opening Review

Determine whether the opening:

- Establishes relevance
- Creates appropriate interest
- Identifies the problem
- Matches the audience
- Avoids clickbait
- Avoids unnecessary biography
- Avoids inflated claims
- Leads naturally into the content

Create three improved opening options when needed.

17. Closing Review

Determine whether the closing:

- Resolves the central idea
- Adds human judgment
- Avoids repetition
- Supports the purpose
- Provides an appropriate next step
- Preserves the brand voice
- Ends with a memorable but credible line

Create three improved closing options when needed.

18. Story Review

For every personal or customer story, evaluate:

- Whether it is true
- Whether it is authorized
- Whether identities are protected
- Whether confidential details are removed
- Whether the story supports the point
- Whether context is sufficient
- Whether the lesson is clear
- Whether the story exploits vulnerability
- Whether the story makes the creator the hero unnecessarily
- Whether the story belongs in public content

19. Customer Example Review

For every customer example, verify:

- Permission
- Accuracy
- Identifiability
- Confidentiality
- Results
- Timeframe
- Typicality
- Context
- Limitations
- Required disclaimer
- Risk of implied guarantee

20. Privacy Review

Identify any:

- Names
- Email addresses
- Phone numbers
- Addresses
- Account numbers
- Customer records
- Health information
- Financial information
- Legal information
- Employee information
- Private conversations
- Internal documents
- Location data
- Login credentials
- Personal identifiers
- Sensitive stories

Recommend:

- Keep
- Anonymize
- Redact
- Obtain permission
- Remove
- Refer for review

21. Confidentiality Review

Check for:

- Proprietary processes
- Trade secrets
- Client information
- Internal pricing
- Internal strategy
- Contract details
- Nonpublic financial information
- Security information
- Internal communications
- Unreleased products
- Private research
- Passwords or access details

22. Intellectual Property Review

Flag possible issues involving:

- Copyrighted text
- Images
- Logos
- Trademarks
- Proprietary frameworks
- Course materials
- Article excerpts
- Lyrics
- Scripts
- Screenshots
- Competitor copy
- Licensed assets
- AI-generated imitations
- Uncredited ideas

Do not provide legal conclusions.

Identify matters requiring professional review.

23. Ethics Review

Evaluate whether the content:

- Misleads
- Manipulates
- Exaggerates
- Exploits fear
- Uses shame
- Uses artificial urgency
- Uses false scarcity
- Conceals material information
- Makes unsupported guarantees
- Targets vulnerable people unfairly
- Presents opinion as fact
- Uses personal information irresponsibly
- Encourages harmful behavior
- Misrepresents AI involvement
- Violates stated brand values

24. Persuasion Review

Evaluate:

- Value proposition
- Evidence
- Urgency
- Scarcity
- Emotional appeal
- Social proof
- Authority
- Risk reversal
- CTA
- Reader autonomy

Identify persuasion that becomes pressure.

25. Bias Review

Check for possible:

- Demographic assumptions
- Cultural stereotypes
- Gender assumptions
- Age assumptions
- Disability assumptions
- Economic assumptions
- Professional stereotypes
- Geographic assumptions
- Political assumptions
- Religious assumptions
- Customer-blaming language
- Founder-centered thinking
- Survivorship bias
- Confirmation bias

26. Inclusion Review

Evaluate whether the content:

- Uses respectful language
- Avoids unnecessary labels
- Avoids stereotypes
- Recognizes different experiences
- Provides accessible alternatives
- Avoids excluding readers without purpose
- Distinguishes audience focus from discrimination
- Uses examples responsibly

27. Accessibility Review

Check:

- Reading level
- Heading hierarchy
- Paragraph length
- Link text
- Alternative text
- Captioning
- Transcript needs
- Color dependence
- Contrast needs
- Mobile readability
- Form clarity
- Keyboard usability
- Cognitive load
- Plain-language alternatives
- Font-size requirements
- Printable version
- Audio or visual alternatives

28. Platform Fit Review

Evaluate the draft for the intended platform.

Check:

- Length
- Formatting
- Opening
- Headings
- Paragraph spacing
- Link placement
- CTA
- Hashtags
- Visual needs
- Search visibility
- Mobile use
- Audience behavior
- Platform conventions
- Risk of content being truncated
- Risk of sounding copied from another platform

29. Search and Discoverability Review

When relevant, evaluate:

- Search intent
- Headline
- Keywords
- Metadata
- Description
- URL
- Internal links
- External links
- Heading structure
- Image text
- Accessibility text
- Relevance
- Risk of keyword stuffing

30. Call-to-Action Review

Evaluate each CTA for:

- Clarity
- Relevance
- Timing
- Effort
- Reader readiness
- Tone
- Proportionality
- Destination
- Link accuracy
- Expectation setting
- Pressure
- Alternatives

Recommend CTAs for readers who are:

- Curious
- Learning
- Comparing
- Not ready
- Ready to talk
- Ready to purchase
- Existing customers
- Potential referral partners

31. Link Review

Create a table containing:

- Link text
- Destination
- Link supplied
- Whether it appears complete
- Whether the label matches the destination
- Whether the reader knows what happens next
- Whether tracking or privacy concerns exist
- Manual verification required

Do not claim a link works unless it has been tested.

32. Legal and Regulatory Risk Review

Flag content involving:

- Legal advice
- Medical advice
- Financial advice
- Employment law
- Advertising claims
- Testimonials
- Guarantees
- Privacy
- Data collection
- Email consent
- Accessibility
- Copyright
- Trademark
- Government contracting
- Procurement
- Regulated industries
- Children
- Safety
- International requirements

Do not provide legal conclusions.

State which sections require qualified professional review.

33. Medical and Health Risk Review

When relevant, flag:

- Diagnosis
- Treatment
- Medication
- Prognosis
- Health claims
- Safety instructions
- Mental-health guidance
- Medical credentials
- Patient stories
- Protected health information
- Emergency guidance

34. Financial Risk Review

- Investment claims
- Earnings claims
- Debt advice
- Tax advice
- Credit claims
- Forecasts
- Guarantees
- Return projections
- Fees
- Pricing
- Financial disclosures
- Customer financial information

Recommend professional review.

35. Security Review

Check for:

- Passwords
- API keys
- Access tokens
- Internal URLs
- System architecture
- Security weaknesses
- Customer records
- Private repositories
- Configuration details
- Exploit instructions
- Authentication details
- Sensitive screenshots

36. Automation Review

When content will be automated, evaluate:

- Trigger
- Audience
- Data source
- Timing
- Personalization
- Approval
- Failure path
- Opt-out
- Frequency
- Repetition
- Escalation
- Sensitive topics
- Human handoff
- Monitoring
- Stop conditions

37. Human Approval Requirements

Identify who should approve:

- Facts
- Sources
- Brand voice
- Customer examples
- Legal language
- Financial claims
- Medical claims
- Privacy
- Security
- Pricing
- Offers
- Automation
- Final publication

38. Editorial Revision Plan

Create a revision plan organized into:

### Critical

Issues that must be resolved before publication.

### Important

Issues that materially improve accuracy, clarity, trust, or brand voice.

### Optional

Polish that may improve style but is not required.

For every revision, include:

- Issue
- Location
- Reason
- Risk
- Recommended change
- Approval needed

39. Line Editing

Provide a line-by-line editing table containing:

- Original text
- Issue
- Recommended revision
- Reason
- Whether meaning changed
- Verification needed

Preserve the creator’s voice.

Do not replace every sentence merely to demonstrate editing.

40. Revised Draft

After completing the review, create a revised draft.

The revised draft should:

- Correct verified issues
- Preserve approved facts
- Preserve the brand voice
- Remove unsupported claims
- Qualify uncertain statements
- Improve clarity
- Protect privacy
- Maintain the original purpose
- Use an appropriate CTA
- Avoid excessive polish
- Avoid unnecessary expansion

Clearly mark any section that still requires verification or professional approval.

41. Change Log

Create a change log containing:

- Section
- Original issue
- Revision
- Reason
- Evidence
- Approval required

42. Final Approval Checklist

Create a checklist with:

- Purpose approved
- Audience approved
- Facts verified
- Sources checked
- Quotations checked
- Statistics checked
- Claims approved
- Brand voice approved
- Privacy approved
- Confidentiality approved
- Intellectual property reviewed
- Accessibility reviewed
- Platform formatting reviewed
- CTA checked
- Links tested
- Required disclosures included
- Professional review completed
- Final human approval recorded

43. Final Decision

Choose one:

- Approved for publication
- Approved after listed minor edits
- Hold for fact verification
- Hold for permission
- Hold for privacy review
- Hold for professional review
- Reject and rewrite

Explain the decision.

44. Human Review Questions

Finish by asking:

- Is the central idea true and useful?
- Does the content sound like the creator?
- Are all claims supported?
- Are all sources accurate and current?
- Are quotations exact?
- Are stories true and authorized?
- Is private information protected?
- Are ethical concerns resolved?
- Is the CTA appropriate?
- Does the platform formatting work?
- Are high-risk sections professionally reviewed?
- Would the creator defend every statement publicly?
- Is a named human accepting final responsibility?
```

## Recommended Follow-Up Questions

### Follow-Up 1: Audit Every Claim

```text
Create a claim-by-claim audit of the draft. Separate verified fact, unverified fact, opinion, interpretation, assumption, prediction, recommendation, and marketing claim.
```

### Follow-Up 2: Remove Generic AI Language

```text
Identify every sentence that sounds generic, overly polished, repetitive, corporate, artificial, or emotionally empty. Explain the problem and suggest a more natural alternative.
```

### Follow-Up 3: Verify the Sources

```text
Review every source and citation. Identify missing dates, weak sources, inaccurate representations, outdated evidence, unsupported quotations, and claims that need stronger support.
```

### Follow-Up 4: Protect Privacy

```text
Identify all personal, customer, employee, financial, health, legal, security, and confidential information. Recommend what should be retained, anonymized, redacted, approved, or removed.
```

### Follow-Up 5: Restore the Brand Voice

```text
Compare the draft with the supplied brand voice. Identify passages that feel unlike the creator and rewrite them without changing the approved meaning.
```

### Follow-Up 6: Challenge the Argument

```text
Act as a skeptical but fair reader. Identify weak reasoning, missing context, unsupported conclusions, alternative explanations, and questions the draft fails to answer.
```

### Follow-Up 7: Prepare the Final Approval Version

```text
Create a final publication version containing only verified, approved, brand-aligned content. Mark any remaining issue that requires human verification or professional review.
```

## Human Review Checklist

Before publication, confirm:

- Is the purpose clear?
- Is the audience correct?
- Is the central idea accurate?
- Are facts verified?
- Are sources current?
- Are quotations exact?
- Are statistics contextualized?
- Are claims proportionate?
- Is opinion distinguished from fact?
- Does the content sound recognizable?
- Has generic AI language been removed?
- Are stories true and authorized?
- Is customer information protected?
- Is confidential information removed?
- Are intellectual-property concerns reviewed?
- Are ethical concerns resolved?
- Is the content accessible?
- Is the platform formatting correct?
- Is the CTA appropriate?
- Do links work?
- Are required disclosures present?
- Has professional review occurred where needed?
- Has a human given final approval?
- Would the creator defend every statement publicly?

---

## Important Principle

Human review is not the final coat of polish applied after AI finishes the work.

It is the point where responsibility returns to the person whose name, brand, judgment, and reputation will stand behind the message.
